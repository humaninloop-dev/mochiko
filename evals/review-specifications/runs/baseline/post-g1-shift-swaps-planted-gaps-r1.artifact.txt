## Clarifications

**1. When does an accepted swap actually change the published rota?**
- **A — immediately on mutual staff acceptance** (matches FR-004 / US-1 AC2): fastest for staff
  swapping close to showtime, but removes the manager checkpoint before a licensing-sensitive
  shift changes hands, and makes US-3's "approve before they land" story purely after-the-fact.
- **B — gated on manager approval** (matches FR-006 / US-3): preserves the checkpoint the pilot
  venues asked for, but a same-day swap now waits on a manager being available before doors.
- **C — split by shift sensitivity**: manager approval required only for roles decision 0003
  already gates (bar, duty manager), auto-applied otherwise. Matches how eligibility already
  varies by role, but is more surface to build, test, and explain to managers.

Whichever is chosen also needs an answer for the missing "withdrawn" state (US-1 AC3) and a
number for FR-002's "reasonable time" expiry window — engineering cannot build an expiry job
off a phrase, and SC-001's 24-hour measurement needs to know if it's the same window.

**2. Should a swap enforce the same shift-eligibility rules the rota builder already enforces?**
- **A — yes**: a swap that would place someone on a shift they're not flagged for (age,
  alcohol sign-off, till training, duty-manager flag) is refused with the reason, same as a
  manager's direct edit today.
- **B — no**: swaps stay eligibility-blind as currently assumed, and whatever approval step
  survives question 1 is trusted to catch it by eye.

Also worth confirming while this is open: is a same-role swap the only kind in scope (usher for
usher), or can a bar shift be swapped for an usher shift? The Overview and stories don't say
either way, and it changes how much eligibility logic option A actually needs.

**3. What happens to the "offer to everyone, first claim" line in the drafted FEAT-006 entry?**
- **A — drop it**: it duplicates FEAT-005 (Open shifts), contradicts this spec's own "out of
  scope: that is open shifts" line, and traces to no story here — leave FEAT-005 for its own
  future spec.
- **B — keep it, but re-scope this spec to also cover FEAT-005**: touch FEAT-005 explicitly,
  add a story for it, and remove the contradicting Intent line.

Either way, the derivation's baseline table needs FEAT-005 added before any map write lands —
right now the dedup pass never saw it.

**4. Should shift-swaps produce a prototype now, or formally waive it?**
- **A — build the SCR/FLOW manifest and prototype before the spec is marked ready**: consistent
  with "UX-bearing: yes," and gives something to usability-check against SC-003's "intuitive"
  target before code is written.
- **B — waive it explicitly** ("No UX surface — prototype waived at intent," as tablet-offline
  does) and let the Figma-then-build path stand: faster to close the spec, but no independent
  screen or flow check happens until well after this review.

As staged, the section is neither, so it can't clear the Screens & Flows check under either
reading.

**5. How should a swap notification reach a staff member with no push available?**
- **A — fall back to SMS per decision 0007**, same as every other staff notification: matches
  existing policy, costs an SMS per fallback.
- **B — push-only for swaps**: accepts that roughly a third of staff (per the 2026 device
  survey) may not see a request until they next open the app or check the noticeboard.

SC-001's 80%-within-24-hours target is very unlikely to hold for option B's population; the
Overview's "reaches its counterpart within seconds" line needs rewriting either way.

**6. Is FR-008's dark-mode toggle actually wanted here?**
- **A — drop it**: no story, decision, or venue-operations ask names it.
- **B — keep it, but name what it serves**: is this specific to the swap screens, or an
  app-wide setting that's been misfiled onto this spec?

---

## Recommended verdict

`needs-revision` — advisory input for the lead, not a clearing verdict. The lifecycle
contradiction (F1), the eligibility gap (F2), the FEAT-005 overlap (F3), and the Screens & Flows
shape (F4) are all build-blocking as written; the notification premise (F5) undermines the
feature's own success criterion before a line of code exists.

## Strengths

The edge cases named (decline, mid-flow cancellation by the manager, same-day double-booking)
are the right ones for a swap feature, the story priorities (request → answer → manager
approval → history) sequence sensibly, and FR-007's blanket audit requirement correctly inherits
the governance floor rather than restating it loosely.
