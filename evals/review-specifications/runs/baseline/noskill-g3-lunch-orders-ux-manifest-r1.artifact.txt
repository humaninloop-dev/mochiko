# Stress-test review — spec `lunch-orders`

Reviewer note: the `mochiko:review-specifications` skill was not available in this
session (not present in the installed skill set), so this review was carried out
manually against the same materials the skill would have used: `tally/CLAUDE.md`,
`tally/README.md`, `tally/docs/`, `tally/FEATURES.md` + `.mochiko/features/`,
`.mochiko/specs/index.md`, and the full `lunch-orders` workspace (`spec.md`,
`stories/`, `derivation.md`, `prototype/`), cross-checked against the sibling
in-flight spec `menu-admin`.

**Verdict: not ready to leave draft.** Two of the findings below are not
polish issues — they are the spec instructing the team to build something that
directly contradicts either a concurrently-accepted spec or the product's own
governance and school-facts documents. Both trace to a specific, identifiable
cause rather than vague ambiguity, so both are fixable without a re-derivation.

---

## Critical

### 1. FR-002 tells parents to see exactly what `menu-admin` says they must never see

- `spec.md` FR-002 (line 54): "The parent MUST see the whole published menu for
  the week, **including items the kitchen has marked hidden**, so that nothing on
  offer is missed."
- `menu-admin/spec.md` FR-002 (line 40): "A hidden item MUST NOT appear in any
  parent-facing screen or response, **including a week's menu a parent has already
  opened**."
- `menu-admin` was created 2026-08-25, accepted 2026-08-29, and is `in-flight` in
  the committed `FEATURES.md` (row hook, line 10: *"hidden items never reach
  parents"*). `lunch-orders` was created 2026-09-03 — a week after `menu-admin`
  was already accepted with the opposite requirement on the same feature
  (FEAT-002).

This isn't just an unreconciled overlap between two specs in flight at once —
it's traceable to a bad input. `derivation.md`'s "Map at baseline (as read at run
open)" (lines 7–12) lists **FEAT-002 as `delivered`**. But the map actually
committed at that point (2026-09-03) already showed FEAT-002 as `in-flight`
since 2026-08-25 — `menu-admin` had been running for over a week. The baseline
table the author derived FR-002 from does not match the map as committed. That
mismatch is the root cause of the contradiction, not an unlucky race between two
authors — worth the lead checking whether this was a stale read or a
transcription error, since it means the rest of the derivation's "baseline"
claims should be re-verified against the actual map too.

Substantively, menu-admin's own rationale ("a hidden item is a safety and stock
decision," e.g. a supplier recall) makes FR-002 as written actively unsafe, not
just inconsistent — showing a recalled item as orderable is the failure mode
`menu-admin` exists to prevent.

### 2. Funded (free-school-meal) lunches are assumed out of existence

- `spec.md` Assumptions (line 137): "**Every family has a card on file**, so
  every order is charged at placement." Overview (line 29) repeats this as fact.
- `docs/free-school-meals.md` (lines 3–8): 41% of pupils at the pilot schools get
  a universal infant free lunch, a further 18% of KS2 get means-tested free
  lunches, "**for a funded meal nothing is charged to the family**," and
  crucially: "**many of these families have no card on file and no wallet
  balance, and some have never topped up**." It also states plainly: "A funded
  child still needs an order placed so the kitchen has a count and the allergy
  flag" — i.e. this spec's own subject matter.
- `CLAUDE.md` governance (line 18–19): "An order is placed only when the family
  wallet covers it, **or when the meal is funded (free school meals) and no
  charge applies**." The spec only builds the first half of that sentence.

Nothing in FR-001..010, the Key Entities (line 74–78, "Order" has no funded/zero-
charge concept), the Screens & Flows, or the FEAT-003 wallet delta
(`derivation.md`: "the balance after an order equals the balance before minus the
total" — no exception) accounts for a meal that is ordered but not charged. The
prototype's own example family includes Tobi Okafor, Reception (`scr-002-child-
picker.html`) — a pupil who, per the school facts, should be getting a universal
free lunch — and the mock treats his order exactly like a paid one. Given FSM
covers something like 40–60% of the pilot pupil population per the docs, this
isn't an edge case; it's close to half the ordering volume with no designed
path. `README.md` (line 24) flags this exact doc as a thing that "keeps coming
up," which makes its absence here more notable, not less.

## High

### 3. Pupil order data leaves the app's confidentiality enforcement entirely

`CLAUDE.md`'s Confidential-data principle (lines 12–17) names its enforcement
mechanism specifically: "the API serialisers carry an allowlist per role; the
contract suite asserts no allergy field in any unauthenticated or kitchen-role
response." FR-006 (line 62) has orders — child, day, chosen item — written by a
worker into a second tab of a Google Sheet the catering manager already edits,
via the Sheets API, within five minutes of placement. That's a second, entirely
different data path for pupil records (child name, class implied, meal choice)
that a spreadsheet's sharing settings govern, not the serializer allowlist the
governance section names as *the* enforcement point. The spec doesn't say who
can access that sheet, what fields it carries, or how it satisfies "shown only
to that child's signed-in parent and to school staff with the right role." Given
the stated rationale is UK GDPR and safeguarding, this deserves an explicit
answer before build, not an assumption that "it's just for the kitchen."

### 4. US-1's own acceptance scenario states the wrong cut-off

`stories/US-1.md`, Acceptance Scenario 2 (line 15): "**Given** an order is
placed, **When** the parent changes Tuesday's meal **before Sunday midnight**,
**Then** the change is saved." The actual cut-off, per FR-005 and
`docs/decisions/0003-order-cutoff.md`, is **17:00 the Thursday of the week
before** — which falls before that Sunday, not after it. As written, the story
that is meant to independently test the feature's headline behavior encodes a
deadline later than the real one; anyone building against this scenario alone
would ship a cut-off that's too permissive, directly conflicting with the
"counts are final at the cut-off" governance principle the whole feature exists
to serve.

## Medium

### 5. The prototype and the spec's screen manifest disagree with each other

`spec.md`'s Screens & Flows table (lines 96–100) declares SCR-001 through
SCR-004; every prototype page's nav bar links to `scr-004-order-history.html` —
but no such file exists (`prototype/screens/` has only 001, 002, 003, 005). The
"Pay" button on `scr-003-order-summary.html` (line 25) links to
`scr-006-confirmation.html`, which is both missing and undeclared anywhere in
the manifest. Conversely, `scr-005-allergens.html` exists, is linked from every
page's nav, and is functional — but is absent from the Screens table entirely.
Net effect: two dead links in every page of the low-fi mock, one of them on the
one button that completes the money flow, and one working screen the spec
doesn't acknowledge.

### 6. The prototype demonstrates the one thing the spec says shouldn't happen

`scr-003-order-summary.html`: wallet balance £9.10, order total £9.70 (lines
17–23), presented with an unconditional "Pay £9.70" button (line 25) as if the
transaction will simply succeed. This is exactly the state US-1 Acceptance
Scenario 3 covers ("wallet holds less than the order total... asked to top up
and the order is held until they do") and exactly the state `CLAUDE.md`'s
"wallets never go negative" principle exists to prevent — but the mock shows
neither a block nor a top-up prompt, just a checkout that would silently violate
the DB check constraint on `wallet.balance >= 0` if built as drawn. Worth
confirming this was a mock oversight (numbers not adjusted after the flow was
laid out) rather than a sign the low-balance path was never actually walked
through.

### 7. Multi-child ordering "without paying twice" is asserted, never specified

`derivation.md`'s proposed FEAT-005 extent states "A parent can order for two or
more children in one sitting without paying twice" — but `spec.md` has no FR for
it, and the only flow (FLOW-001, line 104) is strictly single-child: child picker
→ that child's week menu → that child's summary → pay. There's no screen or step
anywhere for adding a second child to the same basket. Either this capability is
being quietly deferred (in which case it shouldn't be asserted as in-scope in the
feature extent) or it's a real gap in the FRs/flows.

### 8. Redis caching undercuts `menu-admin`'s own real-time requirement

Technical notes (line 151) cache the week's menu in Redis for an hour. But
`menu-admin`'s FR-002 requires a hidden item to disappear "including a week's
menu a parent has already opened" — i.e. near-immediately. An hour-long cache
means a just-hidden item (e.g. the recalled-item scenario `menu-admin` is built
for) could keep showing as orderable to any parent hitting the cache for up to
an hour after the kitchen hides it. Neither spec reconciles this.

## Low

### 9. FR-009 (meal ratings) has no story, no screen, no data model, no source

FR-009 (line 68): "Parents MUST be able to rate each meal after it has been
eaten." *Source: —.* No US references it, no screen or flow shows it, and Key
Entities (line 74) has no rating field on Order or Menu item — yet it appears in
`derivation.md`'s FEAT-005 extent as if it were derived from something. Looks
like scope that entered the FR list without a traceable origin; worth confirming
it's wanted before it's built, or dropping it if it's a leftover.

### 10. Two success criteria aren't actually measurable as written

- SC-002 (line 85): "Parents find ordering easy." No metric, no instrument, no
  threshold — unlike SC-001, SC-003, SC-004, which all name a measurement method.
- FR-010 (line 69): "The menu MUST load fast." The only concrete number (p95
  under 400ms) lives in Technical notes (line 152), disconnected from the FR and
  from any Success Criterion, so there's nothing to hold a build accountable to
  at the requirement level.

### 11. The mid-week-leaver edge case has no owning requirement

Edge Cases (line 47): "Child leaves the school mid-week → remaining orders that
week are credited to the wallet." `docs/decisions/0003` says post-cut-off
changes for a leaver go through the school office phoning the kitchen — but no
FR, screen, or actor in this spec says who or what actually triggers the wallet
credit, or how a phone call becomes a database write. As written it's an
assertion with no mechanism behind it.

---

## What holds up

- The allergy-blocking logic in the prototype (`scr-001-week-menu.html`) is
  internally consistent and correctly cross-checked against Ada's recorded milk
  allergy on every day of the mocked week — item-by-item, this part was clearly
  built and checked, not just asserted.
- FR-004/FR-005 and `docs/decisions/0003-order-cutoff.md` agree with each other
  precisely (mod finding #4's story-level slip).
- The "counts are final at the cut-off" and "wallets never go negative"
  governance principles are correctly reflected in the FR language itself (FR-003,
  FR-004) even though the funded-meal and low-balance-prototype gaps above show
  the follow-through is incomplete.

---

## Recommendation to the lead

Findings 1 and 2 are blocking: FR-002 needs to be struck and replaced with
whatever `menu-admin`'s accepted behavior actually implies for the week-menu
screen, and the entire funded-meal path needs FRs, a screen treatment, and a
wallet-delta acceptance criterion before this spec is buildable. Both are cheap
to fix relative to finding them after code exists — the derivation's baseline
table (finding 1) and the Assumptions section (finding 2) are exactly the kind
of thing worth re-deriving from the live map and the docs directory rather than
patching in place, since if the baseline was stale once it may be stale
elsewhere too.
