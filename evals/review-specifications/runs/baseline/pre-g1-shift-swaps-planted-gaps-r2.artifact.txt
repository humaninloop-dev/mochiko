## Clarifications

**1. When does an accepted swap change the published rota — on mutual acceptance, or only after manager approval?**
- Options: (a) the rota changes the moment both staff accept, and the manager's step is a
  post-hoc review/undo window; (b) the rota only changes after manager approval, and staff
  acceptance just queues the swap for review; (c) it depends on shift role — auto-effect for
  usher swaps, manager-gated for bar/box office/duty-manager swaps.
- Why this matters: FR-004 and FR-006 currently specify both (a) and (b) as MUST requirements.
  This is the single most consequential unresolved decision in the spec — it determines whether
  "who is on shift" is ever momentarily wrong on the published rota, which is the exact problem
  this feature exists to fix.

**2. Should a swap enforce the same role-eligibility rules the rota builder already enforces?**
- Options: (a) yes — a swap that would place someone on a shift they're not eligible for (age,
  till training, alcohol sign-off, duty-manager flag) is rejected with the reason, mirroring the
  rota builder today; (b) no — any colleague can be offered any shift, and eligibility is left to
  the manager's approval step to catch; (c) allowed but flagged, same treatment as availability
  conflicts today.
- Why this matters: the current Assumption ("any colleague is eligible") directly overrides
  decision 0003, which exists because of licensing inspections. Option (b) relies entirely on a
  P2 story (manager approval) that could ship later or be skipped for low-risk swaps.

**3. Given the survey shows only ~36% of staff have push notifications enabled, how should a swap request reach the other 64%?**
- Options: (a) apply decision 0007's existing fallback (push, else SMS, else flag to manager)
  to swap requests explicitly; (b) accept slower reach for now and revisit SC-001's 24-hour target
  accordingly; (c) require the requester to pick a colleague believed to be reachable, with no
  system fallback.
- Why this matters: the spec's Overview assumes near-universal push reach, which docs/staff-devices.md
  contradicts. If the swap-request notification silently relies on push only, most staff never see
  a request sent to them, which would also make SC-001 unachievable as measured.

**4. What happens to an accepted swap the manager never acts on?**
- Options: (a) auto-approve after a stated grace period (e.g. 48 hours) unless refused; (b) it
  stays pending indefinitely and shows on a manager's outstanding-approvals list until acted on;
  (c) escalate to a second approver or the venue-operations lead after a timeout.
- Why this matters: FR-006 gates the rota on approval with no time bound, so an unattended swap
  can leave the two staff believing the trade is done while the published rota — and the door —
  still shows the old assignment.

**5. Is the CSV-on-shared-drive swap history a real requirement, and if so, who is it for?**
- Options: (a) drop it — FR-007's audit trail plus US-4's in-app history view already cover the
  "who did what when" need; (b) keep it, but state the driver (e.g. an external payroll or
  licensing process that reads CSVs today) and define shared-drive access control; (c) replace it
  with an export button gated the same way as other Confidential-adjacent data.
- Why this matters: as written it's an implementation detail with no named requester, duplicates
  the append-only audit table the governance principle relies on, and raises an access-control
  question (who can open the shared drive) that the rest of the spec doesn't address anywhere else.

**6. Does shift-swaps also need to support offering a shift to everyone at the venue (first eligible claimant), or is that entirely FEAT-005's territory?**
- Options: (a) strictly out of scope here — remove the broadcast clause from the staged FEAT-006
  text and leave it for FEAT-005 to pick up when that capability is refined; (b) shift-swaps should
  absorb it and FEAT-005 should be retired/merged into FEAT-006; (c) it's intentionally in both,
  and the map needs an explicit relation note explaining the split.
- Why this matters: the spec's own Intent already rules this out of scope, but the staged map
  entry contradicts that by including it — left as-is, the map and the spec disagree with each
  other about what FEAT-006 is.

**7. Is a clickable prototype needed before this spec is approved, given Intent already calls it UX-bearing?**
- Options: (a) yes — produce the SCR/FLOW manifest and prototype now, before approval, since three
  screens with side-by-side comparisons and a refuse-with-note flow are exactly where a text-only
  spec hides dead ends; (b) formally waive it now by changing the Intent ruling to "not UX-bearing at
  this depth" (with a stated reason) and revisit at implementation; (c) proceed with the Figma
  hand-off as planned, and treat that as the equivalent artifact — but then the spec should say so
  explicitly rather than leaving the section as a placeholder.
- Why this matters: right now the section is neither a completed manifest nor a valid waiver, so
  it can't be checked at all — the flows most likely to have a UX gap are exactly the ones nobody
  can currently click through.
