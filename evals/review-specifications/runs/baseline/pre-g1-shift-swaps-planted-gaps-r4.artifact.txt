## Clarifications needed

### C1: When does an accepted swap actually change the published rota?   (G1)

**Question**: FR-004 and FR-006 describe two different flows. Which is correct?
**Options**: 1. The rota updates the moment both staff accept — manager review (US-3) happens after the fact, and a refusal reverses an already-live change. 2. The rota only updates once the manager approves — "accepted" is a pending state, and US-1's acceptance scenario is wrong as written. 3. Auto-approve after a time window if the manager hasn't acted, otherwise as (2).
**Why it matters**: This is the entire mechanism of the feature — it decides who's actually on shift at any moment, and it's the exact thing the governance principle on attributable rota changes and the licensing-sensitive manager review (US-3) both care about.

### C2: Should a swap check the same eligibility rules the rota builder already enforces?   (G2)

**Question**: Can any colleague take any shift offered in a swap, regardless of role eligibility?
**Options**: 1. No — block a swap onto an ineligible shift (no sign-off, no till training, under 18 for bar) the same way the rota builder does, with the reason shown. 2. Allow it, but flag it to the manager as part of approval, so the manager (not the system) is the eligibility backstop. 3. No check at all — the current assumption stands.
**Why it matters**: Decision 0003 exists because a licensing inspection asks who served alcohol and whether they were signed off; a swap is a second, unguarded path onto a bar or duty-manager shift if this isn't decided.

### C3: Does shift swaps also own "offer to everyone, first to claim"?   (G3)

**Question**: FEAT-006's proposed extent includes a first-eligible-claim mechanic that's word-for-word FEAT-005 (Open shifts), which the spec's own Out-of-scope line excludes. Which is right?
**Options**: 1. Shift swaps is strictly 1-for-1 named-colleague exchange; the claim mechanic is cut entirely and left for FEAT-005's own future derivation. 2. The two capabilities are close enough to merge now — fold FEAT-005 into FEAT-006 with an honest extent union. 3. Keep them separate but note the relation explicitly on both entries.
**Why it matters**: As staged, two proposed capabilities silently claim the same behavior with no story backing it in this spec — the next reader can't tell which run owns "first to claim."

### C4: Are screens required before this spec clears, or does UX slip to plan?   (G4)

**Question**: Intent ruled this feature UX-bearing with three named screens, but none exist yet.
**Options**: 1. Hold the spec here until a clickable prototype (SCR/FLOW manifest) exists. 2. Formally defer screens to plan/design and record that as a waiver amendment to the Intent ruling, not a silent "Figma link to follow." 3. Reopen Intent and rule this not UX-bearing after all, if the flows are simple enough to spec in prose alone.
**Why it matters**: The Screens & Flows check exists so a reviewer (and eventually a designer) can walk the actual request/answer/approve flows before code is committed to them — right now nobody can.

### C5: What happens to swap history — CSV export, or the in-app view, or both?   (G8)

**Question**: The Constraints line commits to a 12-month CSV on the venue's shared drive; US-4 describes an in-app history view. Neither FRs nor entities realize the CSV.
**Options**: 1. Drop the CSV constraint — US-4's in-app view is the whole of swap history. 2. Build both, with the CSV as a scheduled export and an explicit statement of who can reach the shared drive (staff data there is otherwise Confidential, manager-only). 3. CSV only, manager-triggered, no in-app history.
**Why it matters**: A shared-drive file with staff names sits outside the app's own access control, and it's not free to build — it needs its own requirement if it's real.

### C6: How does a same-day swap get manager approval before the shift starts?   (G12, G13)

**Question**: The Overview says most swaps settle the same day and assumes near-universal push reach; the venue-facts survey and decision 0007 say otherwise (about a third of staff reachable by push; managers get no push/SMS at all, only the dashboard bell and a 07:00 digest).
**Options**: 1. Add a manager alert path for swaps awaiting approval close to shift start (in-app only, per decision 0007 — no new channel). 2. Auto-approve a swap if the manager hasn't acted within a defined window before the shift. 3. Accept the risk as-is and rely on managers checking the dashboard.
**Why it matters**: If FR-006's manager-approval gate stands (see C1), a swap that settles same-day but sits unseen until the next 07:00 digest could mean the published rota is wrong at the exact moment the feature exists to fix — "who is actually on tonight."

### C7: How long before an unanswered swap request expires?   (G10)

**Question**: FR-002 says "a reasonable time" with no number.
**Options**: 1. 24 hours, matching SC-001's measurement window. 2. A shorter window (e.g. 4–6 hours) so a same-day swap doesn't stall past the shift. 3. A window that varies by how soon the shift starts.
**Why it matters**: The number also bounds the answer to the parked Open Question about capping how many requests a person can have open at once, and it determines whether SC-001's "80% answered within 24 hours" is even a meaningful target or just measuring the expiry clock.
