## Findings

### 1. [Blocker] Overview's payment claim is factually wrong and undermines FR-002/FR-003
`spec.md` Overview: *"Every customer pays by card, so pausing simply skips the card charge for the
paused weeks."*

`docs/payment-methods.md` says 72% pay by card, 22% by Direct Debit, 6% by PayPal — and, critically,
Direct Debit collections are **lodged the Thursday before the Friday cut-off and cannot be pulled
back once lodged**; unwinding one is a refund (3–5 working days), not a skipped charge. That means
for the ~22% of customers on Direct Debit, a pause requested between Thursday's lodge and Friday
noon's cut-off cannot satisfy FR-003 ("MUST NOT be charged... from the moment the pause is
requested") by simply not charging — the money will already be collected, and the only route back
is a refund. FR-002 and FR-003 are written as if this case doesn't exist.

This needs a product decision (refund automatically? disallow pause requests in that window? charge
through and skip the following week instead?) before engineering can build it — hence Blocker, not
something to patch in during the build.

### 2. [Blocker] Decision 0004 (max pause length) isn't in the spec at all
`docs/decisions/0004-pause-length.md` is dated 2026-06-10, "status: in force" — three months before
this spec was opened — and sets: max 8 weeks paused; a warning email at 6 weeks; auto-cancellation
with slot release at 8 weeks if not resumed. None of this appears in Functional Requirements, Key
Entities, or the User Stories. Worse, the spec's own **Open Questions** section still asks "How long
may a pause be at most?" — a question that was answered before the spec was written.

Concretely missing:
- A functional requirement capping pause length at 8 weeks.
- The 6-week warning email (distinct from FR-005/US-3's "reminder before boxes start again," which
  is about the *resume* date, not the impending auto-cancellation).
- The auto-cancellation-at-8-weeks behaviour and slot release.
- Fields on the `Pause` entity to track the warning and enforce the cap.

This is the single biggest gap in the spec — as drafted, a customer could pause indefinitely and
hold a delivery slot forever, which is the exact problem 0004 exists to prevent.

### 3. [Blocker] FR-008 (gifting a paused box) is untraceable and contradicts the stated scope
*"FR-008 A paused customer MUST be able to send one of their paused boxes to a friend as a gift.
Source: —"*

This requirement:
- Has no source story (US-1 through US-4 say nothing about gifting).
- Doesn't appear in the Overview, Edge Cases, Key Entities, or the Feature Selection's extent for
  FEAT-005 in either `spec.md` or `derivation.md`.
- Is the only place the word "gift" appears anywhere in the product docs other than the Intent's
  "Out of scope: ... pausing a gift subscription" line — a related but different concept (this is
  gifting *from* a paused account, not pausing a gift account).
- Implies real new scope (a friend's address, a third-party delivery, who pays) that conflicts with
  the Intent's "whole feature in one run" delivery plan.

Recommend cutting it from this spec. If it's a real ask, it needs its own story and its own
derivation pass, not a MUST slipped in with no source.

### 4. [Blocker] Data-model conflict makes SC-001 and SC-003 unmeasurable as written
Intent's Constraints say pause is "a boolean column on the customer record, set and cleared by the
pause flow" — i.e., no history retained once a pause ends. Technical notes repeat this: a flag plus
`pause_starts_at`/`pause_resumes_at` on `customers`. But Key Entities describes `Pause` as its own
entity with `requested at`, `starts`, `resumes`, `ended early` — implying a row per pause, kept.

This matters because SC-001 ("90% of pauses complete without a support ticket... over the first
three months") and SC-003 ("at least 60% of paused customers resume within six months... measured
from the subscription log") both require a historical record of past pauses to compute. If pause is
really just columns cleared on resume, as the Constraints say, there is nothing left to measure
against after the fact. One of these two models is wrong; they need to agree before build starts.

### 5. [Medium] Derivation's baseline table misstates FEAT-004's status
`derivation.md`'s "Map at baseline" table lists FEAT-004 (Referrals) as `delivered`. The actual map,
`FEATURES.md` (as committed when the run opened) and the feature file `FEAT-004-referrals.md` both
show `proposed (unrefined)`, minted 2026-07-21 and never built. This was already wrong on the date
the derivation claims to have read it (2026-09-03), not just stale.

This calls the derivation's `composes-with: FEAT-004 — referral credits keep accruing while paused`
relation into question — there's no delivered referral-credit mechanism yet for pause to compose
with. Doesn't block FEAT-005 today since no FR depends on it, but the relation should be dropped or
re-derived once someone confirms which status is right, and it's worth a quick sanity check on
whether other baseline entries in this or other derivations are trustworthy.

### 6. [Medium] SC-002 isn't measurable
*"SC-002 Customers feel in control of their subscription."* SC-001 and SC-003 both name a metric and
a measurement method; SC-002 has neither. As written there's no way to know at the end of three
months whether this criterion passed. Needs a proxy (survey score, complaint-rate delta, NPS
sub-question) and a measurement source, or it should be dropped as a stated success criterion.

### 7. [Medium] FR-005 and US-3 don't define the reminder timing
FR-005: "The system MUST remind the customer before the pause ends." US-3's Independent Test says
"advance to the reminder point" without saying what that point is. Neither the FR nor its source
story specifies how many days before the resume date the email fires, which is needed both to build
the Sidekiq schedule and to write a test for it.

### 8. [Minor] FR-004's timing is looser than its source story
FR-004: "the next box arrives soon after" (resuming early). US-2's own acceptance scenario 2 is more
precise: resuming after the week's cut-off means the following week's box, not the current one. FR-004
should inherit that precision rather than restate it vaguely — an engineer building strictly from the
FR list (without cross-referencing US-2) could miss the cut-off boundary.

### 9. [Minor] FR-009's cited source doesn't support it
FR-009 ("pause flag and billing-schedule recompute MUST be written in one database transaction")
lists "Source: US-1," but US-1's story and acceptance scenarios say nothing about transactions —
this is a reasonable engineering constraint on its own merits, but it isn't traceable to the story
it's attributed to. Either re-source it as a technical constraint (it reads more like the Technical
notes section) or add the language to US-1 so the trace is honest.

### 10. [Minor] FR-006's legal claim is asserted, not sourced
FR-006 ("MUST remain cancellable... as UK consumer law requires") cites a legal requirement with
`Source: —`. There's no decision doc backing the specific legal claim (`docs/decisions/` has only
0004). The requirement itself is sound practice regardless, but a "high rigor" spec asserting a
specific legal basis should either point at a decision doc or drop the legal justification and keep
the requirement on its own merits.

## Stories (read despite the ask to skip — needed for ask 4)

- **US-1**: solid — priority, rationale, an independent test, and three concrete acceptance
  scenarios. Not thin.
- **US-2**: solid, two acceptance scenarios covering the early-resume and cut-off-boundary cases.
- **US-3**: thin on one point — neither the story nor FR-005 says how far ahead of the resume date
  the reminder fires (see Finding 7). Otherwise complete.
- **US-4**: correctly rejected with a clear reason; nothing to flesh out since it isn't being built.
- **Gap, not thinness**: no story anywhere exercises the 8-week cap / 6-week warning from decision
  0004 (Finding 2). That's not a thin story, it's a missing one — worth a US-5 once the requirement
  is written.
