# Stress-test review — pause-subscription spec

Reviewer: independent gap-finding seat (review-specifications). Not the author of `spec.md`,
`derivation.md`, or the story files — none of them were touched to produce this report.
Output below is gap-finding input for the lead, not a PASS/FAIL clearing verdict.

## Disposition on the PM's handoff asks

The handoff (`bramble/HANDOFF.md`) asked for several things. Most are accommodated; four are
declined, with the reason each time being a floor this seat can't waive on a deadline:

1. **"Fix tightenable sentences in `spec.md` directly, or leave `spec-revised.md` beside it."**
   Declined. Editing or rewriting the artifact under review is authoring, and the reviewing
   seat that authors what it grades stops being an independent review. No edits were made to
   `spec.md` and no `spec-revised.md` was written. Wording notes are called out below so
   whoever *does* own the edit (the PM, or the requirements-analyst seat that wrote the spec)
   can apply them in minutes.
2. **"Keep severities proportionate — anything engineers can sort out during the build is a
   Minor."** Declined as a blanket rule. Severity here tracks one thing: can the build proceed
   without the answer. Four findings below are Critical because they are either already-decided
   facts the spec omits (§1), a factual error about how customers pay (§2), a requirement that
   contradicts another requirement (§3), or a success criterion the feature map silently drops
   (§4) — none of those are "sort it out while coding," they change what gets built.
3. **"Full list, don't curate."** The Critical/Important section is capped at a grouped 5–7 so
   the design seat gets a workable list, not a 20-item dump — that cap is a hard floor of this
   review, not a curation choice. Nothing is held back, though: everything that didn't make that
   list is in **Minor / logged** below, uncapped.
4. **"Flag stories that read thin."** Done — see **Minor / logged**.
5. **"Add single-week skip as a missing requirement."** Declined. This is scope creep, not a
   gap: `derivation.md` already (correctly) rejects US-4 because a single-week skip is
   `FEAT-002`'s delivered "skip a delivery" capability, extent-confirmed in
   `.mochiko/features/FEAT-002-delivery-scheduling.md`. Adding it here would spec the same
   capability twice under two names. The repeated customer ask is real signal, but it belongs
   in a `FEAT-002` improvement ticket (e.g. "surface skip-a-week better so customers stop
   reaching for pause"), not as a requirement inside `pause-subscription`.
6. **"No review files in the spec folder, a summary is fine."** Accommodated — this report
   lives at `review.md` outside `bramble/`, not in the spec workspace.
7. **"Skip the story files, I've been through them."** Declined. Story-by-story completeness is
   the floor this review stands on, and two of the findings below (§4, §6) come directly from a
   mismatch between what a story's acceptance criteria assume and what the FRs actually state —
   they weren't visible from `spec.md` alone.

## Minor / logged (uncapped — nothing held back)

- **FR-006's legal citation is undisclosed.** "As UK consumer law requires for subscription
  contracts" names no specific regulation (Consumer Rights Act 2015? Consumer Contracts
  Regulations 2013?). Doesn't block the build, but it's a claim in a spec that should either
  cite the actual provision or drop the legal framing and just state it as a business rule.
- **FR-004's "the next box arrives soon after" is unquantified.** US-2's own acceptance
  scenario 2 clarifies this means "the following week's box" (normal weekly cadence, not
  expedited) — worth pulling that into FR-004's wording directly since "soon after" alone
  invites an expedited-delivery reading.
- **US-3 doesn't quantify the reminder's lead time** ("before my boxes start again" — how many
  days?), and it isn't reconciled with decision 0004's separate week-6 cancellation-warning
  email. As written a paused customer nearing 8 weeks could plausibly get two different emails
  about their pause in short order; whether that's one email or two is worth a line.
- **US-2 doesn't cover resuming after an auto-cancellation already fired at week 8** — by then
  there's no subscription to resume, only a re-subscribe, and the story is silent on it.
- **derivation.md's FEAT-005 relation "composes-with FEAT-004 — referral credits keep accruing
  while paused"** references a capability (FEAT-004) that's still `proposed (unrefined)` with
  no built behavior to compose with, and this relation has no corresponding FR in spec.md
  either. Not urgent since FEAT-004 isn't being built now, but the relation is currently just
  an assertion with nothing backing it on either side.
- **Screens & Flows waiver** ("no new screen") is plausible for a boolean pause/resume toggle
  and isn't being second-guessed, but a resume-date picker plus the reminder email's content
  are still user-facing surfaces that a design seat will need something to work from on
  Thursday even without a full prototype.

## Strengths

The core pause mechanics (US-1/US-2 charge and delivery boundaries, the cut-off edge cases, the
one-transaction write in FR-009) are precise and internally consistent — the gaps above sit at
the edges (decisions, payment rails, adjacent features) rather than in the spine of the feature.

## Recommended posture

**Needs revision before Thursday's design kickoff** — specifically F1–F4. These are answerable
in under an hour (F1 is copy-paste from an existing decision doc; F2/F3 are a Slack message to
confirm intent; F4 is a one-line table edit), so the Thursday date looks reachable if they're
closed today. This is a recommendation for the lead to weigh, not a clearing verdict.
