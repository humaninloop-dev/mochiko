# Stress-test review — shift-swaps spec

Reviewer: independent stress-test pass (mochiko:review-specifications)
Scope: `kestrel/.mochiko/specs/shift-swaps/spec.md`, `stories/US-1..5.md`, `derivation.md`, graded
against `kestrel/CLAUDE.md`, `kestrel/README.md`, `kestrel/docs/`, and the feature map
(`kestrel/FEATURES.md` + `.mochiko/features/*`, `.mochiko/specs/index.md`) as committed at run
open. This is gap-finding input for the lead's own ruling, not a clearing verdict.

recommended_verdict: needs-revision — two direct requirement contradictions and an unresolved
licensing-eligibility gap block build; the feature-map derivation misrepresents its own baseline.

strengths: Clean story-to-FR traceability for the core request/answer/approve loop, an honest
cross-venue rejection with a stated reason, and edge cases that show real venue-operations
thinking (mid-flight shift cancellation, same-day swaps).

## Clarifications

### C1 — When does an accepted swap actually change the published rota?

FR-004 and FR-006 give opposite answers. Options:

1. **Manager approval gates the rota change** (matches FR-006, US-3, and derivation.md's FEAT-001
   delta text): the rota only updates after the manager approves, even though both staff already
   accepted. Slower, but keeps a human in the loop before a licensing-sensitive shift changes hands.
2. **Mutual acceptance changes the rota immediately** (matches FR-004 and US-1 scenario 2); the
   manager's "approval" in US-3 becomes a review-after-the-fact, not a gate. Faster for staff, but
   removes the pre-change check managers asked for in US-3's own rationale.
3. **Approval gates by shift type** — e.g. bar/duty-manager swaps require approval before taking
   effect, usher/box-office swaps take effect on mutual accept. More precisely targeted at the
   licensing-sensitive shifts, but adds a rule the spec doesn't currently have any hook for.

Why this matters: this is the single moment every other requirement (audit trail, notifications,
SC-004's zero-unaudited-changes check) is built around — the spec cannot be implemented with both
answers standing.

### C2 — Does a swap check shift eligibility before it completes?

The spec currently assumes any colleague can take any offered shift. Options:

1. **Swaps enforce the same eligibility rule the rota builder already enforces** (age/sign-off for
   bar, till-training for box office, the duty-manager flag) — a swap that would place someone on
   a shift they're not eligible for is refused with the reason shown, mirroring decision 0003.
2. **Swaps are restricted to same-role exchanges only** — a bar shift can only be swapped for
   another bar shift, sidestepping the eligibility question by construction.
3. **Swaps are eligibility-blind by design**, and the venue manager's approval step (US-3) is the
   intended safety net — in which case FR-006's approval gate becomes load-bearing for compliance,
   not just optional oversight, which should be stated explicitly rather than left as an
   assumption.

Why this matters: this is the same regulatory ground decision 0003 was written to cover
(licensing inspections asking who served alcohol and whether they were signed off); silence here
is a silent waiver of that decision for the swap path.

### C3 — What should staff without push notifications see/receive for a swap request?

The Overview assumes near-universal push coverage, but the venue-facts survey says otherwise.
Options:

1. **Swap notifications follow decision 0007 exactly** — push when available, SMS otherwise, same
   as every other staff notification — and FR-002/003/009 are rewritten to say so explicitly.
2. **Push-only, with a flag to the manager for staff with neither channel** (mirroring FEAT-001's
   existing pattern for staff with no notification channel) — accepted as slower for the ~31% of
   staff who rely on SMS/noticeboard.
3. **Push-only, no fallback, accepted as a known limitation** for this first release — in which
   case SC-001's 80%-within-24-hours target should be caveated or re-baselined against the
   actual push-enabled population.

Why this matters: decision 0007 says "every feature that notifies someone uses these channels and
does not choose its own" — this spec's silence on SMS fallback risks quietly breaking that rule for
roughly a third of the staff population.

### C4 — Is "offer to everyone at the venue" in scope for this run at all?

derivation.md's proposed FEAT-006 entry includes a claim-based, offer-to-all mechanic that this
spec's own Intent rules out of scope (it says that's FEAT-005 / open shifts). Options:

1. **Strike that language from the FEAT-006 entry** — this run stays a one-for-one, named-colleague
   exchange only, and FEAT-005 remains the sole home for the offer-to-all/claim mechanic.
2. **This run is meant to also cover the open-shift-style offer**, and the spec is missing the
   stories/FRs for it — in which case Intent's own "out of scope" line needs to be corrected, not
   the map entry.
3. **The two mechanics are meant to converge into one capability** going forward, and FEAT-005
   should be merged into FEAT-006 rather than kept separate — a bigger map decision than this spec
   alone should make.

Why this matters: as staged, the map write and the spec disagree with each other about what this
feature even is.

### C5 — What is the twelve-month CSV constraint actually for, and does it replace or supplement US-4's in-app history?

Options:

1. **The CSV is a compliance export, separate from and in addition to** the in-app history view in
   US-4 — in which case it needs its own FR, an access-control statement (who on a venue's shared
   drive can open it, given staff-data confidentiality), and an acceptance criterion.
2. **The CSV was a placeholder for "we need year-long retention" and the in-app view (US-4) is
   meant to satisfy it** — in which case the constraint line should be reworded as a capability
   ("retain swap history for twelve months, viewable by managers") and the CSV/shared-drive detail
   dropped as an implementation choice for later.

Why this matters: right now this constraint is unimplemented and untested — nothing in FR/SC
traces to it — while a lookalike requirement (US-4) exists a few sections away with no stated
relationship to it.
