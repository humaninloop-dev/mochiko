## Clarifications

The following are the questions worth taking back to the stakeholder before this spec is buildable.

### 1. When does an accepted swap actually change the rota? (F1)
FR-004 and FR-006 give opposite answers.
- **Option A:** Manager approval gates every swap (US-3's version) — the rota never changes on
  acceptance alone, even for low-risk role pairs. Slower, but matches the stated reason managers
  asked for the review step ("licensing-sensitive shifts").
- **Option B:** Acceptance changes the rota immediately (FR-004's version); manager approval
  becomes a post-hoc review/undo, not a gate. Faster for staff, but "and I approve or refuse it
  with a note" in US-3 currently implies a gate, not an undo.
- **Option C:** Split by risk — bar/duty-manager swaps gate on approval, usher/box-office swaps
  apply immediately. More building, but matches "most swaps will be waved through" from US-3.
- **Why it matters:** this is the core state machine of the feature; every other requirement
  (FR-007's audit trail, FR-003's manager notification, SC-004's rota-vs-audit comparison)
  depends on which one is true.

### 2. Who checks that the counterpart is actually eligible for the shift they're taking? (F4)
- **Option A:** The swap flow runs the same eligibility check the rota builder already runs
  (decision 0003) before a request can even be sent to an ineligible colleague.
- **Option B:** The request can be sent to anyone, but acceptance is blocked with the same
  "refuses an ineligible placement with the reason shown" behavior the rota builder has today.
- **Option C:** No system check; the manager's approval step (US-3) is trusted to catch it.
- **Why it matters:** without one of these, a swap can put an unsigned-off under-18 staff member
  on a bar shift or an untrained staff member on box office — exactly the licensing-inspection
  risk the governance floor and decision 0003 exist to prevent.

### 3. What is swap history, actually — a table or a file? (F3)
- **Option A:** Swap history is rows in the existing `rota_change`/audit mechanism, viewable
  in-app per US-4; drop the CSV-on-shared-drive line from Intent as a stray implementation
  detail.
- **Option B:** Swap history is genuinely exported to a CSV on a shared drive as the system of
  record, and US-4's "manager opens a shift and sees its history" instead means opening that
  file — in which case SC-004's `rota_change` comparison and FR-007 need to say how the CSV
  relates to it.
- **Why it matters:** these produce different data models, different access-control stories
  (who can reach the venue's shared drive?), and different answers to "is this compliant with
  the append-only audit-table principle in CLAUDE.md."

### 4. Is the eleven-hour rest rule right for under-18 staff? (F8)
- **Option A:** Confirm with whoever owns compliance that UK young-worker rest entitlements
  (12 hours, not 11) apply to any staff member under 18, and split FR-005 into an adult rule and
  a young-worker rule.
- **Option B:** Confirm the eleven-hour figure is deliberately venue policy (stricter or looser
  than statute for operational reasons) rather than a literal restatement of the law, and drop
  "as UK law requires" from the requirement text.
- **Why it matters:** the product explicitly serves teenage staff (CLAUDE.md's own governance
  rationale); shipping a wrong statutory claim in a MUST requirement is a live legal-compliance
  risk, not a cosmetic one.

### 5. What should staff without reliable push notifications experience? (F7)
- **Option A:** Swap notifications follow decision 0007 exactly — push where enabled, SMS
  fallback otherwise, flag to the manager if neither — and FR-002/FR-003 are rewritten to say so
  instead of assuming universal push.
- **Option B:** Swap notifications are push-only for this release, and the spec explicitly
  accepts that ~a third of staff won't see a request promptly while on shift, revising SC-001's
  80%-in-24h target accordingly.
- **Why it matters:** roughly a third of staff are reachable by push while working per the 2026
  survey; the spec's own success metric (SC-001) is at risk if the notification path assumed in
  the Overview doesn't match documented device use.

### 6. Are the screens getting designed inside this spec run, or after? (F2)
- **Option A:** Hold the spec open until a SCR/FLOW manifest and a walkable low-fi prototype
  exist for the three declared screens (request, answer, manager view), per the spec's own
  UX-bearing: yes.
- **Option B:** Retroactively waive UX at intent ("No UX surface — prototype waived at intent")
  and treat screens as a follow-on design task outside this spec — but that contradicts the
  Intent's own UX-bearing: yes line and would need that line changed too.
- **Why it matters:** "Figma link to follow" isn't a state this spec workflow has a slot for;
  as written there's nothing to build the request/answer/manager screens from.

### 7. Does shift-swaps duplicate open shifts, or should FEAT-005 be reconciled first? (F5)
- **Option A:** Strike the broadcast/first-claim clause from the proposed FEAT-006 capability
  text entirely — no story supports it, and it's FEAT-005's job.
- **Option B:** If a staff-initiated broadcast offer is genuinely wanted, route it through
  FEAT-005 (currently `proposed (unrefined)`) as that feature's refinement, not as a bolt-on to
  FEAT-006.
- **Why it matters:** FEAT-005 already owns "first to claim it takes it"; letting FEAT-006 grow
  the same mechanic under a different name splits one capability across two map entries and the
  derivation's baseline never even surfaced the overlap because FEAT-005 was left out of the
  "Map at baseline" table.

---

## Strengths

strengths: The spec's core swap loop (US-1/US-2 request-and-answer, with side-by-side shift
detail and clean withdraw/decline/accept paths) is concretely scenario'd and correctly traces
FR-001/FR-002 to it; the manager-approval story (US-3) and the audit-attribution requirement
(FR-007) show real engagement with the venue's licensing and pay-dispute pressures named in the
Overview.

## Recommended verdict

**needs-revision** — not buildable as staged: F1 (self-contradicting rota-update rule) and F2
(no screens for a declared UX-bearing feature) each block implementation on their own, and F4/F8
carry real compliance exposure that should be settled before code, not discovered after.
