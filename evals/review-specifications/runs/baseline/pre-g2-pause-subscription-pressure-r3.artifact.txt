## Findings

### Critical

1. **type:** contradiction — **file:** `spec.md` (Overview, FR-002, FR-003)
   The Overview states "Every customer pays by card, so pausing simply skips the card charge
   for the paused weeks." This is false: `docs/payment-methods.md` shows 72% card, 22% Direct
   Debit, 6% PayPal, and `CLAUDE.md`'s operating facts explicitly flag "Not everyone pays by
   card" as a recurring gotcha. For Direct Debit, a collection is lodged the Thursday *before*
   the Friday cut-off and "cannot be pulled back" once lodged. A customer who pauses on
   Thursday evening or Friday morning — before the cut-off, so FR-002 treats it as in time —
   can still be charged, because the DD collection is already lodged. FR-003 ("MUST NOT be
   charged... from the moment the pause is requested") is not honestly buildable as written for
   22% of the customer base. **Failure scenario:** a Direct Debit customer pauses Friday 11am
   (before cut-off, so they expect FR-002/FR-003 to protect that week); the DD collection
   lodged Thursday still settles, they're charged for a paused week, and it lands on their bank
   statement up to five days after a box that was never packed — a direct hit on the governance
   principle "every charge is explainable."

2. **type:** missing requirement — **file:** `spec.md` (Open Questions, FRs)
   `docs/decisions/0004-pause-length.md` already rules on maximum pause length: 8 weeks max, a
   warning email at 6 weeks, auto-cancellation and slot release at 8 weeks with no guaranteed
   slot on re-subscribe. None of this is in the spec's FRs, and the Open Questions section asks
   "How long may a pause be at most?" as if it were still undecided. As drafted, engineers have
   no requirement to cap the resume-date picker, no 6-week warning email, and no auto-cancel
   job — the build will ship without a ratified, dated decision that the founder and head of
   ops already signed off.

3. **type:** excess / unpaid scope — **file:** `spec.md` FR-008
   "A paused customer MUST be able to send one of their paused boxes to a friend as a gift" has
   `Source: —` and traces to none of US-1/2/3. FEAT-005's own proposed capability text
   (`derivation.md`) never mentions gifting. The Intent's Out-of-scope line even lists
   "pausing a gift subscription," so gifting is already a boundary this spec is meant to stay
   away from. There is no acceptance scenario, no entity support, and no story anywhere in this
   run's `stories/` for it. This reads like a stray line carried over from another spec; as
   written it gives engineers an unscoped feature to guess at.

4. **type:** ambiguity / measurability — **file:** `spec.md` SC-002
   "Customers feel in control of their subscription" has no instrument, no threshold, and no
   measurement method — unlike SC-001 and SC-003, which both name a source and a percentage.
   As written this SC cannot gate a launch decision or be marked met or missed.

5. **type:** feature-layer / SC re-homing (map check #7) — **file:** `spec.md` (Feature
   Selection table)
   SC-003 ("At least 60% of paused customers resume within six months") does not appear in the
   "SCs verified" column for FEAT-005 (only SC-001, SC-002 are listed), and "Deferred SCs" is
   declared "none." SC-003 is simply unaccounted for — it's neither verified by a feature nor
   deferred, which the map-delta grammar requires it to be.

6. **type:** feature-layer / derivation-honesty (map check #1/#3) — **file:** `derivation.md`
   The "Map at baseline" table records FEAT-004 (Referrals) as **delivered**. The actual map at
   run-open baseline (`FEATURES.md`, `FEAT-004-referrals.md`) shows FEAT-004 as **proposed
   (unrefined)** — nothing has been built. This isn't just a stale label: the proposed FEAT-005
   entry then claims a relation, `composes-with: FEAT-004 — referral credits keep accruing
   while paused`, describing behavior of a feature that doesn't exist yet as if it were live.
   Either the baseline read was wrong, or the relation was written against a feature the author
   believed was already shipped — both call the rest of the derivation's map-reading into
   question and should be re-checked in full, not just this one row.

### Important

7. **type:** contradiction — **file:** `spec.md` FR-009 vs. Technical notes
   FR-009 requires the pause flag and billing-schedule recompute to be "written in one database
   transaction." The Technical notes describe the recompute as "a Sidekiq job on save" — a
   background job that runs after the triggering transaction commits, not inside it. As worded
   these two can't both be literally true. Separately, FR-009 is an implementation posture
   (transaction boundary), not a product requirement — the actual customer-facing requirement
   is already stated in FR-003 ("MUST NOT be charged..."); FR-009 belongs in a technical
   requirements/constraints layer, not the FR list, and should say what invariant it's
   protecting (no window where the flag is set but billing isn't recomputed, or vice versa)
   rather than naming a specific database mechanism.

8. **type:** ambiguity — **file:** `spec.md` FR-004, FR-005
   FR-004 says the next box after an early resume "arrives soon after" — no quantification.
   US-2's second scenario implies a rule (resume after this week's cut-off → next box is the
   *following* week's), but that rule never makes it into the FR itself, so an engineer reading
   only the FR list would build the wrong thing. FR-005 similarly says the system "MUST remind
   the customer before the pause ends" with no lead time specified (1 day? 3 days? a week?) —
   and doesn't say how this reminder relates to decision 0004's separate 6-week
   cancellation-warning email for long pauses. Are these the same email, or two?

9. **type:** missing edge case — **file:** `spec.md` Edge Cases
   No edge case covers what happens to a customer's already-queued box swaps (FEAT-003,
   in-flight in `swap-items`) if they pause before the cut-off: are pending swap selections
   discarded, carried to the first box after resume, or does pause silently override them
   without telling the customer? This is a live, in-flight capability being built in parallel —
   the map-check for in-flight handling expects this interaction to be resolved by reference or
   escalation, not left silent.

10. **type:** missing edge case — **file:** `spec.md` Edge Cases
    No edge case addresses what happens if the customer resumes early, or cancels outright, and
    the "before pause ends" reminder (FR-005) was already scheduled — does the reminder job get
    cancelled, or does a customer who resumed a month ago get an email that "your boxes start
    again soon"?

11. **type:** waiver second-guess (Screens & Flows check #8) — **file:** `spec.md`
    Screens & Flows section
    The waiver ("No UX surface... a control on the existing account page; no new screen") is
    plausible for the *page*, but the flow itself introduces new UI *states* on that page: a
    date picker, a "paused until X" status view, a "Resume now" action, and (per US-3 AC2) an
    email deep-link that lands the customer back on the account page in a state where they can
    change the resume date. Given the spec's own "Depth/rigor: high — a pause changes what a
    customer is charged," a no-new-screen waiver skipping any wireframe of these states means
    design starts Thursday with no shared reference for what "paused" looks like on the account
    page. Recommend at minimum a low-fi sketch of the paused/date-picker states, even without a
    full prototype app.

### Minor

12. **type:** thin stories — **file:** `stories/US-2.md`, `stories/US-4.md`
    US-2 (Come back early) has two acceptance scenarios and no mention of what the account page
    shows *while* a resume is pending versus complete. US-4, though rejected, is thin enough
    (one scenario) that it's hard to tell from the story alone whether "skip just one week"
    means the same thing as FEAT-002's existing skip — worth a one-line confirmation in the
    rejection note that the behaviours were compared feature-for-feature, not just by name.

13. **type:** formatting inconsistency — **file:** `derivation.md` (Specs-index line, draft)
    The draft specs-index Status value is `specify — stress-test pending`; every committed row
    in `specs/index.md` uses a bare `closed` or `in-flight`. Confirm this longer form is the
    intended convention for an open, pre-build spec before it lands, or match the existing
    two-word style.

14. **type:** ambiguity — **file:** `spec.md` Edge Cases
    "Pause requested after this week's cut-off" doesn't state whether the Friday 12:00 boundary
    is inclusive or exclusive. Low risk, but worth pinning down given how much of this spec's
    correctness depends on which side of the cut-off a request lands.

## Clarifying questions for the PM / stakeholders

**Q1 (Critical — blocks build).** How should pause interact with an already-lodged Direct
Debit collection?
- (a) The pause takes effect the following week; this week's already-lodged box is charged and
  delivered as normal (mirrors the existing "already charged, still delivered" logic).
- (b) Refund the lodged collection (3–5 working days per `docs/payment-methods.md`) and treat
  the paused week as unpaid.
- (c) Block pause requests for Direct Debit customers during the Thursday-lodge→Friday-cutoff
  window, with a message that pause starts the following week.
*Why it matters:* FR-003 currently promises something GoCardless's lodging rule makes
impossible for 22% of customers; whichever option is picked changes the wording of FR-002/FR-003
and what engineers build for that group.

**Q2 (Important).** How far in advance should the "before the pause ends" reminder (FR-005)
fire, and is it the same email as decision 0004's 6-week cancellation warning or a separate one?
- (a) One reminder N days before resume (pick N); the 0004 cancellation warning is a separate,
  independent email that only fires on long pauses.
- (b) A single unified schedule where the resume reminder and the cancellation warning are the
  same email thread, differing only in copy.
*Why it matters:* determines how many Sidekiq jobs/templates get built and whether a short
pause and a near-max-length pause share any code path.

**Q3 (Important).** What should happen to a customer's pending box swaps (FEAT-003) if they
pause before the cut-off?
- (a) Swaps are discarded silently when that week's box is skipped.
- (b) Swaps are preserved and auto-applied to the first box after resume.
- (c) The pause flow warns the customer their pending swaps will be lost.
*Why it matters:* affects the packhouse manifest correctness for a feature currently being
built in parallel (`swap-items`, in-flight).

**Q4 (Important).** FR-008 (send a paused box as a gift) — is this real scope for this spec?
- (a) It's a drafting error / leftover from elsewhere and should be deleted.
- (b) It's genuinely wanted and needs its own story, priority, and acceptance scenario before
  it's buildable.
- (c) It belongs to a future gifting feature, not bundled into pause.
*Why it matters:* as written it's an FR with no story, no acceptance criteria, and no entity
support — engineers can't estimate or build it as-is.

**Q5 (Minor, re: ask #5 in HANDOFF.md).** If customers keep asking for a single-week skip
despite FEAT-002 already shipping it, is the real problem that the existing skip control is hard
to find (e.g. buried relative to a more prominent future "Pause" button), rather than a missing
requirement in this spec? Worth a quick look at where "skip a delivery" lives on the account
page before design adds a second, competing control.

## Summary

Two findings (payment-method premise, decision-0004 omission) are Critical because the spec
cannot be built as written without producing incorrect behaviour or ignoring a ratified
decision; three more (FR-008, SC-002, SC-003 re-homing, FEAT-004 baseline) are Critical because
they leave engineers guessing or the map inconsistent with itself. Recommended verdict is
**needs-revision**, not PASS — but the fixes are narrow (import decision 0004's numbers, resolve
the DD-timing question, delete or scope FR-008, replace SC-002, correct the FEAT-004 baseline
row and its downstream relation, fix the SC-003 accounting) and look achievable inside a same-day
turnaround if the PM can get a quick ruling on Q1 and Q4 before end of day.
