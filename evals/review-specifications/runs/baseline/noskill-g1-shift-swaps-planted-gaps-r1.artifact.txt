# Stress-test review — shift-swaps

Reviewer note: the `mochiko:review-specifications` skill was not available in this
environment, so this review was conducted manually against the same materials it would
have used — `kestrel/CLAUDE.md`, `kestrel/README.md`, `kestrel/docs/`, `kestrel/FEATURES.md`
and feature entries, and the `shift-swaps` workspace (`spec.md`, `stories/`,
`derivation.md`). No input files were modified.

## Verdict

**Not ready to leave draft.** Two findings are contradictions serious enough that a build
started today would ship the wrong behaviour: an eligibility bypass that undoes a ratified
licensing safeguard, and a self-contradicting rule about when a swap actually changes the
published rota. A third finding shows the map-derivation step missed an existing feature and,
as a direct result, wrote scope back into the new capability that the spec itself declares
out of scope. None of the three is a matter of taste — each is checkable against a document
the team has already committed to.

---

## Critical

### 1. The swap eligibility assumption undoes decision 0003's whole point

`spec.md` Assumptions (line ~129): *"Any colleague at the venue is eligible to take any
shift offered in a swap."*

This contradicts `docs/decisions/0003-role-flags.md` and `docs/roles-and-eligibility.md`
directly: bar shifts require 18+ and a recorded alcohol-service sign-off, box office
requires till training, duty manager requires the flag — and decision 0003 says the rota
builder "refuses an ineligible placement with the reason shown" precisely *because*
"managers asked for the rota to stop them making the mistake, not to warn after." A swap is
another path by which a person ends up on a shift; nothing in FR-001–FR-009 checks the
counterpart's role eligibility for the shift they're being handed, and no edge case covers
an ineligible counterpart.

The manager-approval gate (US-3, FR-006) is not an adequate backstop: US-3 relies on the
manager *noticing* a mismatch by eye ("the swap shows both staff, both shifts, and the
roles involved"), which is exactly the manual/after-the-fact pattern decision 0003 was
written to eliminate. And if FR-004 (see #2) governs instead, the swap takes effect before
any manager sees it at all.

**Recommendation:** drop the assumption; add a functional requirement that a swap is only
offerable/acceptable between shifts whose role each party is eligible for (mirroring the
rota builder's existing check), and add an edge case for the rejection.

### 2. FR-004 and FR-006 contradict each other on when the rota changes

- FR-004: *"An accepted swap MUST take effect on the published rota the moment both staff
  have accepted."* (Source: US-1, US-2)
- FR-006: *"A swap MUST NOT change the published rota until the venue manager has
  approved it."* (Source: US-3)

These can't both be true. US-1's own acceptance scenario 2 backs FR-004 ("When the colleague
accepts, Then the published rota shows the two shifts exchanged"), while US-3 — the reason
P2 manager approval exists at all ("Managers asked for a say on licensing-sensitive
shifts... most swaps will be waved through") — requires the opposite. `derivation.md`'s
proposed FEAT-001 delta sides with FR-006 ("acceptance: an **approved** swap shows on the
published rota"), so the derivation and the spec disagree with each other too.

This isn't a nuance to resolve during implementation — it decides whether the manager
approval step in US-3/FEAT-006 does anything at all, and it compounds finding #1: if FR-004
wins, there is no manager checkpoint left to catch an ineligible swap.

**Recommendation:** pick one flow (US-3's story and the licensing rationale both point to
manager-approval-gates-the-rota) and correct FR-004 and US-1's acceptance scenario 2 to
match — "accepted" should mean "queued for manager approval," not "live."

## High

### 3. The derivation missed FEAT-005 and, as a result, wrote out-of-scope capability into FEAT-006

`derivation.md`'s "Map at baseline" table (lines 7–12) lists only FEAT-001 through
FEAT-004. It omits FEAT-005 (Open shifts, `proposed (unrefined)`), which is on the live
`FEATURES.md` at the moment this run opened. The derivation then states: *"No capability on
the map covers a staff-initiated change to a published shift, so shift swaps is minted new;
nothing to dedup against."*

That's only true because FEAT-005 was left off the table. FEAT-005's own description is "a
manager offers an unfilled shift to eligible staff; the first to claim it takes it" — and
the proposed FEAT-006 entry drafted immediately after (lines 25–27) adds: *"a staff member
can also offer a shift to everyone at the venue and the first eligible colleague to claim it
takes it."* That is FEAT-005's mechanic, restated almost verbatim, folded into the new
feature. It also flatly contradicts `spec.md`'s own Out-of-scope line: *"offering a shift
with no counterpart (that is open shifts)"* — no story in this spec (US-1–US-4) describes an
all-staff offer/claim flow at all.

**Recommendation:** fix the baseline table to include FEAT-005, redo the dedup reasoning
with it present, and strip the offer/claim clause out of the proposed FEAT-006 entry before
it's written to the live map — it belongs to FEAT-005, not this run.

### 4. The Overview's adoption claim is contradicted by the venue-facts doc it should have used

`spec.md` Overview: *"Every staff member carries the Kestrel app and has push notifications
on, so a swap request reaches its counterpart within seconds and most swaps settle the same
day."*

`docs/staff-devices.md` (the 2026 survey, explicitly referenced from `README.md` as the
source for this kind of claim) says: 69% have the app installed, only 52% of *those* have
push enabled (≈36% overall), and only 41% use the app while on shift — with the doc's own
"practical reading" stating push alone reaches about a third of staff at work. Decision 0007
exists precisely to handle this: staff notifications fall back to SMS, and a staff member
with neither channel is flagged to the manager. The spec never mentions the SMS fallback or
the manager-flag case anywhere in the FRs, edge cases, or technical notes — it's written as
if push-only were sufficient.

This matters beyond wording: SC-001 ("80% of swap requests receive an answer within 24
hours") is calibrated against a "reaches everyone in seconds" assumption that the team's own
data doesn't support, and FR-009's push-retry detail (see #7) treats push as the only
channel worth engineering for.

**Recommendation:** correct the Overview to match `staff-devices.md`, and add a requirement
that swap notifications follow decision 0007's channel logic (push → SMS → manager flag) the
same as every other feature.

## Medium

### 5. FR-003's "promptly" is not deliverable through the channels decision 0007 assigns to managers

FR-003: *"The system MUST notify the venue manager promptly when a swap is accepted."*
Decision 0007 (lines 10–12): manager notifications go only "in-app (the manager dashboard
bell)" and "a daily email digest at 07:00 venue time" — "Nothing goes to managers by SMS,"
and the decision's own Consequences line says every notifying feature must use these
channels, not invent its own. For a swap that's licensing-sensitive and (per FR-006) blocked
until the manager approves, "promptly" could in practice mean "up to 24 hours later" if the
in-app bell isn't checked. The spec doesn't acknowledge this tension or say whether the bell
alone is considered "prompt" enough.

**Recommendation:** either reword FR-003 to match what the bell channel can actually
guarantee, or flag the mismatch as a decision to revisit rather than leaving "promptly"
unreconciled with 0007.

### 6. The swap-request state model is missing two outcomes the stories describe

`spec.md` Key Entities lists states as *"requested · accepted · declined · approved ·
expired"* (line ~83). Two acceptance scenarios describe outcomes not in that list:

- US-1 AC3: the requester withdraws before an answer — "the colleague sees it was
  withdrawn." No `withdrawn` state.
- US-3 AC2: the manager "refuses it with a note." `declined` is already used for the
  colleague's answer in US-2 — a manager refusal is a different actor and a different
  meaning, and FR-007 requires every state change to be attributable to its actor, which a
  merged or missing state can't cleanly support.

**Recommendation:** add `withdrawn` and a manager-specific `refused` state (or otherwise
disambiguate colleague-declined from manager-refused) before this becomes the audit schema.

### 7. About half the functional requirements aren't actually backed by the story cited as their source

- FR-002's expiry clause ("MUST expire after a reasonable time") cites US-2, which has no
  scenario about timeouts.
- FR-003 cites US-3, whose scenarios start from "an accepted swap" already existing — none
  covers the manager being notified of the acceptance itself.
- FR-005 (eleven-hour rest rule) cites US-1, which has no rest-conflict scenario.
- FR-008 (dark-mode toggle) cites nothing (`Source: —`) and appears in no story at all —
  it also reads oddly next to the rest of this spec's concerns.
- FR-009 (three retries through the notification worker queue) cites US-2, which never
  mentions retry behaviour, and is an implementation detail more granular than anything else
  in this FR list.

Individually some of these are minor (FR-005's rule is legally sound even if untraced), but
taken together roughly half the FRs have no real scenario behind their citation. That's a
pattern worth flagging to whoever is deriving requirements, not just fixing FR-by-FR.

**Recommendation:** either add scenarios that actually cover expiry, manager-notify, and
rest-conflict, or correct the sourcing; drop FR-008 or justify it; move FR-009's retry count
to technical notes unless a story actually calls for it.

### 8. The CSV/shared-drive retention constraint is stated once and then never implemented

Intent Constraints (lines 17–18): *"swap history is kept as a CSV file on the venue's shared
drive for twelve months."* This never reappears — not in the Functional Requirements, not
in Key Entities, not in Technical notes (which covers the `swap_requests` table, the
notification worker, latency targets, and polling interval, but no export). US-4 (swap
history) is specified purely as an in-app manager view.

**Recommendation:** either add an FR for the CSV export and retention, or, if the in-app
history view in US-4 has superseded the paper/CSV habit this constraint was describing,
say so explicitly and drop the constraint instead of leaving it stranded.

## Low

### 9. SC-002 has no owner in the Feature Selection and isn't deferred either

The Derived features table maps SC-001 and SC-003 to FEAT-006 and SC-004 to the FEAT-001
delta. SC-002 ("Venue managers spend half as much time on swap admin... within three
months") isn't listed against either feature, and "Deferred SCs: none" says nothing was
knowingly pushed out. It simply falls through.

### 10. SC-003 has no measurement method

SC-001, SC-002, and SC-004 each state how they'll be measured (swap log, asking managers,
nightly audit comparison). SC-003 ("Managers find the swap flow intuitive") states no method
at all, unlike its neighbors — as written it can't be confirmed or refuted.

### 11. Edge cases are asymmetric and skip expiry

Only the requester's shift being cancelled mid-request is covered; the counterpart's shift
being cancelled while a request is open is the same scenario from the other side and isn't
listed. Separately, FR-002's expiry ("MUST expire after a reasonable time") has no matching
edge case describing what happens on expiry — whether both parties are told, and whether it
is recorded per FR-007.

### 12. The offline open question conflates the venue tablet with the staff phone app

Open Questions asks: *"Should we cache the published rota on the device so swaps show up
when the venue wifi is down?"* CLAUDE.md's offline governance principle is scoped to "the
venue tablet" for clock-in and the day's rota. Swaps are explicitly a staff-app (phone)
flow per this spec's own UX-bearing line. These are different devices with different
connectivity assumptions (a personal phone on mobile data vs. a venue tablet on the
building's wifi); posing the tablet's offline requirement as an open question for the phone
app risks importing a constraint that doesn't apply, or missing that the phone case is
different and unaddressed.

---

## What holds up

- FR-007 (every state change recorded with actor and time) is well-grounded directly in the
  operating manual's audit principle, and matches decision 0007-style traceability.
- US-5 (cross-venue swaps) is cleanly filtered: correctly marked rejected in both the story
  table and the Filter rejections section, with a disposition that matches the Intent's own
  scope boundary.
- The FEAT-001 delta's *direction* (rota publishing needs to accept a swap-driven
  reassignment) is the right shape of change even though its exact trigger condition
  conflicts with FR-004 (#2 above).
