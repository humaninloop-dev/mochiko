# Pause subscription

> Spec: pause-subscription
> Created: 2026-09-01
> Status: draft — stress-test pending

---

## Intent

- **Scope boundary:** a customer pauses their box subscription for a chosen period from the
  account page and resumes on the chosen date or earlier.
- **Delivery:** whole feature in one run.
- **Depth / rigor:** high — a pause changes what a customer is charged.
- **UX-bearing:** no — pause is a control on the existing account page; no new screen.
- **Constraints:** pause state is a boolean column on the customer record, set and cleared by
  the pause flow.
- **Out of scope:** skipping a single week's box (delivery scheduling); cancellation; pausing
  a gift subscription.

---

## Overview

Customers go on holiday, get a glut from the allotment, or run out of freezer space. Today they
can skip at most two deliveries in a row; anything longer means cancelling and re-subscribing,
which loses the delivery slot. Pause stops boxes for a chosen period without losing the delivery
slot. Every customer pays by card, so pausing simply skips the card charge for the paused weeks.

## Edge Cases

- Pause requested after this week's cut-off → applies from the following box.
- Resume date earlier than the next delivery day → first box on the next delivery day.

---

## Functional Requirements

- **FR-001** A customer MUST be able to pause their subscription from the account page by
  choosing a resume date. *Source: US-1*
- **FR-002** A pause MUST take effect from the next billing date; a box already charged is
  still delivered. *Source: US-1*
- **FR-003** A customer MUST NOT be charged for any box from the moment the pause is
  requested until the resume date. *Source: US-1*
- **FR-004** A customer MUST be able to resume before the chosen date; the next box arrives on
  the next available delivery day (see Edge Cases). *Source: US-2*
- **FR-005** The system MUST remind the customer before the pause ends. *Source: US-3*
- **FR-006** A paused subscription MUST remain cancellable at any time with no charge, as UK
  consumer law requires for subscription contracts. *Source: —*
- **FR-007** The pause flow MUST NOT display or store a full card number or bank account
  number. *Source: governance — payment details never touch our systems*
- **FR-008** A paused customer MUST be able to send one of their paused boxes to a friend as a
  gift. *Source: —*
- **FR-009** The pause flag and the billing-schedule recompute MUST be written in one database
  transaction. *Source: US-1*

---

## Key Entities

- **Subscription** — existing; box size, delivery day, payment method, status.
- **Pause** — subscription, requested at, starts, resumes, ended early (yes/no).

---

## Success Criteria

- **SC-001** 90 % of pauses complete without a support ticket in the following seven days,
  measured from support-system tags over the first three months.
- **SC-002** Customers feel in control of their subscription.
- **SC-003** At least 60 % of paused customers resume within six months, measured from the
  subscription log.

---

## Blockers

### B1 — FR-003 and FR-008 directly contradict each other
- `spec.md` FR-003: "A customer MUST NOT be charged for any box **from the moment the pause is
  requested** until the resume date."
- `spec.md` FR-008: "A paused customer MUST be able to send **one of their paused boxes** to a
  friend as a gift."
- A "paused box" cannot simultaneously not exist (not charged, not packed — confirmed by US-1's
  own acceptance scenario 2: "no charge is made and no box is packed") and be sent to someone.
  FR-008 also has `Source: —`: it traces to no user story, no edge case, and no rationale anywhere
  in the workspace. It reads like content that doesn't belong to this spec.
- **Why blocker, not Minor:** this can't be "sorted out during the build" — an engineer has no
  correct behaviour to build; the two requirements need the spec owner to pick one meaning (most
  likely: drop FR-008 entirely) before work starts.

### B2 — Decision 0004 (max pause length) isn't in the requirements, and is re-asked as an open question
- `docs/decisions/0004-pause-length.md` is ratified and "in force": max 8 weeks paused, a warning
  email at 6 weeks, auto-cancellation and slot release at 8 weeks with no resume.
- `spec.md` has no FR implementing any of this. Worse, the Open Questions section asks "How long
  may a pause be at most?" as if it were still undecided.
- As written, FR-001 lets a customer "pause their subscription... by choosing a resume date" with
  no upper bound enforced, no six-week warning, and no auto-cancel — directly contradicting a
  decision whose stated purpose is protecting scarce delivery slots (the exact resource this
  feature's Overview says pause exists to protect).
- **Why blocker:** this is a missing product decision being implemented, not an implementation
  detail — it needs FRs (and likely a Key Entity field for "warning sent") before build, not
  after.

### B3 — "Every customer pays by card" is false, and the gap it hides is real
- `spec.md` Overview: "Every customer pays by card, so pausing simply skips the card charge for
  the paused weeks."
- `docs/payment-methods.md`: 72% card, 22% Direct Debit, 6% PayPal. Direct Debit collections are
  **lodged the Thursday before the Friday cut-off** and "a lodged collection cannot be pulled
  back" — reversing one is a refund (3–5 working days), not a cancellation.
- FR-003's promise ("MUST NOT be charged... from the moment the pause is requested") is not
  achievable as stated for a Direct Debit customer who pauses on, say, Thursday evening or Friday
  morning: their collection for the coming box is already lodged and will land regardless of the
  pause. The spec has no requirement covering this case (block vs. auto-refund, and the 3–5 day
  lag customers would see).
- **Why blocker:** this affects 22% of active subscriptions and needs a product decision (refund
  automatically? disclose the lag? block pausing until the collection clears?), not an engineering
  workaround discovered mid-build.

## Major

### M1 — FR-006 puts cancellation back in scope after Intent explicitly excludes it
`Intent → Out of scope` lists "cancellation." FR-006 then requires "A paused subscription MUST
remain cancellable at any time with no charge... *Source: —*" — no story backs it either. If the
intent is just "don't let pause block the existing FEAT-001 cancel flow," say that and drop the
MUST; as written it reopens cancellation as in-scope work with no acceptance criteria, and doesn't
say what happens to the pause fields (`pause_starts_at`/`pause_resumes_at`) when a paused sub is
cancelled.

### M2 — FR-009 contradicts the Technical Notes' own description of the recompute
FR-009: "The pause flag and the billing-schedule recompute MUST be written in one database
transaction." Technical Notes: "the billing schedule is recomputed by a **Sidekiq job** on save."
A Sidekiq job runs out-of-process after the enqueuing transaction commits — it cannot share a
transaction with the flag write. One of these two statements is wrong; an engineer will hit this
on day one.

### M3 — SC-002 isn't measurable
"Customers feel in control of their subscription" has no instrument (survey, NPS proxy, ticket
sentiment, anything) attached, unlike SC-001 and SC-003 which both name a measurement source. The
Intent explicitly calls this spec "high" rigor "because a pause changes what a customer is
charged" — the one qualitative SC is the one with no way to check it was met.

### M4 — Key Entities' "Pause" object doesn't match the actual schema in Technical Notes
Key Entities: `Pause — subscription, requested at, starts, resumes, ended early (yes/no)`.
Technical Notes: `customers` gets a flag plus `pause_starts_at` / `pause_resumes_at` — two columns,
on the existing table, no history. There's no column for "requested at" or "ended early." Either
Pause is a real row with history (then Technical Notes is incomplete) or it's just two columns
(then Key Entities is describing a table that won't exist, and "ended early" — needed for SC-003's
resume-rate measurement — has nowhere to live).

### M5 — derivation.md's baseline snapshot misstates FEAT-004's status
`derivation.md`'s "Map at baseline" table lists FEAT-004 (Referrals) as `delivered`. The committed
`FEATURES.md` and `FEAT-004-referrals.md` both show `proposed (unrefined)` — never built. The
proposed FEAT-005 entry carries `composes-with: FEAT-004 — referral credits keep accruing while
paused`, which assumes a referral-credit mechanism that doesn't exist yet. This relation should be
dropped (or reworded to "N/A — FEAT-004 not yet built") before FEAT-005 is written to the live map.

---

## Minor

### N1 — FR-005's reminder timing is undefined
"The system MUST remind the customer before the pause ends" — no offset given, and US-3's
acceptance scenarios use "the reminder point" without pinning a number either. Probably fine as a
build-time parameter, but worth an explicit line saying so, rather than leaving it looking like an
oversight.

### N2 — US-2 is the thinnest of the four stories
Two acceptance scenarios, and no scenario for the interaction with US-3: if a reminder is already
scheduled and the customer resumes early, does that reminder get cancelled? Not a blocker, but
this is the story most worth fleshing out, per handoff ask #4.

### N3 — FR-006's legal citation isn't sourced
"as UK consumer law requires for subscription contracts" is asserted inline with no
`docs/decisions/` reference behind it, unlike FR-007 which correctly cites the governance doc.
Worth a real citation (or a decisions entry) so the claim can be checked later rather than taken
on faith.

## Wording — fixed directly in spec.md

Per the handoff, these were tightened in place since they were phrasing-only:
- **Overview**: split a run-on sentence and replaced "without losing anything" with "without
  losing the delivery slot" (the antecedent two sentences earlier).
- **FR-004**: replaced "the next box arrives soon after" with "the next box arrives on the next
  available delivery day (see Edge Cases)" — this only restates the Edge Cases bullet already in
  the doc, no new decision made.

Not touched: the "every customer pays by card" sentence in the Overview (B3). That one reads like
a wording fix but isn't — correcting it requires a product decision about Direct Debit handling,
so rewriting it quietly would misrepresent an open blocker as resolved. No `spec-revised.md` was
needed; both safe edits were small enough to land directly.
