# Action Plan — Spec: Reporting (`specs/reporting/spec.md`)

## Phase 0 — Frame the gap between what was asked for and what was evidenced

**What I do:** Before writing anything, I settle the single most consequential judgement on this card.

The request thread asks for *"a dashboard with charts, exportable to Excel"* — a solution shape, stated by sales and the founder, with no user problem attached beyond "deals brought it up." The fourteen support tickets describe a different, sharper problem: nine of them are finance admins hand-totalling the reimbursement-run CSV in a spreadsheet for two to three hours every month end, and one of them says explicitly that their accountant *"does not use Excel"* and wants CSV.

I write the spec against the evidenced problem — month-end totals that reconcile — and treat charts as a lower-priority presentation layer over that same data. I do not silently overrule Priya and Tom: the divergence goes in the spec in writing, as an assumption plus an open question they own, so the sprint conversation happens on the page and not after the build.

**What I refuse:** I refuse to write "dashboard with a few charts and an export button" as the requirement. It has no measurable done-condition and it does not solve the two-to-three-hour problem. If someone insists the spec must be exactly that, I would push back once, with the ticket counts, and if overruled, record it as a directive in Assumptions naming who took the decision.

## Phase 2 — Resolve the seven decisions the spec cannot be written without

These are the real work of this card. Each is a decision the input does not make, and each changes the numbers on the screen. For each I state the default I would write and, where it is not mine to take, the stop.

1. **Which date puts an expense in a month.** The tickets conflict directly: one asks *"if an expense was incurred in July but approved in August, which month is it in?"* and says their books run by the month it happened. `docs/data-notes.md` says an expense can be approved in a later month than incurred, and the run picks up whatever is approved and unpaid regardless of month. `docs/glossary.md` says there is no first-class period. **Default: bucket by `date_incurred`**, because it matches how the complaining customers keep their books — recorded as an assumption, with a stated consequence that totals for a closed month can move when a late approval lands, and a requirement that the report shows the date basis on screen and in the export header so nobody misreads it. **This is an open question owned by finance** (Tom settled the analogous 180-day question with finance in the reference spec, so that is the precedent). Branch if the ruling is `approved_at` instead: FR on bucketing flips, the "totals can move after close" assumption is deleted, and reconciliation to a run becomes exact by construction rather than by filter — cheaper, but it stops matching the customers' books, so I would want that trade-off said out loud.

2. **Which statuses count.** A ticket says their totals came out short because an expense was still waiting on a manager. **Default: totals include approved and paid only**; pending (filed, undecided) is excluded from the total but shown as a separate, clearly-labelled "not included — awaiting approval" figure with a count and amount, which is exactly what that ticket needs to chase approvals before closing. Rejected and withdrawn never count (`docs/data-notes.md` says they are never in a run).

3. **Which amount.** A ticket says staff file in EUR, the company reimburses in GBP, and they never know which number to use. `docs/data-notes.md` says every expense stores `converted_amount` in company currency, fixed at the filing-day rate. **Default: every total is in the company currency, summed from each expense's stored `converted_amount`** — never re-converted, never converted from a summed original, because re-converting would break penny reconciliation against the run. Original currency appears only as a per-expense column in the export.

4. **Penny reconciliation.** The strongest ticket line is *"they need to match what the run paid out to the penny, or my accountant bounces it."* This becomes both a requirement (sum of stored converted amounts, rounding applied once at display, never to intermediate subtotals) and the headline success criterion. Note the tension with decision 1: bucketing by `date_incurred` while runs are built from approval state means a month's report and a month's run are *not* the same set. I resolve this by specifying **two views over one dataset**: a period view (by `date_incurred`, for the books) and a **run reconciliation view** keyed to a chosen reimbursement run, which is the one that must tie to the penny. This is the single design judgement I am most confident in and the one I would most want reviewed.

5. **Manager visibility of team spend.** Three manager tickets want team spend for the month and the quarter. `docs/roles.md` says a manager loses sight of a report's expense 30 days after the decision, and states plainly that whether a manager should see their team's approved spend over time *has come up twice and was never decided — both times the answer was "ask finance."* **I will not guess this.** Widening a deliberate visibility boundary is a data-exposure decision and it is not the analyst's to take. **Default: manager team-spend reporting is Out of Scope for this spec**, with the reason and the two prior asks recorded, and an Open Question addressed to finance. **Stop:** I would confirm this with Tom/finance before the sprint planning that consumes this spec. Branch if finance says yes: it comes back as a P2 story with its own requirements on the retention window (the 30-day rule must be explicitly superseded for the reporting surface only, and totals-only vs line-level access decided separately — I would default to totals-only, no per-expense detail, as the smaller exposure). Branch if no: it stays out of scope permanently and the manager tickets get answered as "won't build."

6. **Export format.** Priya and Tom say Excel; a paying customer says their accountant does not use Excel and wants CSV; the only export that exists today is the run CSV. **Default: CSV, matching the existing run export's conventions**, which also means the finance admins can diff it against what they already handle. XLSX goes in Out of Scope with the note that CSV opens in Excel, so "exportable to Excel" is satisfied in substance. This is the second thing I would flag to Priya, since it is her literal words I am not honouring.

7. **Archived categories.** A ticket says they archived "Client entertainment" and cannot find its historical total. `docs/glossary.md` and `docs/data-notes.md` both say archived categories keep their id and their expenses still resolve to the name. **Requirement: archived categories appear in totals for periods in which they have expenses, labelled as archived.** No open question here — the data supports it.

Two smaller ones I decide without ceremony: **cost-center totals use the cost center stored on the expense** (copied at filing, finance may override; moving an employee does not re-key history — so the report reflects history as it was booked, which is what an accountant wants), recorded as an assumption; and **a month with zero expenses renders an empty report with a zero total, not an error or a blank screen**, since `docs/data-notes.md` calls out that seasonal businesses have them.

**Also refused / redirected:** the manager ticket asking for *"approvals sitting more than a week"* is an approvals-queue aging feature, not reporting. It goes in Out of Scope with a pointer that it belongs in a separate request, so it is not lost. The manager ticket referencing *"the budget finance gave me"* implies a budget object that exists nowhere in the data notes; I flag that comparison-to-budget is unbuildable today and out of scope.

## Phase 3 — Draft the spec

**What I write:** `specs/reporting/spec.md`, the eight house sections in order, header as `Feature: Reporting`, `Status: Draft`, `Author: analyst seat`, `Date: 10 September 2026`.

Planned content:

- **Overview** — two or three paragraphs, written from the finance admin's month end: today they export the run CSV and hand-total it for two to three hours; after this they open a period, read totals by cost center and by category, check the figure against the run, and export it.
- **User Stories** —
  - **US-001 (P1)** finance admin sees a period's approved spend totalled by cost center and by category. Scenarios cover: the happy total; a month containing an expense filed in EUR (total is in company currency); a month containing an archived category (it appears, labelled); a month with no expenses (zero, not an error).
  - **US-002 (P1)** finance admin reconciles a period's totals against a named reimbursement run to the penny. Scenarios cover the exact match, and the case where an expense is incurred in one month and approved in the next (the report explains where it landed rather than silently differing).
  - **US-003 (P1)** finance admin exports the current view as CSV. Scenarios: the file downloads with totals and a header stating the date basis, period, currency, and filters; an empty period exports a valid file with a zero total, not a zero-byte file.
  - **US-004 (P2)** finance admin sees what is still awaiting approval and therefore excluded, so they can chase before closing.
  - **US-005 (P2)** employee sees their own claimed and reimbursed totals for a chosen period and exports the list — answers both employee tickets, and adds no visibility risk because it is their own data, which `docs/roles.md` already grants.
  - **US-006 (P3)** spend by category and by month shown as charts on the reporting page — this is Tom's ask, kept, sized honestly, and placed after the data it draws.
  - Each story closes with an **Independent test** in the reference spec's style (a concrete, runnable check with real figures — e.g. file two expenses in one month, one in EUR, approve both, run a reimbursement run, confirm the report total equals the run total to the penny).
- **Functional Requirements** — roughly FR-001 to FR-015, each with `*Source: US-XXX*`, covering: date basis and its visible labelling; status inclusion and exclusion; company-currency totalling from stored converted amounts; rounding applied once, never to subtotals; run reconciliation as an exact equality; archived categories included and labelled; zero-expense periods; cost center as stored on the expense; permission scoping (finance sees the company, employee sees only their own, no manager cross-report access in this spec); period selection bounded to a stated maximum span with a stated default; CSV shape, encoding, and header block; export response-time and row-count limits. Every quantity is a number, not an adjective.
- **Success Criteria** — SC-001 report total equals the reconciled run total exactly, verified across a fixed sample of companies; SC-002 median finance time from opening the report to a filed month-end figure under 15 minutes, against the two-to-three-hour baseline the tickets state; SC-003 a 12-month export completes within a stated time bound; SC-004 reporting-tagged support tickets on totalling fall to near zero within a quarter of launch, against the fourteen in Q3.
- **Assumptions** — A-001 date basis; A-002 status inclusion; A-003 currency and rounding; A-004 cost center as booked; A-005 CSV rather than XLSX; A-006 the spec is written to the ticket evidence rather than the literal "dashboard with charts" wording, and why.
- **Open Questions** — the date-basis ruling (finance, via Tom); manager team-spend visibility (finance — the twice-deferred question, named as such); CSV-vs-Excel confirmation (Priya); whether the run reconciliation view is worth P1 weight or can follow (Tom, at sprint planning).
- **Out of Scope** — manager team-spend and the 30-day window change, pending the ruling; comparison to budget, since no budget exists in the data; approvals-aging queue, a different feature; XLSX; scheduled or emailed reports; anything cross-company.

**Test I would run:** there is no code here, so the check is a self-review pass against `specs/README.md`: all eight sections present and in order; every FR carries a Source naming a story that exists; every story has a priority, at least one Given/When/Then, and an independent test; no adjective standing in for a number; every open question names an owner. I expect that pass to come back clean, and I expect two or three requirements to need a number I have to choose rather than derive — those become assumptions rather than being left vague.
