## Rung-claim honesty (advisory — does not drive the verdict)

Per `mochiko:patterns-plan-minimalism`, graded against `design-disclosure.md`'s ladder:

- **D-005 Redis TTL, claimed rung "3 already exists"** — not honest. IP-003 mandates provisioning a brand-new managed Redis instance; nothing today runs on Redis (sessions are PostgreSQL per D-002). This claim is the same defect as G2 viewed through the minimalism lens.
- **tasks.md Cycle 2 task list, claimed rung "5 builder's room"** — misapplied. Builder's room is for decomposition judgment left to the builder; listing concrete file paths in advance is the opposite of that, and is the exact pre-written-decomposition pattern the ladder exists to catch. Same defect as G12.
- **GET availability-forecast, claimed rung "4 minimum now," justified by "marina ops asked for it at kickoff"** — unverifiable against every artifact in this package (spec, stories, sufficiency report). Combined with G1, this rung claim reads as backfilled justification for scope that was never approved, not a real minimalism judgment.
- **D-004 offer_locks, claimed rung "2 simpler shape"** — the claim itself is plausible, but a "simpler than X" comparison presumes X was actually considered; since no real alternative was evaluated (G3), the rung claim can't be verified as honest or dishonest — it's ungrounded either way.

## Cross-artifact concerns noted, not graded here

## Gaps in the spec itself (for the requirements analyst)

Noted while reading spec.md and its stories, separate from the design-package grading above:

1. **No FR covers waitlist-entry expiry.** The Edge Cases section states "the date range on the entry has passed with no berth freed: the entry expires and the owner is told," but no FR-XXX or success criterion backs it. This is very likely *why* the design round left it uncovered (G9 above) — there's no requirement forcing anyone to design or test it. Recommend adding an FR (e.g., "the system MUST mark a WaitlistEntry expired when its toDate passes with no offer issued, and notify the owner") plus a measurable criterion for the sweep interval.
2. **No FR or acceptance scenario covers the "offer withdrawn on leave" edge case in Given/When/Then form** — it appears only as an Edge Cases bullet, not in US-1 or US-3's scenarios, and there's no vocabulary for what state the withdrawn offer should end up in (feeding G10 above). Recommend folding it into US-3 as an explicit scenario.
3. **The "vessel no longer fits the freed berth" skip-logic edge case** is implied by FR-003's "whose vessel fits" phrasing but has no explicit acceptance scenario in any story (US-2 or US-3) verifying that the skipped entrant keeps their position. Worth an explicit Given/When/Then given it's easy to get wrong (off-by-one in position renumbering).
4. **No FR requires a durable notification record** — US-2 says "they are notified" and US-3 implies the same, but nothing in the spec calls for a persisted notification/audit trail. The design round invented one (Notification entity, citing a nonexistent FR-007 — G11 above); worth the analyst deciding whether a notification record is an actual product requirement or purely an implementation choice, and either adding the FR or telling design not to model it as a first-class entity.
5. **Out of scope list doesn't foreclose adjacent capabilities like "availability forecasting."** Not itself a defect, but the ease with which G1's unproposed forecast feature slipped in suggests the scope boundary in Intent could be tightened (or the sufficiency gate hardened) to make late-round scope creep more visibly a rule violation rather than a judgment call.
