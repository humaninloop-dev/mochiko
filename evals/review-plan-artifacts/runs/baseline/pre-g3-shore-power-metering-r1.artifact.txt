## Notes for the lead

The headline is G5: two producing seats signed off on opposite ingest architectures, and neither
this round's authors nor round 1's review caught it. The store delta the user signed at the round-1
checkpoint is a push design; this round's constraints doc asserts push doesn't exist and decides
pull, on a source marked "memory-asserted." The contract still ships the push endpoint anyway, so
the package now contains three different pictures of how a reading gets into Halyard, and none of
the five cycles build the one the constraints doc actually chose. This isn't a wording fix — it
needs the architect back at the table to either re-verify Enerlink's real capability or re-open
AX-013.

G6 is worth flagging to the lead on its own even apart from severity accounting: it's a billing test
whose oracle enacts the exact bug US-2 was written to prevent, on a feature whose own spec calls out
"Depth / rigor: high (billing)." Whichever seat wrote Cycle 4 should re-check the rest of the money
math by hand before re-issuing.

## Questions for the producing seats

- Has anyone actually confirmed Enerlink v3's push/pull capability against real vendor documentation, or is C-005 still resting on memory?
- If pull is confirmed correct, does product accept relaxing FR-002/SC-001 from 5 minutes to up to 60, or does the architecture need a different shape to hit 5 minutes under a pull-only vendor?
- What story or FR is the consumption-export endpoint actually for — is "US-6" a typo, or a carry-over from a different feature's numbering?
