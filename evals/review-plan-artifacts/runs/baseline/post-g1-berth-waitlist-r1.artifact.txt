## Clarifications needed

### C1: Does marketing consent gate the offer e-mail? (C6)

**Question**: C-006 leaves open whether the offer notice is a transactional e-mail (sent regardless of marketing consent) or gated behind marketing opt-in.
**Options**: 1. Transactional — always sent, no consent gate (matches how booking-confirmed mail behaves today). 2. Gated behind marketing consent. 3. A distinct "service" consent category, separate from marketing.
**Why it matters**: If gated, an owner who opted out of marketing could miss their offer entirely and lose their place — which would need its own spec-level rule (e.g. a fallback channel or an explicit warning at join time). This also decides whether Postmark template selection differs by consent state.

## For the requirements analyst — spec-level observations

Outside this skill's scope (completeness-grading the design package), offered as requested. Not part of the verdict above.

- **spec.md FR-006 vs US-4.md**: FR-006 lists the fields marina staff must see as "position, date range, and vessel length." US-4's own Given/When/Then adds a fourth field — "whether an offer is open" — that FR-006's text omits. Minor, but it's the kind of drift that let C1 (a fully missing endpoint) slip through unnoticed.
- **No FR/SC for waitlist-entry auto-expiry**: the "date range has passed with no berth freed" behavior exists only as an Edge Cases bullet, with no FR-XXX, no SC, and no independent test named. That's a plausible root cause of I5 — the design phase had no measurable target to design against, so it designed nothing.
- **No requirement addresses a Vessel edited after joining**: if an owner's vessel length changes after they join a waitlist (e.g. corrects a typo, or gets a bigger boat), FR-003's fit-check is silent on whether re-evaluation happens. Worth a line either way — "checked at join time only" or "re-checked at offer time" have different implementations.
