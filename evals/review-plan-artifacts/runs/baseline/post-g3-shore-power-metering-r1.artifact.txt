## Findings

### 1. [Critical — material divergence, auto-fail] `consumption-export` traces to a story that doesn't exist

**At:** `contracts/api.yaml:62-71`, cross-referenced against `design-disclosure.md:13` and
`.mochiko/specs/shore-power-metering/spec.md`.

`GET /marinas/{marinaId}/consumption-export` is summarized as "for finance (US-6)". This
feature's spec has four stories, US-1 through US-4 — there is no US-6 anywhere in
`spec.md`, its `stories/` folder, or the sufficiency report's gap list (G-01–G-05). The
disclosure entry justifies it as rung "4 minimum now, claim: finance asked for a CSV" — an
off-record ask with no traceable FR, SC, or story backing it. This is scope the design phase
added on its own initiative, not a gap the sufficiency report scoped the design phase to close.

Per the review's own material-divergence floor, an artifact no gap named auto-fails the package
regardless of the other counts. I'm reporting this as the auto-fail trigger; the lead rules on
whether to cut the endpoint or retroactively source a real requirement for it.

**Fix:** either drop the endpoint from this round's package, or route it back through
requirements-analyst/product-manager to mint a real story/FR it can trace to — it cannot land
under a citation to a story ID that isn't in this feature.

### 2. [Critical] The re-issued package disagrees with itself — and with the signed architecture — on push vs. pull

**At:** `constraints-and-decisions.md:16-45` (C-005, D-004) vs. `store-delta.md:20-26` (signed,
prior set) vs. `contracts/api.yaml:10-14` and `tasks.md:23-38` (both re-issued, new set).

- `constraints-and-decisions.md` (round 2, "D-004 rationale rewritten" per disclosure) states
  the gateway has **no push** ("there is no per-reading push") and rules D-004 as **hourly pull
  by the worker**, with "consumption freshness is up to 60 min."
- `store-delta.md`, signed by the user at the round-1 checkpoint, rules the opposite: SPN-007
  "receives gateway pushes on its own listener," and states outright — "push over pull: the
  5-minute visibility target (FR-002) needs the gateway to tell us, not us to ask."
- `contracts/api.yaml` (re-issued) implements the push side: `POST /gateway/readings`, "the
  gateway pushes a batch of readings," HMAC-signed, per-marina secret.
- `tasks.md` Cycle 1 (re-issued) tests the push side too: a `curl -X POST
  .../gateway/readings` round-trips into the consumption endpoint.

So within the round-2 package, `constraints-and-decisions.md` is the odd one out against its own
sibling files, and it also un-rules an already-signed architecture decision without saying so.
Round 1's G3 asked only for D-004's *rationale* to stop restating the choice — the round-2 fix
rewrote the rationale but never reconciled the choice itself against the signed delta, so the
underlying defect survived the "fix."

This is squarely a design-vs-decided contradiction, which is this seat's to call Critical. I'm
separately flagging — not grading — that if pull-hourly is in fact what ships, "freshness up to
60 min" also contradicts FR-002 and SC-001's 5-minute MUST target; that specific
requirements-vs-constraints angle belongs to `mochiko:review-feasibility`, not to me.

**Fix:** the lead needs a single ruling on which of C-005/D-004 or the signed SPN-007/SPN-009
delta is correct for what Enerlink v3 actually supports, then make every artifact in the
package agree with it.

### 3. [Critical] Cycle 4's own oracle asserts the exact mis-billing US-2 exists to forbid

**At:** `tasks.md:74-88`, graded against `stories/US-2.md:6`.

Cycle 4's setup: tariff 20p to 15 September, 30p from 16 September; 10 kWh used 10 September,
10 kWh used 20 September. The correct line per FR-003 and US-2's own acceptance scenario is
10 kWh × 20p + 10 kWh × 30p = 500p (£5.00) — US-2 spells this out almost with these exact
numbers: "never 20 × 30p." Cycle 4's **Assert** instead requires "The shore-power line total is
600 (20 kWh at the tariff current at generation, 30p)" — i.e., it prices every kWh at whichever
tariff is live when the invoice runs, not the tariff live when the power was used. That is
precisely the bug the story names and rejects. If a builder makes this card go green as
written, they will have shipped the mis-billing US-2 was written to prevent.

This card wasn't touched in round 2 (disclosure's `round_2_changes` only names Cycle 5 as new),
so it's a carry-over defect the round-1 review didn't catch either — flagging now regardless of
which round should have caught it.

**Fix:** rewrite Cycle 4's assert to interval-price each reading against the tariff in force at
its own `readAt` (500p / £5.00), not the tariff in force at generation time.

### 4. [Critical] US-4 (P2, backfill + gap notice, FR-005) has no design surface anywhere

**At:** `tasks.md` (all 5 cycles), `contracts/api.yaml` (all 4 paths), cross-referenced against
`spec.md:27` (US-4) and `data-model.md:45` (MeterReading traced to FR-005/US-4).

No cycle covers US-4. No endpoint or schema field carries a backfill trigger or a gap notice —
the `Consumption` schema has no field for a backfilled-period notice at all. Yet
`MeterReading.source` (`gateway`/`backfill`) and `data-model.md`'s own traceability line
explicitly cite FR-005/US-4, so the data shape anticipated backfill while nothing downstream
ever wires it up. This is a full required story with zero contract, zero cycle, and zero
data-surface follow-through, in a "depth: high (billing)" feature where an un-backfilled gap
silently unders-bills or wrongly disputes an invoice line. Neither the sufficiency report's gap
list nor round 1's review named this, but "every P1/P2 story on ≥1 card" is a design-completeness
floor on `tasks.md` itself, independent of what the gap list happened to enumerate.

**Fix:** add a cycle (and the accompanying contract surface — a backfill-trigger path or a
`gapNotice` field on `Consumption`) for US-4/FR-005 before this can be called complete.

### 6. [Important] Cycle 2 is a horizontal layer tested against a test-suite re-run, not real infrastructure

**At:** `tasks.md:41-52`.

"Readings storage layer and repository" is a repository/storage concern, not an observable
end-to-end behavior — it fails vertical integrity on its face, and its own **Case** field admits
it ("Split — storage is its own concern"). Its **TEST:** is `npm test -- readings.repository`,
which is a test-suite re-run, not an exercise against real infrastructure through the running
system the way every other cycle in this file does it (curl, `npm run invoice:generate`). The
disclosure calls this "rung 5 builder's room," but a builder's-room framing doesn't cure a card
that violates the `**TEST:**` grammar outright. This card wasn't touched in round 2 either.

**Fix:** either fold the storage/repository work into Cycle 3 as an implementation detail behind
its end-to-end test, or give Cycle 2 its own end-to-end TEST case (e.g., a reading round-trips
through the union view via the consumption endpoint) instead of a unit-test invocation.

### 7. [Important] D-005's own disclosure contradicts its adopt-first claim, and the decision record has no real alternative

**At:** `constraints-and-decisions.md:47-56` (D-005) vs. `design-disclosure.md:12,16`.

The disclosure's ladder entry for D-005 reads "twelve plain tables beat a partitioning
extension nobody here has run" — naming a specific alternative. Two lines later, the same
document's `adopt_first` block claims `shelf_named: no ... nothing to adopt` for the same
decision. Those can't both be true. Worse, the named alternative is mischaracterized: PostgreSQL
declarative partitioning has been core (not an extension) since PG10, ships with the already-
approved PostgreSQL 16, and needs no new dependency or sign-off under C-003 — it's a stronger
adopt-first candidate than the write-up credits. Separately, D-005's own options table lists
exactly one option, so there's no comparison on record at all, regardless of what the disclosure
says about it.

**Fix:** either genuinely evaluate native partitioning against the hand-rolled ring-buffer tables
in D-005's options table (cost, ops burden, prune semantics) and record why it loses if it does,
or adopt it — but stop asserting "nothing to adopt" in the same document that names a shelf
candidate three lines away.

### 8. [Important] Declared cycle dependencies don't match the cycles' own narrative

**At:** `tasks.md:41-71`.

Cycle 2 states it delivers "the repository methods the later cycles read through." Cycles 3 and
4 both declare "Depends on: C1" only — neither cites C2, despite Cycle 3 (the consumption view)
being exactly the kind of later-cycle Cycle 2 says it feeds. Either the dependency field is
wrong, or Cycle 2's stated purpose is overstated; either way a builder following the dependency
graph as written could start Cycle 3 before the repository it's supposed to read through exists.

**Fix:** add C2 to Cycle 3's (and check Cycle 4's) `Depends on:` line, or narrow Cycle 2's stated
scope so it's clear it isn't a hard prerequisite for the view cycles.
