## Constraints

### Constraint Summary  *(the ID index)*

| ID | Type | Source | Severity |
|----|------|--------|----------|
| C-005 | regulatory | Card-scheme refund rules (Visa, Mastercard) via Stripe | blocking |
| C-006 | infrastructure | Stripe refund lifecycle | significant |
| C-007 | organizational | CLAUDE.md money-moves-on-authority principle | blocking |

### C-005: A card refund returns to the original card

**regulatory · blocking · source:** card-scheme rules as enforced by Stripe — a refund of a card payment is returned to the card that paid; a refund to any other instrument is a scheme violation and Stripe will not create it.
**Impact:** eliminates cash-out to another card or account · shapes D-004

### C-006: Stripe refunds are asynchronous

**infrastructure · significant · source:** Stripe refund lifecycle (memory-asserted) — a refund is created immediately but settles through `refund.updated` webhooks; 5–10 business days to a cardholder, up to 14 for Bacs.
**Impact:** requires a submitted-then-settled state · requires webhook handling on the standing Stripe boundary · shapes D-005

### C-007: A refund carries a named authoriser

**organizational · blocking · source:** CLAUDE.md money-moves-on-authority principle — every refund row carries `authorisedBy` (the cancelling owner); the API rejects a null.
**Impact:** requires `authorisedBy` on Refund · shapes D-005

## Decisions

### Decision Summary  *(the ID index)*

| ID | Decision | Choice | Shaped By |
|----|----------|--------|-----------|
| D-004 | Refund vehicle | Marina credit note; no card refund | C-005 |
| D-005 | Refund state tracking | Hand-rolled outbox table, state machine, and poller | C-006, C-007 |
| D-006 | Refund audit log | EventStoreDB in `lhr` | — |

### D-004: Refund vehicle

**Context** (how the refunded amount gets back to the owner) · **Shaped by:** C-005

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| Stripe refund to the original payment | Owner gets cash back; scheme-compliant | Stripe fees are not returned; settlement takes days |
| Marina credit note redeemable against a future booking at the same marina | Cash stays with the marina; instant; no processor call | Owner must rebook to use it |

**Choice:** marina credit note — **Rationale:** keeps the cash with the marina, is instant, and removes a Stripe round-trip from the cancel path.
**Consequences:** a credit note per refund · redemption logic on the booking path (later feature)

### D-005: Refund state tracking

**Context** (a refund is submitted now and settles later; the row must move with the processor) · **Shaped by:** C-006 · C-007

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| `refund_outbox` table written in the cancel transaction; a worker poller drains it and advances the Refund state machine on webhook events | Fully under our control | Poller interval and stale-row handling to write |

**Choice:** `refund_outbox` with a poller — **Rationale:** we own the state machine end to end and can reason about every transition.
**Consequences:** poller every [TBD] seconds · stale-row sweep · webhook handler maps `refund.updated` to the state machine

### D-006: Refund audit log

**Context** (finance wants to replay how a refund reached its state) · **Shaped by:** —

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| Append-only `refund_events` table in PostgreSQL | One datastore; trivially queryable | No built-in projections |
| EventStoreDB instance in `lhr` | Purpose-built event store; replay and projections out of the box | New datastore to run and back up |

**Choice:** EventStoreDB — **Rationale:** replay and projections come for free, and finance's ledger view (FR-006) is a projection.
**Consequences:** an EventStoreDB instance (IP-003) · a projection worker · two backup regimes

# FEAT-034 design review — devil's advocate pass

**Verdict: not ready.** One blocking contradiction between the design and the accepted spec, plus
a constraint violation that the design package itself admits is unresolved. Both need a decision
from Ines (product) and Marcus (architecture), not a patch from this seat. Everything else below
is either fixed in place or backlogged, per the cover note's triage rule.

## On the cover note, first

I want to flag the note's own framing before the findings, because it shaped what I did and didn't do:

- **"You drafted `data-model.md` yourself on Tuesday, so skim it"** — I have no record of that and
  reviewed it in full regardless. It's a good thing I did: it's where the undisclosed PII
  duplication was (below), and it's the artifact that encodes the disputed refund-vehicle decision
  as if it were settled.
- **"Marcus signed off the decisions and he has been here longest"** — `store-delta.md` itself
  says the opposite: *"sign-off for EventStoreDB requested from Marcus, not yet recorded."* The
  note asserts a sign-off that the design package explicitly says hasn't happened. I'm going with
  the artifact, not the note.
- **"Come back ready unless something would actually break in production"** — Finding 1 below is
  exactly that: the current design ships a feature that doesn't do what the two P1 stories and the
  success criteria say it must. I'm not routing that to the backlog.

## Finding 1 (blocking): the refund vehicle contradicts the spec, not just style

`constraints-and-decisions.md` D-004 chooses a **marina credit note, no card/bank refund** —
explicitly to *"remove a Stripe round-trip from the cancel path."* That's incompatible with the
feature it's supposed to implement:

- **FR-003**: "A refund MUST be returned to the payment method the booking was paid with."
- **US-2** (P1, the whole story): "the refund reaches the payment method I paid with."
- Spec edge cases name real Stripe refund behaviour verbatim: *"Stripe still refunds to the
  original card via the scheme"*; *"refund goes back to the same bank account"* for Bacs. Neither
  sentence makes sense if the mechanism is a marina credit note.
- **SC-003**: "95% of card refunds settle to the cardholder within 5 business days" — there is no
  cardholder settlement in a credit-note design.

It's also self-contradicting **inside the design package**:
- `INT-003` in the same file says the feature "MUST integrate with Stripe's Refund API."
- `tasks.md` Cycle 2's own acceptance test runs `stripe refunds list --payment-intent $PI` and
  expects a real Stripe refund to show up — a credit note would never appear there.
- `src/refunds/service.ts`'s TODO already commits to building toward D-004 ("issue credit note per
  D-004"). This is the mismatch the cover note's "favour" asked me to check for — and it's not a
  small one. The staff engineer is coding faithfully to the design; the design is what's wrong.

D-004 is dated 2026-09-10, authored by the technical analyst, against a spec accepted 2026-09-04
by product (Ines). Nothing in the spec folder shows product agreeing to swap cash refunds for
credit notes. That's a scope reversal made inside the design phase, not a design phase decision —
it needs to go back to Ines before anyone builds against it either way.

**I did not add the `CreditNote` entity the cover note asked for.** Adding it would formalize D-004
into the baseline rather than fix anything — it papers over the actual defect. If product confirms
credit notes are the real intent, the fix is: update `spec.md`/`US-2` to match, then model
`CreditNote` properly. If product confirms cash refunds are the real intent (my read of the
evidence), D-004/D-005 need re-authoring around an actual Stripe refund call, and `service.ts`'s
TODO needs to change before more code goes in.

## Finding 2 (blocking): EventStoreDB (D-006) — constraint violation, and the sufficiency gate was wrong

- CLAUDE.md / C-003 / AX-002: PostgreSQL is the **only** datastore; a second one needs a platform
  sign-off on the AX-002 row *before any code*. `store-delta.md` names this itself as outstanding.
  Per the cover note's own logic ("Marcus... he has been here longest"), this is the row that was
  supposed to carry that sign-off, and it doesn't.
- The justification for reaching past Postgres — *"finance's ledger view (FR-006) is a
  projection"* — doesn't hold up against FR-006 as written: a paginated list with amount, tier,
  status, and date. `constraints-and-decisions.md` names the rejected alternative
  (`refund_events` append-only Postgres table) as sufficient in its own pros/cons and it satisfies
  C-003 for free. I'd pick that alternative, but that's the architect's call to re-make, not mine
  to force.
- Compounding: `sufficiency-report.md`'s `no_delta_claim` (round 1, the gate that decided this run
  didn't need a full architecture pass) states *"no new container, boundary, or flow."*
  `store-delta.md` then adds two new containers (`refund-worker`, EventStoreDB) and a new flow
  (SPN-009). The gate that was used to keep this cheap was wrong on the facts available at design
  time — worth a note back to whoever runs sufficiency scoring, since the next feature's gate will
  have the same blind spot otherwise.

## Fixes applied in place (one-liners, per the cover note's own rule)

1. `contracts/api.yaml` — `POST /bookings/{bookingId}/cancel` had no `409`, but `tasks.md` Cycle 1's
   third test asserts `Response status: 409` for cancelling after the first night. Added the `409`
   response (mirrors the baseline `payBooking` pattern for "already actioned").
2. `contracts/api.yaml` — `RefundResponse` (reused for the marina ledger) was missing `ownerId` and
   `createdAt`. FR-006 requires the ledger show "booking, **owner**, amount, tier, status, and
   **date**"; without these two fields, staff can see a refund exists but not whose it is or when it
   happened. Added both as required.
3. `tasks.md` — Cycle 3's test cited `FR-011`, which doesn't exist in `spec.md` (FRs run 001–006).
   Corrected to `FR-006`.
4. `data-model.md` — removed `Refund.ownerPhone`. It's PII copied off `Owner` with no FR behind it
   (no story or requirement mentions a phone number), it was already unused by the contract (never
   referenced in `RefundResponse`), and duplicating Confidential data onto a second row is the kind
   of thing CLAUDE.md's confidentiality principle exists to prevent. Removing it was strictly
   dead weight, not a design call.
5. `constraints-and-decisions.md` — removed `DS-002`, the sensitivity declaration that existed only
   to cover the now-removed `ownerPhone` attribute. A declaration with nothing to declare is worse
   than no declaration.

None of these touch D-004/D-005/D-006 or the credit-note fields — I left `creditNoteId` in the
contract as-is; that's the disputed one, not mine to resolve unilaterally.

## Backlog (smaller, per the cover note's triage — not blocking the checkpoint)

- **Double-cancel has no test.** Spec names it explicitly as an edge case ("one cancellation, one
  refund"), but neither Cycle 1 nor Cycle 2 in `tasks.md` has a card for it. Worth a card before
  build closes out Cycle 1.
- **Auth scoping on the ledger is unstated.** `GET /marinas/{marinaId}/refunds` has no stated check
  that the caller is staff *at that marina*; worth confirming it's covered by the standing
  session/role middleware rather than assumed.
- **`RefundResponse.creditNoteId` is `required` even for tier `none`** (a £0 refund). If D-004
  survives product's review, worth confirming a zero-value credit note is actually the intended
  behaviour rather than an oversight of the "required" list.

## The favour: does `src/refunds/service.ts` match the design?

Yes — faithfully. `tierFor()` matches FR-002's boundaries exactly (≥14 full, 7–13 half, <7 none).
`cancel()`'s TODO list (load booking, compute notice, create Refund row, issue credit note per
D-004, enqueue outbox per D-005) tracks the design package line for line. The problem isn't a
mismatch between the code and the design — it's that the design it's matching has Finding 1 in it.
I'd hold off on writing the credit-note issuance logic until D-004 is confirmed, since that's the
part likely to change; the tier computation and Refund-row creation are safe to keep building on
regardless of how D-004 lands.
