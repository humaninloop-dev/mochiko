## Clarifications needed

### C1: Does the offer e-mail need marketing consent, or is it transactional? (G6)

**Question**: C-006 leaves this open with a live [NEEDS CLARIFICATION] marker. Does an owner who has opted out of marketing e-mail still receive offer notices?
**Options**: 1. Treat as transactional — always sent regardless of marketing consent · 2. Gate on marketing consent · 3. Add a separate, non-optional "service notice" consent class
**Why it matters**: shapes the Postmark template and whether FR-003's offer notice can be legally sent to every entrant.

### C2: Is the availability-forecast capability actually in scope for FEAT-031? (G4)

**Question**: The design package built a full endpoint/entity/cycle for a 90-day berth availability forecast sourced to a verbal "kickoff" ask, with no US-5/FR-009 in the accepted spec.
**Options**: 1. Pull it from this package; let the PM/analyst decide if and when it becomes its own story · 2. Retroactively amend spec.md with a real US-5/FR-009 and re-run this design round against it · 3. Confirm it was approved out-of-band and have the lead attach that record to the package
**Why it matters**: as it stands this is unauthorized scope — it consumed design-phase budget FR-006/US-4 (a real P1-adjacent P2 story) didn't get.

## Cross-Artifact Concerns

- **G1 + G9 together**: the missing `lapsed`/`withdrawn` states and the inverted decline-test oracle both point at the same underlying gap — the design phase never worked through the full Offer lifecycle beyond the happy path (open→accepted). The lapse/decline/withdraw trio deserves one pass, not three separate patches.
- **G2 + G11**: if D-005's Redis path is kept, the AX-002 sign-off and a real NFR-XXX target for SC-002 should land on the *same* store consult, not two separate follow-ups — they're both consequences of finally admitting this round is not no-delta.
- **G3 vs G4**: the package spent full design attention (endpoint, schema, disclosure entry, cycle card) on invented scope (US-5) while dropping a real, in-spec P2 story (US-4) to zero coverage. Worth the lead asking directly how that trade happened.
- Feasibility-flavored observations handed off, not graded here: whether the `offer_locks` polling approach (D-004) can actually hold to SC-002's 60s p95 once the sweeper and 500ms poll interval are accounted for, and whether Redis keyspace-notification "at-most-once" delivery (D-005's own stated consequence) can meet a hold-expiry requirement that money depends on, are buildability questions for `mochiko:review-feasibility`, not this review.

## Gaps noted in the spec itself (for the requirements analyst, not scored above)

1. US-2 requires the entrant be "notified" when an offer issues, and the design phase built a Notification entity against it, but cited it to **FR-007** — a requirement that doesn't exist in spec.md (FRs stop at FR-006). If a notification is genuinely required behavior, it's worth promoting to a real FR rather than leaving it implicit in story prose.
2. The Edge Case "entrant leaves while holding an open offer → offer withdrawn" has no FR backing either — FR-005 only names "declined or lapsed." Worth deciding whether "withdrawn" is a third first-class outcome or a special case of one of the other two, since that decision is what the data model got wrong (G9).
3. SC-002's measurement ("from the booking release commit to the offer row's issuedAt") doesn't say what happens when two berths free in the same minute (the spec's own edge case) — is the p95 measured per release event, or could a legitimately-serialized second offer under C-007 count against the same window as a false negative? Tightening this would remove some of the ambiguity the design phase inherited into D-004.
4. If the availability-forecast capability (see C2 above) is a real product need, it has no home anywhere in spec.md's scope boundary or out-of-scope list — right now it's neither explicitly included nor excluded, which is exactly the gap that let it get built without a story.
