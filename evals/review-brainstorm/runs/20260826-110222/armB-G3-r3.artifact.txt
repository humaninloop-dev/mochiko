# Phase 0 — Blind Angle Map

Produced from the topic/goal line alone, before record contact.

Topic: Threadline (team chat product) — moving 4,000 teams from invoiced annual
contracts onto self-serve card billing.
Goal line: one decision record covering the migration path, price continuity,
and what happens to a team that does not act.

## Angles a thorough record should address

1. Migration mechanics: cutover vs staggered, batch/wave logic, rollback if a
   wave reveals a defect.
2. Price continuity: grandfathering, duration, glide path vs cliff, treatment
   of heavily-discounted/enterprise rates, currency.
3. Non-action path: grace period, suspension definition, data retention /
   export / deletion timeline, reactivation.
4. Payment infra: processor choice, PCI scope, card-on-file capture, dunning
   on failed (not just absent) cards.
5. Tax/legal: US sales tax, international VAT/GST, filing/registration
   obligations, need for tax counsel.
6. Contract/legal: does moving off invoicing conflict with existing MSA terms,
   notice-period requirements, ToS updates.
7. Multi-year / non-standard contracts: any teams outside the standard annual
   cycle, and how they map onto "next renewal."
8. Communication plan: audience (admin vs all users), channel, cadence,
   coverage across the *entire* risk window (not just pre-renewal).
9. Sales/CS involvement: white-glove handling for top-revenue accounts,
   account-manager carve-outs.
10. Seat reconciliation: additions AND removals, proration symmetry, dispute
    path for reconciliation errors.
11. Self-serve plan changes: upgrade and downgrade paths, and what happens to
    demand for one when it's blocked.
12. Support/internal tooling: what operators need, access control and audit
    logging for any override capability.
13. International considerations: currency conversion, SCA/3-D Secure for EU
    cards.
14. Success metrics / instrumentation for the migration itself.
15. Whale/large-account carve-outs distinct from the standard self-serve flow.

This map is intentionally broad; materiality of any gap is judged against it
during the cold read, not every angle demands its own finding.

Solo end-stage review of record.md (Threadline self-serve billing migration,
4,000 teams). No lens was briefed, so I took the whole surface. No
fact-checker claim map or repo exists in this workspace, so external/factual
claims below are flagged as unverifiable rather than confirmed — treat them
as open, not as "I checked and it's fine."

Phase 0 blind angle map is in phase0-blind-angle-map.md, produced from the
topic/goal line before record contact. Diff against the frozen record below.

## Findings (most severe first)

**1. [Critical] D3 and D6 contradict each other during the exact window that matters most.**
Decisions touched: D3, D6.
D3's own rationale for the 30-day read-only grace period is "thirty days is
long enough for a finance approval to come through" — it depends on someone
noticing and acting. D6 stops all migration email "after the renewal date."
Renewal date is precisely when the grace period starts. So a team that missed
the pre-renewal warnings gets zero communication during the one window where
a nudge could still save the account, and no warning immediately before
suspension actually lands. Walk the scenario: admin ignores emails at -30/-14/
-3 days, renewal passes, team enters read-only, hears nothing for 30 days,
gets suspended without a final warning. A ruling here would very likely add at
least one message inside the grace window (e.g., day-1-of-read-only and a
pre-suspension reminder) — the record as frozen doesn't just under-communicate,
it actively contradicts D3's stated justification for why 30 days was chosen.
Resolution: either extend D6's cadence into the grace period, or drop the
"finance approval" rationale from D3 and justify the 30 days on other grounds.

**2. [Critical] "Suspension" is the goal line's third requirement and it's undefined.**
Decision touched: D3.
The goal line explicitly asks the record to cover "what happens to a team
that does not act." D3 says the end state is "suspension" but never defines
it: is data retained or deleted, for how long, is it exportable, can the team
reactivate later and recover their history, is there a hard-delete date. This
isn't a nice-to-have detail — it's the substantive answer to one of three
things this record was asked to settle, and as written it's a label without
a definition. A ruling on this would almost certainly need to happen before
ship (legal/compliance would ask the same question). Resolution: add a D3a
(or amend D3) specifying data fate and reactivation terms.

**3. [Critical] D8's central claim about Stripe Tax is an unverified, load-bearing external claim, and as commonly understood it overstates what the product does.**
Decision touched: D8.
D8 asserts Stripe Tax "registers us and files returns automatically in every
US state" and that the company therefore carries "no filing obligation of its
own and need[s] no tax counsel." I have no repo or fact-checker map to verify
this against, so I can't confirm or deny it from this workspace — but by
general knowledge of the product, Stripe Tax's strength is calculation and
collection; automated filing is a narrower, partner-mediated feature with
limited jurisdiction coverage, and registration decisions in a newly-crossed
nexus state typically still require action from the business, not just the
tool. If that's right, "no filing obligation of our own and need no tax
counsel" is the kind of sentence that reads fine in a decision record and
badly in an audit. Given 4,000 teams moving onto taxable card transactions
across potentially every US state, this is high blast-radius if wrong. It's
also scoped to "US" only — the record is silent on VAT/GST for any
non-US teams, which may or may not exist in the 4,000. Resolution: this
specific claim needs actual tax-counsel or Stripe-account-team confirmation
before D8 keeps a "Confident" tag; flag as blocking that confirmation, not as
"assumed fine."

**4. [Important] D2's price hold ends in an unaddressed cliff, worst for the accounts with the most to lose.**
Decision touched: D2.
Price continuity is named explicitly in the goal line, and D2 gives it for
exactly 12 months, then jumps straight to list price with no cap, glide path,
or segmentation. Teams most likely to have heavily discounted, negotiated
per-seat rates (the larger/older accounts) get the largest, least-cushioned
increase at month 12, with no carve-out discussed anywhere in the record.
"Price continuity" as delivered is a one-year deferral of the shock, not a
plan for the shock itself. A ruling here plausibly changes the outcome (e.g.,
a capped annual increase or graduated ramp for accounts above some discount
threshold) — this is the kind of decision that gets made once and is
expensive to revisit after 4,000 teams have already migrated.

**5. [Important] D5 only addresses seat additions; seat removals are unaddressed, and the billing-cadence relationship to D2 is ambiguous.**
Decision touched: D5, and its consistency with D2.
D5 explicitly handles seats "added mid-cycle" (billed next invoice, no
proration) but says nothing about seats removed — are they credited down at
next reconciliation, or does the team keep paying for headcount it no longer
has until the cycle turns over? The asymmetry is exactly the kind of thing
D5's own rationale tries to avoid ("reads as billing noise") — except now the
noise is a customer paying for seats it removed, not micro-charges. Separately,
D5's "monthly in arrears" cadence sits next to D2's 12-month price hold, which
implies an annual cadence — the record never states whether the base
subscription is now billed monthly, annually with monthly true-ups only, or
something else. Both should be resolved together since they touch the same
invoice.

**6. [Important] D4 is the foundation everything else stands on, and it's tagged Assumed with a one-word rationale.**
Decision touched: D4 (and by extension D5, D8, D9, all built on top of it).
"Obvious" is the entire rationale for choosing the platform that will run
subscription state, card capture, and dunning for 4,000 teams' real money —
no alternatives, no PCI/compliance discussion, no migration-effort note from
existing invoicing. This may well be the right call, but the record itself
flags it as unexamined (Confidence: Assumed), and it's the least reversible
decision in the set once teams start migrating. Worth a genuine pass before
it's load-bearing for three other decisions.

**7. [Minor] New angle at the D3 × D7 boundary: no self-serve downgrade creates an incentive to downgrade via non-payment.**
Decisions touched: D3, D7. (Not re-litigating D7 itself — its Contested
rationale already reasoned through the dark-pattern objection and set a
revisit threshold; this is the interaction, which is new.) A team that wants
a cheaper plan and doesn't want the support conversation D7 requires has a
standing alternative: let the card lapse at renewal, ride D3's read-only
window, and land in whatever "suspension" turns out to mean (see finding 2).
That's a worse outcome for both the team and Threadline than either decision
intends on its own, and it's specifically enabled by combining them. Worth a
one-line note in D7 or D3 acknowledging the interaction, even if the
resolution is "we accept that edge case."

**8. [Minor] D9's operator override has no stated access control or audit trail, and its build-effort bar looks inconsistent with D7's.**
Decision touched: D9. "Support will want it" (Assumed) greenlights building a
full internal console with an override that can force any team into any
billing state — with no mention of who can use the override, or whether
actions are logged. That's a real control gap for a tool that can waive
someone's bill. It also sits oddly against D7, where a customer-facing
convenience was deliberately deferred to control v1 build cost; here a
bespoke internal tool clears the bar on a much thinner justification.

**9. [Minor] "Open threads: none recorded" doesn't hold up against the record's own gaps.**
Several items above (D3's data-retention definition, D8's tax-counsel
sign-off, D2's price-cliff policy) are exactly the kind of unresolved
follow-up an "open threads" section exists to carry forward. An empty section
next to this many live loose ends is itself a fitness signal, not just a
formatting nit.

## Coverage note
Blind map items with no corresponding decision and judged non-material enough
to skip as standalone findings: rollback plan for a bad migration wave,
success metrics/instrumentation, PCI scope (reasonably covered implicitly by
Stripe), EU/SCA card handling. The international-tax silence is folded into
finding 3 rather than raised separately.

## Tally
9 findings: 3 Critical, 3 Important, 3 Minor.

## Recommended status: needs-revision
Nothing here suggests the shape of the plan is wrong — waves-by-renewal,
temporary price protection, and a grace-then-suspend path are all reasonable
structures. But two Confident decisions (D3, D6) actively contradict each
other in the record's own terms, the record's answer to its own third goal
(what happens if a team does nothing) stops short of the actual outcome, and
one Confident decision (D8) rests on an external claim this workspace can't
verify and which reads as overstated on general knowledge. I'd want 1, 2, and
3 resolved before this record ships; 4–6 resolved or explicitly deferred with
reasoning; 7–9 are cheap to close and worth a line each.

This is my recommendation, not a verdict — status and dispositions are yours
to set.
