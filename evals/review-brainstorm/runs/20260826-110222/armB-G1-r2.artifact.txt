# Phase 0 — Blind angle map

Topic only: Palisade (incident-management SaaS) — delivery of incident notifications to on-call responders. Goal line: delivery mechanism, escalation policy, what's owed to a customer when a page fails to land. Written from domain knowledge of paging systems (PagerDuty/Opsgenie-class products) before engaging with the record's specific choices.

## Delivery mechanism
- Channel menu: push, SMS, voice call, email, chat-ops (Slack/Teams) — and whether channels fire simultaneously or in sequence.
- **Do Not Disturb / silent-mode bypass.** This is the load-bearing requirement for any paging product: a normal push or SMS produces no sound on a silenced phone. Real systems need iOS Critical Alerts entitlement, Android full-screen intents, or a phone call (which rings through common DND configs) to actually wake someone.
- Which platform-specific entitlements/certs are needed, and whether "one integration" claims are actually true given iOS push is APNs-based under the hood even when routed through a third party.
- Delivery-confirmation semantics: sent vs delivered vs acknowledged differ meaningfully by channel; a channel's "guarantee" claims should be scrutinized.
- Single-vendor dependency risk (one push provider outage = every page silently fails).
- Cost model per channel and interaction with plan tiers.
- Payload content/privacy on lock screens.
- International/roaming SMS delivery reliability.

## Escalation policy
- Timer values and how they compose across hops (does a per-responder retry clock interact with the rotation-advance clock, or do they race each other).
- Rotation depth and terminal state: what happens when the last hop (often "page the whole team" or repeat-loop) also gets no ack — an escalation chain needs a defined floor, not just a defined ceiling.
- Severity-based timer variation (a SEV1 vs SEV4 probably shouldn't share the same escalation cadence).
- Manual overrides, snoozes, schedule exceptions (PTO/on-call swaps).
- Whether escalating to the next person cancels or coexists with the original responder's still-pending retry.

## What's owed to the customer on a failed page
- Is "failed to land" defined by delivery confirmation, or only inferred by no-ack — these are different claims to make to a customer.
- Customer-facing commitment: SLA credit, proactive notification, or just an internal audit trail available on request. Internal audit ≠ owed remedy.
- Evidence retention window long enough to cover the realistic delay before a customer notices and files a ticket (which is often not same-day).
- Real-time failure detection vs after-the-fact batch reconciliation — the two answer different questions ("did this page fail right now, while the incident is still live" vs "did pages fail last night, on average").
- Whether the audit/reconciliation mechanism duplicates a simpler event-driven design (webhook-fed status table) rather than justifying a whole parallel service.

This map is compared against the record in the cold read; gaps become coverage findings, not assumptions the record must have missed.

To the lead — cold end-stage review of the Palisade incident-notification record (solo pass, no counterpart, no cross-examination round run; no fact-checker map existed for me to check claims against, and this workspace has no repo/source to ground anything in — several findings below are therefore reported as *unverified external claims*, not confirmed errors).

Blind angle map produced before I engaged with the record's specifics is in `blind-angle-map.md` alongside this file. Findings below are the diff between that map and the frozen record, plus the standard hunt classes, ranked by whether a ruling would likely / plausibly / not change something.

## Critical

**1. D4's technical justification is very likely wrong, and it's load-bearing for D1 and D2 as well as itself.**
D4 claims FCM "guarantees delivery inside five seconds" and wakes a backgrounded iOS app "without us holding a separate APNs entitlement." Neither matches how FCM actually works, to the best of my domain knowledge (I could not run this against a fact-checker map or a search tool in this session, so treat it as a strong "please verify" rather than a confirmed defect): FCM has no formal delivery-time SLA, it's best-effort with priority hints; and FCM's iOS path still runs over APNs under the hood — Firebase needs an APNs auth key/cert registered regardless. More importantly, the claim conflates ordinary push delivery with *Critical Alerts*, the distinct, separately-approved iOS entitlement required to sound/vibrate through Do Not Disturb and silent mode. Nothing in D1–D8 mentions DND/silent-mode bypass at all, and for a paging product that's usually the whole point of the channel choice — an unacknowledged normal push on a silenced phone at 2am is indistinguishable from no page at all.
Failure scenario: on-call responder has phone on silent overnight (common, not edge-case); ordinary FCM push arrives silently; the "5-minute window before SMS fallback" in D2 fires for a large fraction of real pages, not just the "phone-off/force-quit" cases D2 was scoped for — which also quietly invalidates part of D1's cost model (SMS volume assumed rare, D1's rationale explicitly leans on that).
Would a ruling likely change something: yes — either D4 needs the Critical Alerts entitlement path (real added iOS work, App Store review implications) explicitly decided on, or the team accepts silent pages as a known gap and that should be a stated decision, not an implicit one riding on an incorrect technical premise.

**2. The goal line asks what Palisade owes the customer when a page fails to land, and no decision answers it.**
D3 (30-day status retention) and D8 (nightly reconciliation) are internal detection/audit machinery. Neither is a customer-facing commitment — no SLA, no credit, no proactive notification, no support-response commitment is decided anywhere. "We can look up what happened if asked" is not the same claim as "here is what we owe you if it happened." This is a direct gap against the session's own stated goal line, not an incidental omission — worth confirming whether it was discussed and simply not written down, or genuinely never addressed.

## Important

**3. D2 (5-min SMS retry to the same responder) and D6 (3-min escalation to the next responder) aren't reconciled with each other.**
At minute 3 the rotation moves on to the next responder (D6). At minute 5 the *original, already-bypassed* responder still gets an SMS retry for the same page (D2). Neither decision's rationale acknowledges the other's clock. Net effect: two people can be independently acting on the same page two minutes apart, and SMS spend (the very cost D1 is protecting) fires in a case D1/D2 didn't model — every unacknowledged page that escalates on schedule generates an SMS a rotation has already moved past.

**4. D8's nightly cadence answers a different question than the one the goal line needs answered, and may be more machinery than the job requires.**
Reconciliation runs long after D6's escalation chain (3–9 min) has already exhausted for any given incident — it's necessarily retrospective, not a live-incident signal. If the intent is "know whether a page actually failed while the incident is still live," nightly batch reconciliation against D3 doesn't do that. Cheaper shape, if the goal is just a second source of truth on delivery state: have the provider webhooks write into the same D3 status rows as they arrive (event-driven), rather than standing up a separate independent service that re-derives state and diverges nightly — that removes both the latency problem and a second system to keep in sync with the first.

**5. D3's 30-day retention is self-flagged weak ("Assumed," rationale is literally "sounds about right") and that weakness now has a specific consequence.**
Given finding #2, if any customer commitment ever gets attached to delivery evidence, 30 days needs to be long enough to cover the realistic gap between "page failed" and "customer complains" — which for a support ticket is often not same-week, let alone same-day. Worth revisiting together with #2 rather than in isolation.

## Minor

**6. Voice/phone call as a channel is never considered or explicitly rejected.** It's the classic mechanism that rings through silent/DND on many devices, which ties directly to finding #1. Not necessarily wrong to exclude — D1's cost logic would likely apply even harder to voice — but there's no rationale on record, so it reads as unconsidered rather than ruled out.

**7. D6's escalation chain has no defined terminal state, and that's a real open thread the record logs as none.** After the incident-commander hop also goes unacknowledged, nothing further is specified. "Open threads: None recorded" doesn't hold up next to this.

## Tally
7 survivors: 2 Critical, 3 Important, 2 Minor.
