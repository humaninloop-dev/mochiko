=== review.md ===
Reviewed the frozen record solo, no cross-examination on this pass, so treat this as one skeptical read rather than a consensus. Six findings, ordered by how much they worry me. First two I'd block on before calling this frozen; the rest are things to fix before build starts.

1. The record doesn't answer the question it was chartered to answer.
   The goal line names three things: delivery mechanism, escalation policy, and what we owe the customer when a page fails to land. D1–D8 cover the first two in real depth. The third is just... absent. D3 and D8 talk about how *we* detect that a page failed internally, but nothing says what happens for the customer when it does — no SLA language, no credit/remedy, no commitment about notifying the customer, not even a definition of "failed to land" from their side (never delivered? delivered but never acked? escalated all the way to IC?). "Open threads: None recorded" is itself the tell — this should have been logged as an open thread, not silently dropped. As it stands the record can't be called done against its own charter.

2. D2 and D6 fight each other on the clock, and nobody reconciled it.
   D6: unacked at 3 min → escalate to next responder; unacked again at 3 min → page the incident commander (~6 min mark). D2: unacked at 5 min → SMS the *same* responder again. Run the numbers together: by the 5-minute mark, the original responder has already been escalated past (at minute 3), and possibly the IC is already involved (minute 6 is close behind). So the SMS fallback — whose entire rationale is "catch the phone-off/force-quit case" — lands on someone who's no longer the active responder for that incident. Either the SMS target should track the *currently assigned* responder rather than the original one, or the fallback needs to be cancelled/reassigned on escalation, or the two timers need to be brought into one state machine. Right now the record specifies both mechanisms as if they run independently, and the arithmetic says they can't.

3. D4's "Confident" rating leans on two specific technical claims I can't square with what I know of FCM, and nothing in the record lets me check them further.
   "FCM guarantees delivery inside five seconds" — FCM's high-priority tier is best-effort, not a hard guarantee; it's still subject to OS-level throttling (Doze, Low Power Mode, background limits). And "wake a backgrounded iOS app without us holding a separate APNs entitlement" — my understanding is FCM's iOS path is a proxy in front of APNs, so you still need an APNs auth key/cert uploaded to Firebase and the push-notification entitlement on the app; "one integration, no APNs" doesn't match how that pipeline works. I have no source access this round and no fact-checker map to confirm either way, so I'm flagging both as claims to verify, not confirmed errors — but D1's whole cost argument and D2's "five minutes is generous" framing both inherit the risk if push turns out less reliable than stated.

4. D8 is a real engineering investment anchored to a retention window chosen by gut feel.
   D3's own confidence is "Assumed" and its rationale is literally "sounds about right." D8 — a new in-house service ingesting provider webhooks and reconciling nightly — is defined against D3's 30-day rows. Nobody checked whether 30 days covers the window in which a customer might actually dispute a missed page (a postmortem or contract escalation surfacing a month-plus later would already be past the retention window by the time D8's own audit trail is needed as evidence). Worth revisiting D3 with an actual owner before D8 gets built on top of it.

5. D8's cadence doesn't match the product's own urgency claim.
   The thing D8 is auditing pages a human within minutes; the audit that catches it failing runs once a night. A delivery failure could sit undetected for up to ~24 hours in a product whose core pitch is sub-minute urgency. If that gap is intentional (e.g., near-real-time reconciliation was considered and rejected for cost/complexity), say so in the rationale — right now it reads like nightly was the default nobody stress-tested against the failure case D8 exists to catch.

6. Minor, not a defect: D5 strips the description from the push payload, so acting on a page (as opposed to just acknowledging it) requires opening the app and a network round trip. That's probably fine since D2/D6 key off *ack*, not resolution, but worth a sentence in the rationale acknowledging the tradeoff so it doesn't look like an oversight later.

Everything else — the cost argument in D1, "on-call leads asked for three minutes" in D6, the contested call in D7 — I'm taking as stated; those rest on inputs outside the record and I have no way to check them from here, which is fine, that's just what an assumption looks like from a cold read.

Net: I'd unfreeze for one more pass. Priority order — settle #1 (it's the actual gap in scope), fix the #2 timer conflict (it's derivable from the record's own numbers, not a judgment call), then get someone who owns the FCM integration to confirm or correct #3 before D4 keeps its "Confident" label.
