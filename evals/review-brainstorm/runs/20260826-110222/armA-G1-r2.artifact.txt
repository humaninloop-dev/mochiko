# Phase 0 — Blind angle map

Built from the topic and goal line only, before opening record.md:

Topic: Palisade (incident-management SaaS) — how we deliver incident notifications to on-call responders.
Goal line: delivery mechanism + escalation policy + what we owe a customer when a page fails to land.

No repo/source files exist in this workspace, so this map is domain reasoning only (on-call/paging-product
domain — the space PagerDuty/Opsgenie/Splunk On-Call occupy), not grounded in any local artifact.

## Angles a thorough treatment of this topic should visit

**Channels & their failure physics**
- Push (mobile app), SMS, voice/phone call (robocall + keypress ack), email, Slack/Teams/chat-ops, desktop/browser, wearables.
- Per-channel failure modes: OS-level silencing (iOS Focus/Do Not Disturb, Android Doze/battery optimization),
  app force-quit, phone off/no signal, uninstalled app / stale push token, SMS carrier filtering, voice call declined
  vs. unanswered.
- Mechanisms that exist specifically to defeat silencing (e.g. Apple's Critical Alerts entitlement, Android
  high-priority/full-screen-intent notifications) — paging products typically treat this as core, not optional.

**Escalation policy**
- Ack timeout, chain depth, sequential vs. simultaneous multi-notify, severity-tiered timing (Sev1 vs Sev5),
  business-hours/on-call-schedule overrides, what happens when the chain is fully exhausted (dead-end handling),
  concurrent-incident contention for the same responder.

**Delivery guarantees / observability**
- What "delivered" vs "acknowledged" means per channel, provider-reported status vs. independently verified status,
  retention of that status and why, who consumes it (support, customer-facing SLA reporting, internal reliability
  metrics).

**Customer commitment when paging fails**
- SLA definition for page delivery, credits/remedies, proactive customer notification when a failure is detected,
  the difference between "we detected a failure after the fact" and "we did something about this failure in time
  to matter," support/escalation path for a customer report.

**Cost & vendor**
- Per-channel unit economics, cost pass-through by plan tier, vendor concentration risk (single push provider,
  single SMS provider), build-vs-buy for any status/audit tooling.

**Content & privacy**
- What's safe to put in a lock-screen-visible payload, multi-tenant data isolation, compliance posture (SOC2/GDPR)
  for notification content and delivery logs.

**Testing & self-verification**
- Can a responder test their own reachability ("send me a test page")? Is the paging pipeline itself monitored
  (who pages the team if paging infrastructure is down)?

**Actors**
- On-call responder, incident commander, team/schedule admin configuring policy, Palisade support, the customer's
  own downstream stakeholders who care that the incident got handled.

**Timescale / rollout**
- v1 scope vs. deferred work, and what data/signal triggers revisiting deferred scope.

This map is intentionally broad; materiality of any gap is judged after diffing against the frozen record.

Recommended status: **critical-gaps**

## Critical

**1. The record never decides what Palisade owes a customer when a page fails to land — the third named pillar of the goal line is simply absent, and "Open threads: None recorded" hides that.**
Decisions: goal-line pillar 3 (unowned); adjacent to D8.
Failure scenario: a page exhausts the full escalation chain (D6: two hops, then the incident commander) and the
IC also never acks. Today, nothing in the record fires until D8's *nightly* reconciliation batch runs — no
real-time customer notification, no SLA credit, no support-ticket trigger, no stated remedy at all. For a
product whose entire value proposition is "someone will know," a fully-silent failure with no next-day-or-later
response is a product-trust gap, not a footnote.
Resolution path: add a decision (or send this back to the session) stating what triggers when the chain is
exhausted unacked, and what obligation — proactive notice, credit, escalation to a human on Palisade's side —
attaches. "None recorded" should not stand as the open-threads line while this is unaddressed.

**2. D2's 5-minute SMS fallback and D6's 3-minute/two-hop escalation are two independent timers on what reads as the same non-ack event, with no stated reconciliation.**
Decisions: D2, D6.
Failure scenario: responder A is paged. At 3 minutes unacked, D6 escalates the push to responder B. At 5 minutes
— measured from what event, and against whom? — D2 fires SMS to "the same responder." Same as which one: A
(who has already been escalated past) or whoever currently holds the page? If it's A, the org is paying for an
SMS to someone the policy has already moved on from. If it's "whoever currently holds it," D2 needs to say so,
because as written it names a fixed responder, not a rotation position. Either reading is defensible, but the
record doesn't say which, and the two clocks were evidently designed independently (D2's rationale never
mentions D6's hop schedule or vice versa) for the two decisions the goal line explicitly asked this record to
reconcile.
Resolution path: one sentence stating whether the 5-minute SMS timer runs against the original page or the
currently-escalated hop, and whether it fires in parallel with or in place of a hop.

**3. No channel or mechanism addresses OS-level silencing (iOS Focus/Do Not Disturb, Android Doze/silent mode) — the record only ever weighs delivery vs. acknowledgement, never delivery vs. audible/actionable.**
Decisions: D4, D5, D1.
Failure scenario: a responder's phone is asleep on a nightstand with Focus mode on. FCM delivers the push
successfully (satisfying D4's delivery claim) but it never rings, vibrates, or surfaces — the phone reports
"delivered," Palisade's status table says "delivered," and the human never sees it until the 5-minute SMS
fallback (assuming #2 above resolves in the responder's favor). This is close to the median failure mode paging
products are built to defeat — it's why Apple's Critical Alerts entitlement exists and why competitors treat it
as core. The record's confidence in D4 ("Confident") is confidence in a claim (delivery guarantee) that doesn't
address the actual risk (audibility).
Resolution path: a decision on whether Palisade requests/uses a DND-bypass mechanism for push, and if not,
whether that's an accepted gap or pushes more weight onto the SMS/escalation fallback timing (which #2 shows is
already underspecified).

## Important

**4. D4's technical rationale is very likely factually wrong on two points, undermining a "Confident" decision.**
Decision: D4.
Detail: (a) FCM does not "guarantee" delivery inside five seconds — Google's own documentation describes FCM as
best-effort, with no delivery-time SLA, and delivery can be delayed or dropped by Android Doze/battery
optimization or lack of connectivity; "guarantee" overstates it. (b) Sending push through FCM to iOS still
requires an APNs authentication key/certificate registered in the Firebase project — FCM brokers to APNs for
iOS, it doesn't bypass it. The claim that one integration avoids "holding a separate APNs entitlement" appears
to describe how FCM does not actually work for iOS delivery.
Failure scenario: if (b) is wrong, the "one integration covers both platforms" framing that justifies choosing
FCM alone is wrong — there's a second credential (the APNs key) to provision, store, and rotate regardless, and
that operational cost was never weighed because the rationale assumed it away.
Resolution path: have whoever specced D4 confirm against Firebase's current iOS setup docs whether an APNs key
is required in the Firebase console; if it is, D4's rationale needs to drop the "no separate entitlement" claim
and its confidence should be revisited.

**5. Voice/phone-call escalation was never considered or rejected — it's simply absent, and it's the channel this class of product usually treats as most reliable for waking someone.**
Decisions: D1, D2, D6 (coverage gap, not a re-litigation of any Contested decision).
Materiality: this record chose between exactly two channels (push, SMS) for the entire delivery + escalation
policy. A robocall with keypress acknowledgement bypasses silent-mode/DND more reliably than push (ringtone
volume vs. notification banner) and doesn't depend on the app being installed or a token being valid. Given
finding #3, a plausible different ruling exists: escalate to voice at the same point SMS currently fires, or use
it in place of SMS for the "definitely wake this person up" case. The record shows no evidence this alternative
was weighed and set aside — it's a genuine coverage gap.
Resolution path: one line noting whether voice was considered and why it lost, or add it to the fallback
sequence.

**6. D1 rejects SMS/any-non-push-primary purely on unit cost; it never weighs reliability-of-primary-channel against a paging product's core promise, and never considers cost pass-through as an alternative to elimination.**
Decision: D1.
Steelman: if push can silently fail to surface (finding #3) while SMS/voice more reliably interrupt a
distracted or asleep human, then for a product sold on "we make sure someone knows," optimizing the *primary*
channel for infra cost rather than wake-reliability is an inversion of what the customer is paying for. The
rationale as written ("margin on the Team plan cannot absorb it") treats this as fixed, but raising the Team
plan price or gating channel choice by plan tier is a live alternative that was never named, let alone rejected.
Resolution path: confirm whether reliability-vs-cost for the primary channel was actually discussed and just not
recorded, or wasn't discussed — if the latter, this decision was adopted on cost alone without the tradeoff the
rest of the record's own concerns (D5's carefulness about lock-screen exposure, D8's appetite for a second
source of truth) suggest the team does care about.

**7. D3's retention window carries the record's own "Assumed" mark with a rationale that is pure passive acceptance ("Sounds about right"), and never weighs compliance/SLA-dispute retention needs or its own dependency from D8.**
Decisions: D3, D8.
Failure scenario: a customer disputes a missed page 45 days after the fact (e.g., during a delayed postmortem or
a billing/SLA argument) — the underlying per-notification status row is already gone, and D8's nightly
reconciliation, if it also doesn't retain divergence records past 30 days, has nothing to reconcile against for
that window either.
Resolution path: state the actual driver for 30 days (support-ticket handling time is asserted but not
measured) and check it against any SLA-dispute or compliance retention window the product intends to offer.

## Minor

**8. D8 is adopted on a thin, generic rationale ("we want a second source of truth") with no build-vs-buy comparison, and its own output — "alerting on divergence" — never connects to any owner, action, or the still-open customer-remedy question (finding #1).**
Decision: D8.
This is real machinery (webhook ingestion, independent state re-derivation, nightly batch reconciliation) being
justified without weighing an existing observability/webhook-relay vendor as a cheaper shape, and without
answering "alerts whom, to do what" — as written it risks being built and then not closing the loop it seems to
exist for.
Resolution path: name who consumes the divergence alert and what they do with it, and note whether an off-the-
shelf option was considered and rejected for a stated reason.

**9. "Open threads: None recorded" is itself a fitness problem given findings 1–3 above** — the record asserts
completeness it doesn't have, which matters for a document meant to stand alone for whoever picks it up next.
Resolution path: this should self-resolve once 1–3 land as actual open threads or decisions.
