=== review.md ===
Reviewing the frozen sync decision record solo, cold, no repo and no claim map to lean on — so what follows is internal-consistency and domain-knowledge checking, not fact-checking against source. Flagging that scope limit up front so you know what kind of confidence to put on this.

Bottom line: I would not call this converged. One required piece of the goal line isn't in the record at all, and there's a high-risk interaction between two "Confident" decisions that nobody seems to have run through together.

**1. The record doesn't cover what it was asked to cover.**
The goal line names three things: sync model, conflict handling, and what an inspector sees when a sync fails. D1–D9 give you the first two, but there is no decision anywhere about inspector-facing failure state — no queued/retrying/failed indicator, no error surfaced on-device, nothing. D9 gives ops a per-device telemetry dashboard, which is a different audience entirely. "Open threads: None recorded" is itself the tell — this should have shown up there even if nobody had an answer yet. Right now the record is silently short a third of its own scope.

**2. D1 + D7 stacked together is the sharpest risk in here, and it isn't named as one.**
D1 resolves conflicts by device-clock timestamp. D7 ships no merge UI and drops the losing version with no trace. Individually each is defensible under "single-author in practice." Together: the resolution key is a value users can trivially have wrong (unsynced clock, wrong timezone, a device that got its clock manually reset) or wrong through no fault of the inspector, and the outcome of getting it wrong is a full inspection silently deleted with nothing recoverable and nothing shown to the person who lost it. D7's own rationale acknowledges this was raised and overridden — but the override rests entirely on "conflicts are rare," not on the mechanism being safe when a conflict does happen. Worth a second look specifically at "clock" as the tiebreaker, independent of how rare you believe conflicts are.

**3. D7's own revisit condition points at something that doesn't exist yet.**
D7 says "we revisit if the conflict counter says otherwise." There's no decision anywhere establishing a conflict counter. D9's telemetry is queue-state transitions, not conflict events specifically. As written, the condition for revisiting a contested, data-destructive decision has no instrument behind it — so in practice it never gets revisited.

**4. D3 + D6 leaves supervisors signing off on possibly-incomplete inspections without knowing it.**
D3 marks an inspection `synced` on JSON arrival — photos ride a separate queue and can lag well behind. D6 puts the inspection in front of the supervisor for sign-off the moment it shows `synced`. Nothing in the record distinguishes "fully synced, photos included" from "data's here, photos still in flight or stalled." If photo evidence matters to a sign-off (and on a field-inspection app, it usually does), a supervisor can currently approve based on incomplete evidence with no visible signal that anything's missing.

**5. D2's battery rationale and D9 point in opposite directions.**
D2 batches uploads on a 60s timer specifically because keeping the radio hot drained batteries on the pilot. D9 then asks for every queue-state transition streamed to the analytics warehouse in real time. That's the same always-on radio behavior D2 was written to avoid, just moved to a different payload. Nothing in the record reconciles this — D9 reads like it was decided independently of D2's constraint ("Assumed" confidence, rationale is just "we will want the data").

**6. D8's stated guarantee is broader than what backs it.**
The rationale correctly cites that WAL gives SQLite crash-safety against a mid-transaction kill. The decision then concludes "an interrupted sync can never leave a half-written inspection on the device." Sync here is a multi-step, multi-store workflow (DB write, status flag, a separate photo queue per D3) — WAL durability on the SQLite file doesn't by itself make that whole workflow atomic. This is tagged "Confident"; the underlying citation supports a narrower claim than the one written down.

**7. D1 and D7 both lean on one unverified premise, doubling the exposure if it's wrong.**
"Inspections are single-author in practice" is asserted as settled fact in both rationales, with nothing behind it in the record. Fine if true — but it's currently load-bearing for two separate decisions at once, one of which (D7) is explicitly the contested one. If that premise turns out to be optimistic, both fall together, not independently.

What I'd want back before calling this closed: an actual decision (even a placeholder) for inspector-visible sync failure state, and a real look at #2/#4 as a pair rather than as isolated decisions — those are the two places where the record's own logic, not an outside fact, is the problem.
