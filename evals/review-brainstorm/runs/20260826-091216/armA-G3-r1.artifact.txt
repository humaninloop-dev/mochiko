# Phase 0 — Blind Angle Map

Built from the topic statement and goal line only, before opening `record.md`. No repo exists in this workspace, so no repo-grounding reading was available or applicable.

Topic: Threadline (team chat product) — moving 4,000 teams from invoiced annual contracts onto self-serve card billing.
Goal line: migration path, price continuity, what happens to a team that does not act.

## Actors
- Team admin / billing contact (may have turned over since contract signing)
- Non-admin end users on affected teams (impact of read-only/suspension)
- Finance/procurement departments at customer orgs, incl. PO-only / card-incapable orgs (government, universities, some enterprises)
- Sales / account managers holding existing relationships or multi-year custom deals
- Support team (volume, tooling, escalation path)
- Internal finance/revenue ops (cash-flow timing shift, revenue recognition)
- Legal/compliance (contract amendment terms, tax, data protection)
- Payments/engineering team (PCI scope, integration risk)
- Executive sponsor (churn and revenue-risk exposure)

## Cost dimensions
- Card processing fees vs. invoice/ACH — who absorbs the margin change
- Support cost of a 4,000-team migration wave
- Churn/revenue loss from teams that don't or can't convert
- Build cost of migration console and Stripe integration
- Dunning/retry cost for failed cards
- FX and non-USD pricing if any teams are international

## Failure modes
- No card on file at renewal (explicitly a goal-line topic)
- Card present but declines/expires after being added
- Org structurally cannot pay by card (procurement/PO mandate)
- Admin contact is stale or departed — notice never reaches a human who can act
- Contract terms require formal notice/mutual agreement to change payment method, not just email
- Multi-year or non-standard-term contracts complicating "next renewal"
- Seat-count disputes during reconciliation
- Data handling during read-only/suspended state (retention, export, deletion, GDPR)
- Renewal-date clustering defeating any "spread the load" assumption
- Custom/negotiated per-seat pricing diverging sharply from list price

## Timescales
- Pre-renewal notice window
- Grace/read-only period after a missed renewal
- Price-hold period post-migration and what happens at its expiry
- Long-run reactivation path after suspension

## Alternatives / rejected roads to steelman if visited
- Single global cutover vs. wave-by-renewal
- Immediate price move to list vs. held rate
- Immediate suspension vs. grace period
- Build in-house billing vs. buy (Stripe or other)
- Self-serve downgrade vs. support-mediated

## Second-order effects
- Sales comp impact if AEs are paid on invoiced annual deals
- Revenue recognition / cash flow timing shift for finance
- Tax exposure across US states and any non-US jurisdictions
- Internal-tool abuse surface if an operator override exists
- Fairness/consistency across teams migrating at different calendar times (price drift, list-price changes over the rollout year)

## Open questions a thorough treatment should answer
- Is any segment of the 4,000 teams categorically unable to use card billing, and if so what happens to them?
- Does "current rate" for price continuity include negotiated discounts, and what happens to those specifically at expiry?
- What is the customer base's geographic footprint (US-only vs. international) — this gates tax and currency angles?
- What communication, if any, happens *during* a grace/suspension window, not just before it?
- What data-handling guarantees exist for a suspended team's content?

# Message to lead — solo end-stage review of record.md (Threadline self-serve billing migration)

Spawned solo, no counterpart, no cross-examination round. Findings below are self-filtered against the materiality bar the debate would normally apply, but they are undebated — hold them to that standard when you read them.

Blind angle map was built first (see `blind-angle-map.md`) from the topic and goal line alone; the coverage findings below are the diff between that map and what the record actually decided.

Tally: ~20 candidate angles/hunt-class items raised internally during the cold read; **9 survived** my own materiality filter. The rest died at the diff (no plausible ruling change) or were rejected-roads already visited by the record (D7, Contested, correctly not re-litigated).

## Critical

**1. D3 has no path for teams that are structurally card-incapable, not just slow.**
D3's 30-day-read-only-then-suspend assumes every team can eventually add a card. Government agencies, universities, and PO-only enterprises are common annual-invoice customers and often cannot pay by card at all, by policy or statute — for them the "grace period" isn't a grace period, it's a guaranteed termination clock regardless of ability or willingness to pay.
- Failure scenario: a state-university team in good standing for 3 years hits renewal, has no card in 30 days (can never legally add one), and is suspended purely for a payment-mechanism mismatch, not non-payment.
- Resolution path: does Threadline currently have any teams under PO-only/procurement payment terms? If yes, this decision needs an exemption or manual-invoicing carve-out before it ships; if no, the record should say so explicitly rather than leaving it silent.

**2. D8's central factual claim ("Stripe Tax carries the whole US filing obligation... we need no tax counsel") looks overstated and is rated Confident with essentially no scrutiny.**
Stripe Tax's base product calculates and collects tax; automatic multi-state registration and return filing is a separate, opt-in capability, and merchant tax liability for registration/filing typically isn't fully absorbed by default configuration. This is a load-bearing compliance claim behind a Confident rating, on a decision that explicitly disclaims needing legal/tax review — for 4,000 teams' worth of revenue that's real exposure if wrong. It also only addresses US tax; nothing in the record covers non-US VAT/GST if any of the 4,000 teams are outside the US.
- Resolution path: get written confirmation from whoever validated this (Stripe rep or tax counsel) that the filing/remittance SKU — not just calculation — is enabled and covers every state where Threadline has nexus; separately confirm whether any customers are non-US and need their own tax decision.

## Important

**3. Inconsistency between D3 and D6: zero communication during the very grace window D3 relies on.**
D6 stops all email after the renewal date. D3's rationale for the 30-day window is "long enough for a finance approval to come through" — but if a team never gets a "you're in read-only, N days left" nudge during that window (e.g., they missed or spam-filtered the pre-renewal emails), the grace period's usefulness is undermined by the record's own no-contact-after-renewal rule.
- Resolution path: clarify whether "nothing after" in D6 was meant to include the grace window, or whether a grace-period reminder should be carved out.

**4. D5 states the seat-addition billing path but never states the seat-removal path.**
"Seats added mid-cycle are charged on the following invoice" is explicit; whether removed seats generate any credit, and on what cadence, is unaddressed. For 4,000 self-serve teams with ongoing headcount churn, an unstated removal policy is a predictable dispute generator.
- Resolution path: state explicitly whether/when seat removals are credited.

**5. D2's 12-month price hold assumes "current rate" is a mild variant of list price; for negotiated enterprise discounts it may not be.**
Many annual invoiced contracts carry custom volume/loyalty discounts. For those teams, month 13 isn't a normal price increase — it's the loss of a specific negotiated discount, potentially a much larger jump than typical, which undercuts D2's own stated rationale of avoiding "two shocks landing together."
- Resolution path: check the distribution of current-rate-vs-list-price delta across the 4,000 teams; if a meaningful cohort has deep discounts, D2 likely needs a ramp rather than a cliff for that cohort.

**6. D9's operator override has no stated audit or access control.**
An internal tool that lets an operator "force any team into any billing state" is a real financial-control surface (can grant free service, reverse a suspension, alter what a team is billed) with nothing said about logging, role-gating, or approval.
- Resolution path: confirm whether audit logging + role restriction is already an assumed build requirement, or needs to be added to D9's statement explicitly.

**7. Coverage gap: no decision addresses data retention/export/deletion for a team in read-only or suspended state.**
For a chat product, "suspended" implies a real question about what happens to message history — retained, exportable, eventually deleted — with GDPR-shaped stakes if any customers are in the EU/UK. The record is silent on this entirely.
- Resolution path: confirm whether this is covered by a separate, pre-existing data-retention policy, or is genuinely undecided and needs a D-entry before ship.

**8. Coverage gap: non-US tax obligation is entirely unaddressed.**
D8 explicitly scopes to "US filing obligation." Nothing states whether any of the 4,000 teams are outside the US, or what happens to VAT/GST compliance if so. This compounds finding #2.
- Resolution path: confirm the customer base's geographic footprint; if non-US teams exist, this needs its own decision rather than silent inheritance from D8.

## Minor

**9. D1's "spreads support load across the year" assumes roughly uniform renewal-date distribution across the 4,000 teams.**
Enterprise annual contracts often cluster around fiscal-year boundaries (e.g., calendar-year-end, April). If the actual distribution is clustered, the load-spreading benefit the decision leans on may be much weaker than stated.
- Resolution path: check the actual renewal-date histogram before treating "spreads across the year" as a settled mitigation.

## Not raised

D7 (self-serve downgrade) is Contested and was already ruled with the steelman in view — I did not find a genuinely new angle against it, so it's excluded per protocol. D4 (Stripe Billing, "Obvious") and D9's overall build-vs-buy framing are thin-rationale but I judged the underlying calls sound enough not to block on; flagging here only for visibility, not as a survivor.

## Recommended status: **critical-gaps**

Basis: a load-bearing claim that looks broken on inspection (D8, finding #2), and a Critical coverage gap where a plausible in-scope decision (an exemption path for card-incapable orgs) would likely have been reached had the angle been visited (finding #1). The rest are Important/Minor and would normally not block alone, but the two Criticals do.
