No repo, no source files, and no fact-checker claim map were available to me, so everything below is either an internal-consistency check against the record's own text or a domain-knowledge check I can stand behind without needing your codebase. I'm flagging five things, ordered by how much I think they matter before this ships as final.

**1. The goal line has three legs; the record only delivers two.**
You framed the session as covering "the delivery mechanism, the escalation policy, and what we owe a customer when a page fails to land." D1/D2/D4/D5 cover delivery, D2/D6 cover escalation. Nothing in D1–D8 states an actual customer-facing commitment for a failed page — no SLA, no notification duty to the customer, no remedy, no credit, nothing. D3 (30-day status retention) and D8 (nightly internal reconciliation) are both about Palisade's internal ability to *detect and investigate* a failure after the fact — neither one is a promise to the customer about what happens when a page doesn't land. "Open threads: None recorded" reads as if the record is complete, but this third leg of the stated goal is just absent, not resolved. I'd want this called out explicitly as an open thread rather than implied to be covered by D3/D8, unless there's a decision I'm not seeing.

**2. D4's central technical claim looks wrong, and D2/D6's timing leans on it.**
D4 states, at Confident, that "FCM guarantees delivery inside five seconds." That's not how FCM works — Google documents FCM (and the APNs path it wraps for iOS) as best-effort, with no delivery-time guarantee, and high-priority messages remain subject to OS-level throttling on both platforms. The record then uses that claim to justify the five-minute SMS fallback in D2 as "generous against that guarantee." If the guarantee doesn't exist, that justification doesn't hold, and it's worth asking whether five minutes was chosen for a real reason independent of this claim. Separately, "without us holding a separate APNs entitlement" is misleading: the iOS push capability/entitlement is an Apple app-level requirement regardless of whether delivery goes through raw APNs or FCM — FCM removes the burden of running your own server-to-APNs integration, it doesn't remove the entitlement. Worth fixing the rationale even if the underlying channel choice (FCM for both platforms) stands on its own merits.

**3. D2 and D6 don't say who's holding the pager once escalation has already moved on.**
D6 escalates an unacknowledged push to the next responder at three minutes. D2 fires an SMS fallback "to the same responder" at five minutes. By minute five, the rotation has already moved past the original responder per D6. Is the SMS fallback (a) sent to the original responder regardless of the escalation state, (b) sent to whoever currently holds the page, or (c) canceled once escalation has advanced? The record doesn't say, and this isn't a nitpick — it decides whether a responder who already got escalated away from gets a confusing SMS for an incident that's no longer theirs. This is exactly the kind of thing "Open threads" exists for.

**4. D1's cost rationale is a specific, confident claim with nothing behind it.**
"SMS would become the third-largest line item in infrastructure spend" is stated at Confident with no figure, source, or even an order-of-magnitude estimate attached. I can't check it against anything in this workspace, so I'm not calling it wrong — but a claim that specific, carrying a Confident tag, usually has a number behind it somewhere. If it does, it'd strengthen the record to reference it; if it's a gut estimate, Confident may be overstating it.

**5. D7's "revisit" has no owner or trigger, and it's the one decision you tagged Contested.**
"We revisit once we hold a quarter of real paging data" is a real commitment dressed as a rationale — there's no date, no owner, and no defined threshold for what "a quarter of real paging data" means (calendar quarter? volume?). Given it's the only Contested item in the record, I'd expect it to show up in Open Threads with an actual trigger, not just live inside the rationale prose where it's easy to lose track of.

Net: D3, D5, D6's core mechanics, and the channel choice in D1/D4 (setting aside the rationale text) read as reasonable and internally coherent. The two things I'd block on before calling this truly frozen are #1 (the record doesn't actually answer one-third of what it was scoped to answer) and #2 (a Confident-tagged claim that's factually off and feeds the timing logic elsewhere in the record).
