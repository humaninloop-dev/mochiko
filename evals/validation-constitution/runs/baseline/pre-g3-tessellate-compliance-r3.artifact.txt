## Critical issues (block sign-off)

**C1 — Region stamp omits an attached compliance module.**
CLAUDE.md line 15: `Ratified: v1.0.0 · ... · modules: gdpr · a11y`. The ledger header and the ratified synthesis both carry three compliance modules: gdpr, **pci-dss**, a11y. Dropping pci-dss from the one line every reader will trust is not a typo to wave through — this is a card-payments marketplace, and pci-dss is the legal-mandate module that governs the riskiest data class in the fact profile. Region stamp must equal ledger, always; it does not.

**C2 — An unwaivable legal-mandate obligation is waived, and the artifact says so itself.**
The ledger's own text: *"Legal-mandate module obligations are unwaivable (D4.2) — a waiver row naming one is a validator FAIL."* Two lines below that sentence sits exactly such a row: `pci-dss quarterly ASV scan (Requirement 11.3.2)`, waived until "first paying customer." The governance-intent review already flagged this at Critical severity (S1) and the founder overruled it — but that overrule happened at the intent/ratification checkpoint, and does not launder the waiver mechanism itself: D4.2 says a legal mandate cannot be discharged through the waiver table, full stop. Carrying it forward into the ledger as a normal waiver row is the FAIL condition the ledger warns about, in the ledger.

**C3 — The waiver and the release gate contradict each other, and the contradiction would block the launch.**
GI-013's release-gate table requires, for **every** promotion to `prod`: *"a dated ASV report for the current quarter under `compliance/pci/`"* — blocking. GI-012 waives the ASV scan *"until the first paying customer."* At launch there is no first paying customer yet — no ASV report can exist — so as written, the very first production promotion cannot pass its own release gate. Either the gate is wrong, the waiver is wrong, or nobody actually traced GI-012 against GI-013. Given the two-week launch clock, this needs the lead's attention before anything else on this list.

**C4 — A principle was minted that the ratified synthesis explicitly excluded.**
`governance-intent.md`, Deliberate exclusions: *"GI-016 — Documentation language and style are not governed... nobody is going to police it (Inês)."* Yet CLAUDE.md line 28 ships a live principle: *"All internal documentation MUST be written in British English,"* backed by a full Three-Part ledger entry and a `cspell` CI gate. This isn't drift or a vague phrasing — it is the opposite of what was ratified, minted anyway, and stamped "traced" in the trace summary. This is fabricated governance, not merely excess.

**C5 — The quantified, blocking principle the founder personally supplied was shipped as an unenforceable platitude.**
Synthesis GI-011 (Confident, elicited from the founder's own bounce-rate data): *"LCP at or under 2.5s at p75 on product and checkout, measured by Lighthouse CI on every PR; a regression past 2.5s blocks merge."* `npm run lhci` is even listed as a real, detected command. What shipped: CLAUDE.md line 27, *"Pages SHOULD load quickly"* — no number, no command, not even in the Quality Gates section — and the ledger's own enforcement/testability fields read *"Engineers watch the Lighthouse scores" / "Pass: pages feel quick" / "Fail: complaints."* This is precisely the Vague-Principle-plus-Missing-Enforcement anti-pattern class the producer told the lead not to spend time checking. It was worth checking.

**C6 — The PCI rules file doesn't cover the path where the card number actually first appears.**
`.claude/rules/mochiko/cardholder-data.md` is path-scoped to `src/payments/**/*.ts` only. But the fact profile and the ledger's own GI-009 text are explicit that the PAN transits `src/checkout/` *before* the tokeniser in `src/payments/`, and the intent-review record shows this exact gap was raised and marked "resolved — scope widened" (S2). The ledger prose was widened; the machine-enforced path pattern was not. Any agent working in `src/checkout/` — where the raw PAN lives — gets zero cardholder-data governance injected. For a SAQ D scope this is the highest-consequence single defect in the set.

## Important issues

**I1 — Coverage threshold shipped stricter than ratified, without authorization or a flag.**
Synthesis GI-005 (floor-asserted, high row): *"≥80% warning, ≥60% blocking — no session override."* Shipped in both CLAUDE.md and the ledger: 70% blocking. "No session override" means exactly what it says; a silent, unflagged change to a floor-card number — even a stricter one — is a fidelity break the trace summary should have caught and didn't.

**I2 — GI-009's two homes describe two different enforcement mechanisms.**
Ledger: enforcement is a custom ESLint rule keyed on functions whose parameter type is `Pan`. Rules file: enforcement is a naming convention (`*Untokenised`, unexported). Neither home says how the other is actually checked; as written a function could satisfy one and not the other. Pick one mechanism and make both homes agree with it.

**I3 — Unresolved placeholder in the shipped region.**
CLAUDE.md line 25 (the a11y principle) ends `<!-- GI-XXX -->` — literal placeholder text, never resolved to `GI-010`. The correct tag *is* used two lines later on the matching quality-gate line, so this is a leftover, not a naming choice. Placeholders are an automatic incompleteness finding.

**I4 — `security.md` carries universal content under a path-scoped rules file.**
`paths: ["**/*"]` for HTTP-boundary auth/session/validation rules is, in effect, "everywhere" — but path-scoped rules inject on Read, and the region's own note says agents must be *triggered* to read them (e.g., before creating a file). Genuinely universal, always-binding content belongs stated in the CLAUDE.md region itself (it already is, as a one-line pointer); relying on a wildcard path match as the sole delivery mechanism for the full auth rule is fragile compared to the region. Worth the lead's judgment call, not an automatic FAIL, but flag it.
