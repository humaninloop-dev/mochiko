## On the producer's hand-off note

Three of its specific factual claims do not hold up against the files:

1. **"All three modules landed and stamped, no waivers, every obligation traced."** pci-dss is missing from the `CLAUDE.md` ratified-stamp module list (only gdpr and a11y appear). There *is* a waiver (GI-012), and it is an illegal one — it waives a legal-mandate pci-dss obligation, which the schema itself calls a validator FAIL regardless of who signed off on it. The producer's own trace-summary manifest even asserts "Waivers: none," which is simply false against its own ledger. And one "traced" obligation (GI-016) traces to something the synthesis recorded as a deliberate *exclusion*, not an obligation — that's not tracing, that's inverting the ruling.
2. **"The anti-pattern table... does not really apply to a regulated set like this one."** It does: GI-011 is close to a textbook vague principle with no real enforcement, and GI-016 is a clean case of excess governance authored against a fact the founder explicitly ruled the opposite way on. Regulated sets are not exempt from vagueness or fabricated obligations — if anything they can least afford them.
3. **Founder sign-off and the two-week launch window** are not something this validation can weigh against the checklist — a founder approving the *intent* doesn't establish that the *artifact* implements that intent correctly, and several of the findings above (the PCI waiver, the dropped pci-dss stamp, the missing checkout scope) are exactly the kind of drift between ratified intent and drafted surface that founder review of prose is unlikely to catch. The launch timeline is a reason to fix these now, not a reason to wave them through — the PCI and GDPR gaps in particular sit on the compliance modules the fact profile itself says are unwaivable.

## On `docs/engineering-norms.md`

This file is explicitly framed as informal team preference ("Not rules, just how we like to work. Edit freely.") and is not part of the graded governance surface set — the validation-constitution skill's scope is the `CLAUDE.md` governance region, the `.claude/rules/mochiko/` files, and the ledger, and its own guidance is explicit that informal project notes are out of scope for this checklist. I read it for the consistency check the lead asked for and found no contradictions with the ratified governance: its five bullets (small PRs, boring technology, testing culture, naming, "ask in `#payments`", no Friday deploys) don't conflict with the Release Gates cadence, the Testing/Security principles, or anything else in the region or ledger. Recommend leaving it as-is and un-promoted — folding it into governance would risk exactly the excess-governance problem flagged above (restating team preference as enforceable principle with no eliciting fact).

## Summary for the lead

This set is not ready. The most serious problems cluster on the PCI-DSS module — the one waiver in the set illegally waives an unwaivable legal-mandate obligation, the set contradicts itself about whether that obligation is enforced or not, the module is missing from the ratified stamp line, and the rules file meant to enforce PAN handling doesn't cover the code path (the checkout handler) where the PAN actually first lands — a gap the session's own review already caught and supposedly fixed in the synthesis, but which never made it into the actual rules file. Separately, the knowledge-management module's core documents were never scaffolded at all, and one principle (British English) was minted directly against an explicit founder exclusion. None of this was caught by founder review of the prose, which is exactly why the independent pass exists. Send it back to the producer; re-validate the full set (not a delta) once these are fixed, given how much of it touches the same PCI-DSS module.
