**Headline finding — the GDPR exclusion doesn't hold up.**

GI-001 excludes the `gdpr` module on this reasoning: "the client contact records are B2B business contacts, which fall outside the GDPR's scope under the legitimate-interest basis." That conflates two different things. Legitimate interest (Art. 6) is a *legal basis for processing* — it doesn't remove data from GDPR's *material scope* (Art. 4). Scope turns on whether the data relates to an identifiable natural person, full stop, B2B or not. `contactName` and `contactEmail` in the `clients` table are personal data of identifiable people regardless of whether the relationship behind them is commercial. German DPAs (including Berlin's own, the BlnBDI) treat B2B contact data as personal data without exception.

So `gdpr` should attach, not be excluded — and this isn't cosmetic: it's the difference between "a leaked client list is a reputational disaster" (current framing, GI-002 risk surface) and "a leaked client list is a reportable breach with a 72-hour clock," which the synthesis actually already half-says in the risk surface line without following through into the module ruling. I'd fold this before authoring starts — even a floor-level `gdpr` expression (breach notification path, data minimization, processor terms with Neon/Vercel) is cheap to write now and expensive to retrofit after there's real client data in the table.

**Is `high` overkill? No — but one line item inside it is.**

Given the actual legal exposure here — Stornorechnung-style invoice immutability (GI-012) is real German tax law, and the GDPR correction above adds a second concrete regulatory surface — I don't think `high` is disproportionate to the *risk*. The one-way ratchet is a real commitment, but the risk it's committing against is real too, so I wouldn't undo it.

What I would push back on is GI-013, the weekly architecture-review-minutes-under-`docs/adr/` commitment. That's process weight sized for a team bigger than 1.4 people. For a solo founder plus a two-day-a-week contractor, a *mandatory weekly* cadence is the kind of rule that gets quietly skipped the first time Lena is heads-down shipping, and a governance surface with visibly-lapsed rules is worse than one that was scoped honestly from the start. Compare GI-017, which excludes observability beyond `console.log` "until there are paying customers" — that's the right shape: scope the process to a trigger you actually control, not a calendar. I'd suggest the lead consider re-scoping GI-013 the same way (e.g., "as needed, minuted when a decision is made" rather than weekly) rather than dropping it — the intent behind it is sound, the cadence isn't.

**CLAUDE.md — does the wording and the rules hold up?**

Line by line:

- "Prettier + eslint, both in CI, must pass" — matches `ci.yml` exactly. Good.

Whoever's right, someone should reconcile it before it's ratified into the surface set. And as written, the rule has no escape valve — if Tomas isn't on his two days and something breaks in production, Lena is stuck between "no self-merge" and "fix it now." The bigger gap isn't a wording problem, it's an absence: GI-012 — invoice immutability, the single most legally load-bearing thing in the whole synthesis — has no line in CLAUDE.md at all yet. Given this file is the draft of the surface set, that's the one I'd make sure doesn't get lost between "minted in synthesis" and "authored into the surface."
