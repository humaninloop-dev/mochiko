## Blocking

**1. GI-001's module ruling contradicts the identity section's own risk statement, and the legal reasoning under it looks wrong.**
Fact profile: "personal data of EU residents: only the account email — the client contact records are B2B business contacts, which fall outside the GDPR's scope under the legitimate-interest basis, so the `gdpr` module does not attach." Two paragraphs later, under Risk surface: "a leaked client list would be a reputational disaster and **a GDPR-reportable breach**." The synthesis asserts in one place that GDPR doesn't attach and in another that a breach of the same data would be GDPR-reportable. That's a direct fact↔risk contradiction, not a stylistic quibble.

It also looks substantively wrong, and I'd flag it as an external-sourced fact needing a real check rather than my say-so: GDPR's scope turns on whether data relates to an identifiable natural person, not on B2B vs. B2C — a named contact's name and email at a client company is personal data regardless of the commercial context. "Legitimate interest" (Article 6(1)(f)) is a *lawful basis for processing* covered personal data; it is not a scope carve-out that removes data from GDPR's reach. If that reasoning doesn't hold, `gdpr` should attach, which would ripple into floor expression and probably a minted principle (breach-notification handling, data minimization on the clients table, etc.) — this isn't a small correction.

*Resolution:* before folding, verify this against an actual GDPR reference (or default conservatively: treat client contact records as personal data and attach the module) and reconcile the fact-profile line with the risk-surface line so they don't say opposite things.

## My own take (asked for, not part of the verdict above)

If I were choosing governance for a two-person, no-customers-yet invoicing tool from scratch, I'd go asymmetric rather than uniformly `high`: hard floor exactly where the money and legal exposure actually live — invoice immutability (GI-012 is a good example of this done right, well-sourced to a real legal fact), VAT/rounding test coverage, and real handling of whatever personal data turns out to be in scope once #1 is sorted — and light-touch everywhere else until there's a second engineer or a paying customer to protect. A weekly architecture-review ritual and a hard no-self-merge rule read to me as borrowed from a bigger team's playbook rather than earned by anything in this fact profile. That's a preference, not a ruling — `high` may well be right once #1's contradiction is resolved and the rationale in #4 gets filled in; I just don't think the current record justifies it yet on its own terms.
