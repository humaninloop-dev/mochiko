## Findings

**1. CRITICAL — GI-001's own risk declaration contradicts its module screening.**
GI-001 concludes the `gdpr` module does not attach, reasoning that the translators' client
contact records (company, contact name, contact email, billing address, VAT id) are "B2B business
contacts, which fall outside the GDPR's scope under the legitimate-interest basis." That reasoning
conflates a *lawful basis for processing* (legitimate interest) with *exemption from scope* —
they're not the same thing, and a named contact person's email and name are personal data under
GDPR regardless of whether the relationship is B2B. The same synthesis then undercuts its own
conclusion two sections later: the Identity block's risk surface says outright that "a leaked
client list would be a reputational disaster and a **GDPR-reportable breach**." A fact profile
that screens a module out and then names a breach under that exact regulation in its own risk
surface is a self-contradiction, not a settled fact. It also lines up with something already true
of the scaffold: CLAUDE.md's day-one rule — "Client records are our users' clients, not ours.
Treat them as confidential; never log them" — is already written as if this data is sensitive.
Three independent signals (the risk line, the existing convention, ordinary GDPR scope) point the
opposite way from the module screening's conclusion.
*Failure scenario:* authoring proceeds on "gdpr does not attach," so no breach-notification
procedure, retention limit, or data-subject-request path gets floored — and the client list is
exactly the asset the synthesis itself flags as a reportable-breach risk.
*Resolution path:* route back to Lena (and ideally external counsel, since this is a legal
scope question, not a team-preference one) — confirm whether client contact-person data is in
GDPR scope. If yes, re-run the module screening with `gdpr` attached before anything is authored;
that changes what the floor requires.

**2. MEDIUM — GI-003's depth-level rationale is a bare echo, and it's locked one-way.**
"Declared level: high" carries the rationale "the lead recommended `high` — invoicing is money;
Lena agreed," and the ratchet note says high is terminal. There's no visible record that the
greenfield default (low, arbitrated upward on cause) was actually offered before the jump to
high — the rationale reads as a stamp, not an arbitration trail. For a one-way, permanent

*Resolution path:* confirm the low-first recommendation actually happened and capture the real
reasoning (most likely the invoice-immutability legal requirement and VAT/rounding exposure named
elsewhere in the synthesis) rather than leaving "invoicing is money" as the whole record.

*Resolution path:* ask Lena directly whether she wants the standing cadence, or whether an

contradicted by its own risk declaration — independent of how easy it is to fix. Authoring
should not start yet.

## On CLAUDE.md as the draft surface set (you asked directly — flagging that principle
formulation/enforceability is normally outside this review's jurisdiction, closed at D1, so treat
this as a direct answer to your question rather than a formal finding)

Line by line it's in decent shape: the TypeScript/lint/prettier rules are crisp and CI already
enforces them; the Drizzle-only-migrations rule matches the adopted layer-rules module and the
package.json scripts. Two things I'd want the producer to notice rather than silently inherit:

handoff" problem.

## Is `high` overkill for two people and no customers? (your question, my opinion, not a
finding — D1 is Lena's ruling and I'm not reopening it)

Not overkill everywhere, but probably not evenly earned everywhere either. The parts of this
product that touch money and legal correctness — invoice immutability, VAT/rounding math, and
(pending finding 1) client-data confidentiality — justify real rigor on Lena's own logic: "if we
get that wrong my users get fined, not us." That's a genuine floor-worthy risk regardless of
customer count. A weekly architecture-review ritual and a blanket "tests for everything" mandate
for a 1.4-person team, though, look like ceremony reaching past what the fact profile actually
pays for. If I'd been in the room, I'd have argued for `high` scoped to the money-and-data path
specifically, with everything else at the greenfield default and escalated later if the team or
risk surface grows — rather than one uniform terminal level applied to the whole surface on day
one.

## What I'd have chosen for this product

Given the shape of it — solo founder plus a two-day-a-week contractor, real legal exposure on
invoice correctness and (likely) real GDPR exposure on client data, three-year horizon aimed at
sale or wind-down, no customers yet — I'd have leaned toward a graduated floor: strict on
invoice-math correctness and data confidentiality (tests, immutability enforcement, no-log rules,
a real GDPR posture once finding 1 is settled), and deliberately light everywhere else — skip the
standing meeting, skip blanket coverage mandates, keep the existing lightweight PR-review habit
Lena already has instead of adding new ritual on top of it. The risk here isn't uniform across the
codebase, and I don't think the governance should be either.
