Coherence lens — Halyard governance-intent review

My recommended status: **needs-revision**. Two of my findings are severe enough (foundational
HIPAA/BAA reality claims contradicted by the repo) that I'd want them resolved before
ratification rather than folded in as accepted risk, but both look like a quick round-trip to
Marcus rather than a re-run of the session, so I'm not calling it critical-gaps outright — your
call once you've seen the shape of them.

I formed all of this cold, against the frozen synthesis, the codebase analysis, and the repo
files, before opening the coverage seat's notes. Tally: 7 findings raised independently, 1
overlaps with theirs (folded below), 6 survive as mine alone. I also reviewed their three other
findings and agree with all of them.

**The two I'd stop on:**

1. GI-007 (FLOOR-OBS, marked Confident) states the PHI redaction filter "in `halyard/logging.py`
   keeps intake fields out of log lines." The codebase analysis explicitly says the opposite —
   it looked for a redaction filter in `halyard/settings/` and `halyard/logging.py` and found
   none. GI-011, the minted "PHI never in logs or error reports" principle, leans on that same
   nonexistent mechanism. As written, you'd be authoring a floor rule and a principle around code
   that isn't there — worst case, a real intake field ships into a log line the day this goes
   live, or the redaction filter really exists somewhere else and this is just a stale path.
   Either way it needs a straight answer from Marcus before it's Confident.

2. GI-004 (FLOOR-SEC, also Confident) says hosting is covered for BAA purposes because Halyard
   runs on "Heroku's HIPAA-eligible Shield tier." `app.json` shows `standard-1x` dynos and
   `heroku-postgresql:standard-0` — not Shield-tier resources — and the analysis notes no
   Private Space or Shield configuration anywhere in the repo. Given GI-001 records live,
   three-year BAAs with two clinic groups, believing the hosting-side BAA obligation is covered
   when it may not be is a live legal gap, not a wording nit. Needs a direct check: is Halyard
   actually provisioned on Shield outside what the repo config shows, or does this claim need to
   come out of "Confident" until it's true?

**A same-card contradiction:**

3. GI-004's own expression says "`pip-audit` blocks merge at high/critical" — i.e. dependency
   vulnerability scanning is live and gating. GI-014 waives "dependency vulnerability scanning
   runs" under that same FLOOR-SEC card as "not now." Both can't be true. The CI file backs
   GI-014's version — there's no pip-audit step anywhere in `ci.yml`, only ruff. Pick one: either
   pip-audit is a new gate you're adding now (drop the waiver) or it's genuinely not-now (drop
   the clause from GI-004's expression).

4. Related and smaller: GI-014's Contested mark has no rationale beyond "not now" and no revisit
   trigger, unlike its sibling waiver GI-015 which has both. An un-rationalized ruling is a
   finding on its own even before you get to the contradiction above.

**Two more on my own list:**

5. GI-013 (Storybook + visual regression, minted, marked Confident) is elicited from Marcus
   saying "sure, if it's not much work" — that's a hedge, not an unconditional ruling, and
   nothing in the intent resolves what happens if it turns out to be real work retrofitting
   Storybook onto existing components. Either firm up the ruling or scope it (e.g. new
   components only) so the mark matches what was actually said.

6. GI-015's waiver justification — "Heroku router logs already record every request, which
   covers it" as a stand-in for authentication audit logging — is doing more work than it can
   bear. Platform router logs are request/path/status level; they don't generally attribute an
   authenticated user identity or distinguish a failed login from a successful one, which is
   what "audit logging of authentication events" usually means. Worth a direct question to
   Marcus about what the router logs actually capture before this locks in as Confident.

One thing I deliberately did *not* do: the coverage seat's notes mention being "inclined to say
the two-person team makes the whole hipaa module heavy" but didn't write it up, and you asked me
to consider the same angle. I looked, but a compliance-module-derived obligation is out of
jurisdiction for an "excess" call regardless of team size — that's true whatever GI-015 and
GI-016/hipaa look like from a two-person-team lens, so I didn't flag the module itself. What I
did instead is #6 above: not "is this too much," but "does the stated justification for waiving
part of it actually hold up." That's the version of this concern that's actually mine to raise.
