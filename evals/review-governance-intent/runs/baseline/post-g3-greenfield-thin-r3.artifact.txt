## Findings, most severe first

### 1. CRITICAL — GI-001's GDPR negative contradicts GI-002's own risk line, and the legal reasoning is wrong on the scaffold's own evidence
GI-001 rules the `gdpr` module doesn't attach: "the client contact records are B2B business contacts, which fall outside the GDPR's scope under the legitimate-interest basis." Two lines later, GI-002's "Risk surface" says: "a leaked client list would be... a GDPR-reportable breach." The same document asserts both that GDPR doesn't apply and that a breach would be GDPR-reportable.

The reasoning is also wrong against the scaffold itself: `schema.ts`'s `clients` table has `contactName` and `contactEmail` — personal data of an identifiable natural person under GDPR Art. 4(1), regardless of the B2B relationship between Quillon's user and their client. "Legitimate interest" (Art. 6(1)(f)) is a lawful *basis for processing* personal data, not an exclusion from GDPR's scope — it presupposes the data is in scope.

**Failure scenario:** the producer authors the surface set with no GDPR-derived obligations (no breach-notification procedure, no data-subject-rights handling, no lawful-basis documentation) because the module was ruled out. The `clients` table leaks. Lena, per her own GI-002 line, now owns "a GDPR-reportable breach" with no governance surface that ever anticipated it.

**Resolution path:** ask Lena directly whether she means the GI-002 risk line literally. If yes, `gdpr` attaches and GI-001's negative must be corrected before fold. If she means something narrower, GI-001's negative needs to be restated so it stops contradicting GI-002.
