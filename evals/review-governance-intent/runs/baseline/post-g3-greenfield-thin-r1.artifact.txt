## Survivors

**F1 — CRITICAL — GI-001 self-contradicts on GDPR.**
The fact profile's own risk surface says "a leaked client list would be a reputational disaster and a
GDPR-reportable breach," but the same fact profile's module ruling excludes `gdpr` entirely, reasoning
that client contact records are "B2B business contacts, which fall outside the GDPR's scope under the
legitimate-interest basis." That reasoning conflates a *lawful basis* for processing (legitimate
interest, one of six bases under Art. 6) with a *scope exclusion* — GDPR has no B2B carve-out, and a
named contact person's email/name at a client company is personal data of an identifiable natural
person regardless of the B2B context. `CLAUDE.md` itself already treats this data as needing that level
of care ("Client records are our users' clients, not ours... never log them").
*Failure scenario:* the producer authors v1.0.0 with no GDPR floor obligations (breach-notification
timing, processor terms for Neon/Vercel, data-subject-rights handling) because the module was waved off
— then the exact leak GI-001 names happens, with no governance surface in place to have required
handling it.
*Resolution path:* one question to Lena — "does Quillon store the name or email of any identifiable
person, including a client's contact person, who is in the EU?" GI-001's own fact list already answers
yes. Re-run the module gate with `gdpr` attached and correct GI-001 before authoring.

**F2 — CRITICAL — "Team reality" is contradicted by the scaffold's own on-disk file.**
The synthesis states "no formal code review" as team reality (used to settle dimension 5 by convergence
from dimension 1). `quillon/CLAUDE.md` line 5, already on disk, says: "Every PR gets a review from Lena
or Tomas before merge — no self-merging." That's a formal review gate, already declared.
*Failure scenario:* the producer authors from a team-reality fact that's already false on the scaffold's
own file, producing a surface set that's internally inconsistent with the repo it's meant to govern —
and GI-013 (F4 below) was arbitrated against this same false premise.
*Resolution path:* ask Lena whether the CLAUDE.md PR-review rule is still current practice. If yes,
correct "Team reality" in the synthesis before folding anything else that depends on it.

**F3 — CRITICAL — GI-006 (FLOOR-ERR) has an unrecorded ruling.**
Every sibling floor row records what was decided (GI-004 "at floor level," GI-007 "kept"). GI-006's
Ruling/Expression cell is blank, yet still carries a Confident mark.
*Failure scenario:* the producer has nothing to author error-handling floor expression from, and either
invents a default (defeating the point of interrogating for one) or silently drops the floor.
*Resolution path:* lead confirms with Lena what FLOOR-ERR's expression actually was and fills the cell
before folding.

**F4 — needs-revision — GI-013 (weekly architecture-review meeting) rests on the false team-reality
premise (F2) and has no quoted rationale, unlike GI-012's fully-quoted, specific legal reasoning.** A
lead proposal accepted with a bare "ok." is thin next to its neighbor. A weekly recurring meeting is a
real process commitment for a solo founder plus a contractor present two days a week.
*Failure scenario:* the surface mandates a cadence Tomas's schedule can't reliably sustain; it lapses
within a month and teaches the team that governance obligations are optional.
*Resolution path:* once F2 is corrected, re-confirm with Lena whether the weekly-meeting cadence is
actually wanted, or trace the real need to a lighter surface (e.g., an ADR entry on the decisions that
happen, not a scheduled meeting).

**F5 — needs-revision — GI-011 ("tests for everything") has no quoted rationale**, unlike its
neighbors. The one concrete testing need actually evidenced in the document (Risk surface, and
independently in CLAUDE.md's own rule) is invoice math — money, VAT, rounding — not blanket coverage.
*Failure scenario:* the producer authors an unscoped "test everything" floor obligation; a two-person
team either can't sustain it and starts ignoring the gate, or burns time testing low-risk UI while the
actually-named risk is just one instance of a generic rule rather than a named, elevated one.
*Resolution path:* ask Lena whether "everything" is really meant, or whether the intent should trace
specifically to money-path code, per the fact that's actually in the record.

**F6 — needs-revision — GI-016's weekly release notes carry no "until paying customers" caveat**, while
GI-017 explicitly discounts observability on exactly that basis, for the same pre-launch state.
*Failure scenario:* a weekly release-notes obligation gets authored for an audience that doesn't exist
yet, and either erodes unenforced (same risk as F4) or busies a two-person team writing notes nobody
reads.
*Resolution path:* ask Lena whether release notes should wait for paying customers too, for consistency
with how GI-017 already reasons about this same state.

**F7 — needs-revision (process/ledger) — GI-003's rationale doesn't show a recommend-then-arbitrate
trail.** It reads "the lead recommended high... Lena agreed," not "recommended low, Lena arbitrated up
because—." This is a ledger-fidelity note, not a challenge to the level itself — I'm not grading this
stricter than the declared level, and "invoicing is money" is a legitimate escalation reason. But the
ratchet is one-way and terminal, so the trail is worth getting right before it's locked.
*Resolution path:* confirm whether Lena was actually offered the low default first and chose to
arbitrate up (record that trail), or redo the recommendation step properly before ratifying.

## Asked for separately: my own view (opinion, not a review finding)

For a true two-person, pre-revenue greenfield with no contractual commitments, I'd have split the depth
decision instead of setting one blanket level: keep the money-path rigor high — immutable issued
invoices, mandatory tests on invoice/VAT/rounding math, a real DB constraint — because that risk is real
and doesn't scale with team size. But I'd have kept process ritual (weekly architecture meetings, weekly
release notes) light or trigger-based ("adopt this once there's a second engineer / once there are
paying customers") rather than mandated now, the same way GI-017 already reasons about observability.
Two people, one of them two-days-a-week, is exactly the situation where ritual-heavy governance gets
quietly ignored rather than followed — which is worse than not having it, because it teaches everyone
that the rules are optional. That's a preference, not a ruling: Lena already confirmed `high` at the
checkpoint, and this doesn't reopen that.
