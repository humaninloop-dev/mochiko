# Devil's-advocate report — Quillon sizing gate (single, solo seat)

## Verdict: hold. One blocking finding; the rest are producer-facing cleanup, not blockers.

## Blocking: GI-001's GDPR negation is wrong, and the synthesis contradicts itself on it

GI-001 rules the `gdpr` module out on the theory that the client contact records (company,
contact name, contact email, billing address, VAT id) are "B2B business contacts, which fall
outside the GDPR's scope under the legitimate-interest basis." That's a category error, not a
judgment call: legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) is a *lawful basis for processing* — it says
processing is permitted, not that GDPR doesn't apply. A contact name and email tied to an
identified natural person is personal data under Art. 4(1) whether the relationship is B2B or
B2C. There is no B2B carve-out in GDPR's material scope.

The synthesis catches itself out three paragraphs later: the Identity section calls a leaked
client list "a reputational disaster and a GDPR-reportable breach." You cannot have a
GDPR-reportable breach of data GDPR doesn't cover. One of these two Confident-marked statements
is wrong, and it's the module-trigger one. Lena confirmed this synthesis as thorough at
checkpoint — this is exactly the kind of internal contradiction a single cold seat exists to
catch before it hardens into an unattached module.

Concretely, this means the client table (`contactName`, `contactEmail`, `billingAddress`,
`vatId`) is in GDPR's scope: breach-notification obligations, a real basis for the "never log
client records" instinct already in CLAUDE.md, and probably a data-processing posture toward
Neon and Vercel as processors. I'd send GI-001 back for correction — attach `gdpr` (or whatever
the equivalent module is) with the fact profile — before folding dispositions. Everything else
in the fact profile (no health data, no cardholder data, no contractual commitments) reads
clean; this is a single, isolated, but load-bearing error.

## CLAUDE.md — does the wording hold up?

Four of six rules are solid and machine-checkable, matching the real commands table and
CI workflow exactly:
- TS strict / no `any`, prettier+eslint in CI, schema-lives-in-one-file-via-Drizzle — no issues.
- "Write tests for the invoice math... totals, VAT, rounding" is well-scoped and matches GI-012's
  Storno reasoning. This is *better targeted* than the minted principle it should trace to.

Two rules need attention before the producer treats this file as a draft surface set:

1. **"Every PR gets a review from Lena or Tomas before merge — no self-merging" contradicts the
   synthesis's own Team reality line: "no formal code review."** A mandatory reviewer with no
   self-merge *is* a formal code-review gate — that's the definition of one. Either the Team
   reality note is stale or CLAUDE.md is overclaiming. Worth resolving because it also isn't
   backed by anything mechanical: I see no branch-protection config anywhere in the scaffold, so
   today this rule is an honor-system sentence, not an enforced gate. If release-gates or
   floor-sec is going to encode this, it needs an actual GitHub branch-protection rule, not just
   a line in CLAUDE.md.

2. **"Never log \[client records\]" sits in tension with GI-017's "observability beyond
   console.log excluded until paying customers."** console.log *is* a log. If a developer
   debugs the invoice pipeline with `console.log(client)`, they've violated rule 5 using the
   only observability channel GI-017 leaves them. The producer should either scope rule 5 to
   "never log full client objects — log invoice/client IDs only" or otherwise reconcile the two
   rules explicitly, rather than leaving both as absolute statements that collide in practice.

Also loose: "before shipping it" (rule 4) doesn't say before merge or before deploy — minor, but
worth tightening since it'll become a rule the producer has to encode as a gate somewhere
specific.

## Is `high` overkill for a two-person greenfield?

No — and separately, it's not actually up for debate. GI-003's ratchet is one-way and marked
terminal, so re-litigating the level itself isn't a live option regardless of my opinion. On the
substance anyway: I agree with `high`. Invoice-math correctness and the statutory Storno rule in
GI-012 are legal-liability surfaces that don't shrink because the team is small — a founder and a
part-time contractor are *more* exposed to a wrong VAT line than a company with a finance
department, not less.

What I'd push back on is one specific **expression** of `high`, not the level: **GI-013's
weekly architecture-review meeting with committed minutes** is process theater for a team of
one-and-a-fifth people. A synchronous weekly ritual with a formal artifact is proportionate to a
team big enough to lose shared context between people — Lena and Tomas aren't that team yet. I'd
have the producer author this as "an ADR gets written under `docs/adr/` when a nontrivial
architecture decision is made" rather than calendar-driven. Note for the record: Lena's "ok" to
this proposal at dimension 9 reads like acceptance of a lead suggestion in the room, not a
considered choice about a recurring time cost for a two-person shop — worth a second look, not
a hard block.

`GI-011`'s "tests for everything" is also broader than what Lena herself reached for on day one
(CLAUDE.md scopes tests to invoice math specifically). I'd narrow the minted principle to match
her instinct — money-path and legal-invariant logic (invoice math, VAT, rounding, the immutability
constraint) — rather than leave a literal "everything" mandate that a two-person team can't
sustain without it becoming the rule everyone quietly ignores.

One more small gap: GI-006 (FLOOR-ERR) has no ruling/expression recorded in the table — every
other floor card says "at floor level," "kept," etc.; this one is blank. Probably a transcription
gap rather than a real omission, but for a money product, error-handling floor expression (partial
writes, idempotent retries on invoice issuance) is not the row I'd want left ambiguous. Worth the
lead confirming what was actually decided here before authoring.

## My own view, since you asked

For a solo founder plus a two-day-a-week contractor building an invoicing product with real
statutory constraints (German Storno rules) and no customers yet, I'd land in the same place:
`high`, but expressed as lightly as the money risk allows. Concretely I'd keep release-gates and
knowledge-management as adopted (good fit, low friction), keep BE-HEX dropped (hexagonal
architecture is real overkill at this size), but I'd make the GDPR floor explicit rather than
absent — a breach-notification runbook and a real data-processing posture toward Neon/Vercel as
processors — precisely because a two-person team is the team least equipped to improvise one
during an actual breach. I'd cut every ritual that assumes more than two people are in the room
(the weekly meeting) and keep every rule that assumes money math is where the real damage lives
(invoice immutability, VAT test coverage). The failure mode I'd be most worried about a year from
now isn't under-governance — it's a solo founder inheriting a `high`-level surface set that's
heavy on ceremony and light on the one thing (GDPR) that was actually true about the product from
day one.
