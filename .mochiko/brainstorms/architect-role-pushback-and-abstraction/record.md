## Analysis — is author≠grader causing the additive review bias? (lead assessment, Q4 input)

- **A1 — No. The cause is charter asymmetry, not independence.** Every sanctioned finding class in both plan reviewers is absence-shaped (completeness: missing/unmeasurable) or conflict-shaped (feasibility: contradiction/violation). A reviewer's only sanctioned win is a finding; every sanctioned finding implies "add". A reviewer with no remove-shaped class cannot say "too much" no matter how freely it talks to the author. Removing independence removes the fence, not the bias.
- **A2 — Independence demonstrably catches real defects in the same driver case.** FEAT-002 round 1's two BLOCKING finds were cold, independent catches: F1 (apiKeyHelper is a settings key — the design's clearing premise was false) and F2 (child-env construction ordered after first spawn). Both are defects a self-grading producer had already not seen. Prior evidence same direction: the paired-cold-review data point — only 2 of 6 Important findings found by both reviewers (kinako-derived; naive de-duplication loses Important-class findings).
- **A3 — "Free interaction" already exists where it is safe.** Layer-2 in-loop mesh is the ruled default (`team-method-vs-command-shape` D1); producers and verifiers may interact freely mid-loop. Cold review is deliberately a *stage property* at end-stage (D2 there). The user's appetite for interaction can be met inside that ruling: an **interrogatory round** — the altitude reviewer questions the producer ("which requirement pays for this?"), answers land on record, verdict stays solely the reviewer's.
