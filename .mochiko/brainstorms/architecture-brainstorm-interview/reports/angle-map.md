## Angle map

Drawn from the frame's problem, destination and out-of-scope lines only, plus free repo grounding. Angles are numbered locally (cite as "angle N"); none is a durable ID. LB marks a load-bearing angle: a ruling would likely change if the session never took it.

### Class: jurisdiction — floors and owners the change would hit

1. LB · Focus versus the breadth invariant. Destination (a) has the desk brainstorm "the part of the architecture they need to focus on", but `arch.breadth-invariant` and `patterns-architecture-shelves.breadth-invariant` are class floor: every in-scope shelf row walked, never silently skipped, "no magnitude scaling". Is focus an ordering and depth choice inside the full walk, or a selection that needs a recorded supersession of two floors? Does breadth bind once per baseline or on every visit?
2. LB · Who picks the focus, from what. "Chosen from the context the workflow is given" is undefined per visit kind: `$ARGUMENTS`, the health view (`arch.dm-health-first`, a floor that runs before the ask), the spec, FEATURES.md, code. A desk-picked focus is a narrowing; `arch.truth-user-ruling` and `brainstorm.user-steers` put scope moves on the user's word. Is the focus a guess on a frame card the user rules, and do health items seed it or crowd out the user's own demand?
3. LB · A feature's delta demand versus implement's design phase. No other command routes a feature's structural need to the desk; implement's design phase writes the store's `proposed` elements and the run's `architecture.md` drawing, signed at its design checkpoint (implement render, `impl.baselines-in-place-marked`). `arch.no-delivery-harness` keeps delivery out of the desk. What does a feature-delta visit produce that the design phase consumes, and through which read (the sufficiency check reads the index)? If the user goes straight to `/mochiko:implement`, the larger guess surface is untouched.
4. Recommend-then-arbitrate is already a floor (`patterns-architecture-shelves.recommend-then-arbitrate`: default, its cost, what argues against it, then the user rules). Brainstorm's options rule (two real options, one cheaper) and ratified-yes streak overlap it. Do the new mechanics sit on top, extend, or replace it? Replacing touches a floor in a skill the frame did not put out of scope.
5. Who holds the conversation. `spec.lead-holds-story-stage` keeps the user conversation with the lead, inline; the desk's producer is principal-architect on a plan-only dispatch, graded by a peer, reviewed by tech-lead (`arch.seat-architect-producer`, `arch.sound-loop-floor`). If the DM interviews, the architect's craft reaches the user only through recommendations; if the architect interviews, the lead-holds rule breaks. Is the interview the produce leg or before it?
6. LB · The cheaper road: do nothing at the desk. Route architecture thinking to `/mochiko:brainstorm` and add only a handed-in door, as specify did (`spec.stage-inputs-in-hand`, `spec.handed-in-slate`: a named record opens as facts, forks skipped, the close never skipped). The options rule demands this road be weighed by name.
7. Convergence cost later. BACKLOG.md:37-41 opens a three-command convergence session after this one, "boilerplate only, never judgment; until then the three rule sets drift by ruling". Each mechanism the desk adopts is either copied in brainstorm's or specify's wording, so it converges later, or adapted, so it diverges. A desk binding of `mochiko:analysis-iterative` also needs its own own-rules-win line, as `spec.own-rules-win` and `brainstorm.own-rules-win` carry.

### Class: mechanism — what the desk takes, adapts or drops

8. LB · The per-mechanism table and which stop rule governs. Frame card, decision map, fact before decision, options with the cheaper road, ratified-yes streak, vague-answer re-ask, show before asking, blind second list, blind angle map, cold review, record as you go, accept screens: each needs a take, adapt or drop ruling per visit kind, not a wholesale import. The desk closes on a one-line goal (`arch.dm-converge-goal`, enforced by `arch.fail.no-verdict`); brainstorm closes on an empty map; specify's story stage closes when nothing is open. Two done conditions in one visit conflict.
9. LB · The blind list analog and off-shelf concerns. Specify's blind journey list gets the intent and frame, never the slate; its differences become candidate rows (`spec.blind-journey-list`). The desk analog: a fresh seat gets the context and returns the concerns it sees; differences against shelf plus store become candidate AX rows. Kinako's store holds 33 AX rows, mostly product-specific ("Corpus storage and format", "Engine port isolation") rather than the 13 backend shelf dimensions, so real concerns live off the shelf. How do off-shelf concerns enter today, and does the blind list become their door?
10. Show before asking, in architecture. `brainstorm.show-before-asking` puts a rough artifact in the message: a mock, a worked scenario with real numbers. The architecture form is a small Mermaid sketch of an option or a numbers scenario (tenant count, p95). Are these interview sketches throwaway, or do they seed the maintained diagrams of destination (b)?
11. LB · Fact before decision, and who verifies outside claims. Desk facts come from code, specs, the map, floor cards and outside the repo (provider limits, pricing); shelf defaults are memory-asserted (`patterns-architecture-shelves.memory-asserted-provenance`). The external-claims doctrine says producers disclose and review seats verify, but the desk's tech-lead grade names no grading skill (`arch.seat-tech-lead-grader` carries no pointer). Who live-checks a load-bearing outside claim before a stance lands?
12. Cold review per visit. Desk writes already take the tech-lead review leg. Adding a brainstorm-style cold review grades the same rulings twice; dropping it loses the blind map's coverage hunt. Excess-machinery risk: name the cheaper shape, for example the blind map folded into the tech-lead grade on standard-size visits only.

### Class: visit-shape — scaling, kinds, persistence

13. Size per visit, never from the governance dial. Brainstorm has a user-ruled size (small, standard, too-big). `arch.no-depth-dial-coupling` is a floor: the desk never reads the governance depth level. So any scaling is a new per-visit desk condition the user rules. Which mechanisms fire at which size, and what does a one-row amendment pay? Each added seat (blind list, review, architect, plan peer, tech-lead) multiplies cost and triggers the transport floor.
14. Visit kinds differ in kind. First baseline greenfield elicits; brownfield reconstructs and confirms an `Assumed` derivation (`arch.tools-brownfield-reconstruction`), which is a confirmation wall, not elicitation. A drift finding takes a closed three-way disposition (`authoring-architecture-store.drift-user-disposition`). Fired triggers route to `/mochiko:feature`. Does "every visit" really mean every kind, and is brownfield confirmation by exception or per element?
15. LB · State across a long visit. A baseline visit is long; brainstorm writes as it goes (`brainstorm.record-as-you-go`). The desk has no session artifact: the store home declares only `spine.md`, `concerns.md` and `concerns/` (`mochiko-cli home .mochiko/product/architecture`), and store writes land only as graded, ruled batches. An interrupted visit loses its decision map and rulings in progress. Where does in-visit state live: a new home, or the brainstorm home, either one a migration?
16. Non-backend surfaces have no shelf. `patterns-architecture-shelves.honest-gaps` says only the backend shelf is seeded; other surfaces lean on project facts and floor categories. For frontend, mobile and desktop products the interview is the only substance, which raises the stakes of how it derives rows. Composed full-stack scopes need focus across surfaces.

### Class: user — what "tight to what the user wants" means here

17. Wants versus technology. Users usually hold product wants (scale, tenancy, compliance, offline, latency, cost ceiling), not technology preferences. Brainstorm's vague-answer re-ask and its convention probe could force rulings the user cannot make well, producing noise. Is "you pick" a legitimate delegation, recorded as such, given `arch.truth-user-ruling` still needs the user's word? Are questions put as user-visible consequences, in plain words with one example each?
18. LB · Streak calibration on default-heavy walks. A 13-row walk where most defaults fit makes a ratified streak the normal case; brainstorm flags every three ratified rulings in a row and switches form (`brainstorm.ratified-yes`). Applied unchanged it fires constantly. How does the desk tell informed acceptance from fatigue, given fatigue is the exact failure in the problem line?

### Class: record — provenance, rationale, re-opening

19. Provenance in the store. Specify marks each slate row `user's words`, `lead's guess, ratified`, `blind list` or `handed in` (`spec.slate-provenance`). An AX row carries Ruling and Rationale only (`mochiko-cli template architecture-concerns`), so "shelf defaults plus the architect's guesses" cannot be seen in a store today, nor measured after the build. Adding a field is a template migration plus a store-skill change.
20. Rationale's home. `authoring-architecture-store.present-tense-no-history` links rationale that lives in a record. If a desk visit yields a record, where does it live, does every visit mint one, and what does that add to the KM landing (`arch.dm-km-landing`) and the session index?
21. Re-opening ruled rows. A focus interview on later visits can re-litigate decided rows. When may a decided AX row reopen: only on an amendment demand, a fired trigger or a drift finding, or whenever the interview drifts there?

### Class: diagram — home, owner, upkeep, taste, render, audience

22. LB · Who owns ER and state-machine diagrams. The product data model lives in `.mochiko/product/data-model.md`, a baseline written in place by implement's design phase (`mochiko-cli home .mochiko/product`, `impl.baselines-in-place-marked`); state machines belong to `mochiko:patterns-entity-modeling`. The store is one home, one writer (`authoring-architecture-store.one-home-one-writer`). A desk-drawn ER or state diagram is either a second home for data-model truth that drifts, or a desk write into a baseline it does not own.
23. LB · "One diagram, not a gallery." The spine template says of its container diagram "One diagram, not a gallery — a second view earns its place only where the first cannot carry it", and denies undeclared `##` headings in `spine.md`. A maintained set of many types needs an earning rule per type (when a state machine or flowchart earns its place), the plan-minimalism ladder applied, or it sprawls.
24. LB · Upkeep and drift. The landing fold flips statuses and writes `As-built` and `Drift` on touched rows (`authoring-architecture-store.fold-duty`); the drift probe grades `As-built` claims against code (`arch.drift-empirical`). Who redraws a diagram when a feature lands: implement's store landing, or the next desk visit? Is a diagram an as-built claim the probe grades? A stale diagram misleads agents more than a missing one.
25. One truth, two encodings. The spine already carries an element table and a container diagram. When they disagree, which wins for an agent? Are diagrams derived from the tables by code (kernel-class tooling, admitted only by recorded ruling, GI-019-kernel-tooling-admission) or hand-drawn by a seat (a judgment write, so the sound loop applies)? The repo-root index is regenerated by the store skill: do diagrams ride into it?
26. LB · Taste carried into Mermaid is an untested bet. The diagram-design README's tagline is "No Mermaid slop."; it says "For docs that live in Git and change every sprint, Mermaid is still the better fit." and "Diagram Design is for the diagram you are going to publish." (verified: github.com/cathrynlavery/diagram-design, fetched 2026-10-10). Its taste rules (one accent colour, 1px hairlines, no shadows, 4px grid, a style guide as the single source for colours and fonts) are SVG-level. Which survive Mermaid's themeVariables and classDef, and GitHub's renderer limits? Which of its 44 types have a Mermaid carrier? The renderer limits are memory-asserted here and want a live check.
27. Render verification. `mochiko:patterns-system-design` says a diagram must render, never ship as a raw block. The plugin is clone-only with no renderer; BACKLOG.md:793 still lists "the rendered-diagram primary path" as open, and the dogfood exercised the un-rendered fallback. How is diagram validity checked, and does a validator in `mochiko-cli` need its own admission ruling?
28. Readers and their reads. Which agent reads which diagram: the sufficiency check reads the index, the design phase reads the spine, gap-finding reads the signed drawing. Does Mermaid help a model more than the element table, at what token cost? For humans: the fixed delta colours in DIAGRAM-CONVENTIONS.md (#2e7d32, #f9a825) in dark mode, and accessibility titles.
29. Convention collisions. Product-level diagram craft has no owner: `authoring-architecture-store.diagram-craft-routing` gives `patterns-system-design` feature-level craft only. BACKLOG.md:907 queues a "trust-boundary diagram convention" in the security-depth build. A desk diagram craft must not fork a third convention.

### Class: evidence — the bet's test, consumers, build shape

30. LB · Evidence and the success test. The frame says the problem has not been felt in a live run. Kinako's 33 rows read 26 decided, 7 open, and no `not-now` or `n-a` (stance lines of its `concerns.md`); what do the two dogfood stores actually show about defaults plus guesses? What observation after the build shows tighter rulings? Specify's precedent: "What a run is judged on is decided from that log, never before it" (BACKLOG.md:31).
31. Downstream contracts and gates. Any new store field, diagram or record changes what review-sufficiency, the design phase, review-feasibility's architecture pass and gap-finding's first message read. Command payload char budgets, the contract suite's deterministic set and per-unit gate audits all apply to the build.
32. One build or two. Part (a), the interview, and part (b), the diagrams, have different owners, homes and unknowns; (b) carries the rendering and taste unknowns. Bundled in one build item, (a) can stall on (b).
